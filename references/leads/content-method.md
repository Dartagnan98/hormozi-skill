## The Content Unit (Hook -> Retain -> Reward)
Every piece of content has three parts:
1. **HOOK**: Give a reason to redirect attention. Objective: get them to notice.
2. **RETAIN**: Keep attention long enough to deliver value. Objective: get them to consume.
3. **REWARD**: Satisfy the reason they consumed it. Objective: make them want more.

Long content = many content units strung together. Short content = fewer units. Start short, build up.

---

## Hooks: 3 Elements

### 1. Pick Good Topics (5 Sources)
- **Far Past**: Life lessons, behavior changes ("I used to think X, then Y happened")
- **Recent Past**: Pull out your calendar, look at conversations, meetings, events
- **Present**: Real-time capture. Email yourself ideas. Test on Twitter.
- **Trends**: Current events that apply to your expertise
- **Manufactured**: Create experiences to document (e.g., "I lived on $100/month")

### 2. Pick Good Headlines (7 Characteristics)
From news media research on high-performing headlines:
- **Recency**: "Just happened" beats "happened 5 years ago"
- **Relevancy**: Personally meaningful to the target audience
- **Celebrity**: Including prominent people/authorities
- **Proximity**: Close to home geographically
- **Conflict**: Opposing ideas (pineapple vs no pineapple, freedom vs security)
- **Unusual**: Odd, rare, bizarre, one-of-a-kind
- **Ongoing**: Stories still in progress, dynamic, evolving

Combine all 7: something that just happened, relevant to the reader, includes a celebrity, right near them, with conflict, that's bizarre, and still unfolding = maximum attention.

### 3. Match the Format
Match the format that has rewarded them before on that platform. Make content look like high-performing content on that platform. Same video formatted differently for IG Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts.

## Retention: 3 Structures
1. **Lists**: Things/facts/tips presented one after another following a theme. Give the number upfront ("7 ways to..."). More flexible, less explicit reward.
2. **Steps**: Actions in order to accomplish a goal. Less flexible, more explicit reward ("Do these 5 steps to sell your house").
3. **Stories**: Events (real or imaginary) with lessons. People want to know what happens next. Fiction works too (Star Wars, Lord of the Rings).
- Best content uses ALL THREE interwoven together.

## Reward
- How good your content is = how often it rewards your audience / the time it takes to consume. Think: **value per second**.
- "There's no such thing as too long, only too boring." People binge 900-page books and Netflix series.
- People don't have shorter attention spans. They have higher STANDARDS because competition is fierce.
- If your audience grows, you did good. If it's not growing, your stuff isn't good enough (not the algorithm).

## Content Tip: Make All Content for Strangers
- Never make inside jokes. Never reference past content without context.
- Existing audience won't mind the "if you're new" intro.
- New audience will feel included, not like an outsider.

## Proof-Promise-Plan Introduction Framework
For educational content, start every piece with:
1. **Proof**: Why should they listen to you? Demonstrate credibility.
2. **Promise**: What will they learn/get from this content?
3. **Plan**: How will you deliver on that promise? (Steps, structure)
- Can be in any order; emphasize whichever is most important.

## Monetizing Content: The Give/Ask Ratio

### The Ratio
- **Television** (mature platform, maximally monetizing): 3.6:1 ratio (47 min content : 13 min ads per hour)
- **Facebook** (mature): 4:1 ratio (4 content posts : 1 ad)
- **TikTok** (growth phase): Nearly zero ads for years. All give, give, give.
- These ratios are for platforms that STOPPED growing and are maximally extracting.
- If you want to GROW: give until they ask. Way beyond 4:1.

### Hormozi's Approach
- **"Give in public, ask in private."** Your public reputation stays purely on giving.
- The moment you start asking publicly is the moment you slow growth.
- DM conversations and private sales keep your brand pristine.
- If your product is exceptional, even the "ask" deposits goodwill (book for $2.99 still reinforces brand).

### Two Ways to Ask
1. **Integrated**: Advertise within each piece of content, keeping give:ask ratio high. Example: 58.5 min of value + 1.5 min CTA in a 60-min video = 39:1 ratio.
2. **Intermittent**: Make many pure-give pieces, occasionally make an ask piece. Example: 10 gives then 1 ask.

### Where to Place CTAs
- After a valuable moment or at the end of content
- In a PS statement (the 2nd most-read part of any written content after the headline)
- Make CTAs contextual to the content topic

### Cautionary Tale
- Friend had podcast, it blew up, he asked too frequently -> podcast shrank -> asked more -> got less -> vicious cycle.
- Hold back asks as long as possible. Only ask with hand open, not shoving.

## Scaling Content: Two Approaches
1. **Depth then Width**: Master one platform -> maximize quality & quantity -> add next platform. Better for resource-constrained/beginners.
2. **Width then Depth**: Post on one platform -> go regular -> add closest similar platform -> continue until all covered -> maximize all. Better for experienced creators.

## 7 Content Lessons
1. **Switch from "how to" to "how I"**: Less ego, more receptive audience. "My favorite ways to generate leads" not "The best way to generate leads."
2. **We need to be reminded more than taught**: 1 in 6 of Hormozi's audience didn't know he had a book despite daily posts for 2 years. Repeat yourself way more than you think.
3. **Puddles, Ponds, Lakes, Oceans**: Start narrow. Be king of a small puddle before jumping to the ocean. A plumber in a town -> local business pond -> brick-and-mortar chains lake -> general business ocean.
4. **Content creates sales tools**: Content that generates inquiries can be (a) run as paid ads and (b) compiled into "greatest hits" for sales team to send prospects matching objections.
5. **Free content retains paying customers**: Customers don't separate where value comes from. More amazing free content = paying customers stay longer.
6. **No such thing as too long, only too boring**: Streaming platforms prove people binge hours of content.
7. **Avoid pre-scheduling posts**: Manually posting gives that final quality check moment. Manual posts outperform pre-scheduled ones.

## SPCL Framework (Status, Power, Credibility, Likeness)
The four elements that create influence (= high likelihood of compliance with requests):

1. **Status**: You control scarce resources people want. Money, access, knowledge, connections.
2. **Power**: Say-do correspondence. You give instructions, they follow, good things happen. More reinforcement cycles = more power.
3. **Credibility**: Third-party validation. Guinness records, certifications, awards, testimonials, media appearances.
4. **Likeness**: They see themselves in you. Physical resemblance, shared values, similar background. Just be you.

## 6 Lessons from 40 Months / $4M / 35,000 Pieces of Content

1. **Edutainment -> Education**: Entertainment viewers want more entertainment. Education viewers want more education. They don't cross over. Education drives book sales, opt-ins, and applications. Entertainment doesn't.

2. **For Us -> For You**: Make content for your ideal customer, not what's easiest. Tactics: clear packaging (not vague thumbnails), proof-based introductions, emphasis on language over production, list/steps/stories over vlogs.

3. **Wide -> Narrow**: Narrow topic focus attracts the right people. Business content brings business owners.

4. **Views -> Revenue**: Track ad revenue as primary metric, not views. Ad revenue = views x RPM (revenue per thousand). Higher RPMs = right audience. In a 90-day experiment, entertainment content doubled views but halved ad revenue.

5. **Shorts -> Longs**: Shorts viewers watch more shorts. Long viewers watch more longs. Longs drive far more conversions. Shorts on one platform can lead to longs on another.

6. **Assume More -> Assume Nothing**: Title like they don't know you. Introduce yourself every time. Explain all references. Make content for strangers. Warm people like reminders; cold people need introductions.

## CONTENT STRATEGY (Deep Pass)

### Agency Acquisition 3-Phase Framework
- Phase 1: Hire basic agency ($3-5K/month) for 3-6 months -- learn fundamentals, establish cadence
- Phase 2: Hire advanced agency ($15K+/month) for 6 months -- learn nuanced strategies. Explicit agreement: "I want to learn how you do it, then train my team"
- Phase 3: Co-create -> transition -> consulting retainer -> cut
- Ran this exact playbook for YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, podcast, paid ads

### Agency Trap Pattern (10 Agencies, All Failed Same Way)
1. Excited about leads they'll bring
2. Onboarding feels valuable
3. Best senior rep assigned
4. See results
5. Senior rep moved to newest customer
6. Junior rep takes over
7. Results suffer
8. Complain -> senior comes back temporarily
9. Results never recover
10. Cancel -> repeat cycle

### Repost Your Best Content
- "Look at your number one piece of content from last year. Post it again."
- It will do just as well or better because new audience hasn't seen it
- "We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught"
- Simpsons: same introduction every episode with tiny variations

### Thumbnail/Headline: Clarity Over Curiosity
- Go from VAGUE to CLEAR
- "If they want that thing they will click on that thing and then you just give them that thing"
- Clarity beats curiosity for educational content

### Shorts Don't Feed Longs
- Prevailing theory: shorts -> longs -> customers
- Reality: shorts viewers watch more shorts, long viewers watch more longs, customers buy more
- These are different audiences. Longs drive significantly more conversions

### All Views Are Not Created Equal
- A video with 100K views can generate more revenue than one with 10M views
- RPM (revenue per thousand views) is the true quality metric
- Highest RPM months also had most book sales, opt-ins, and business applications

### Pre-Work > Post-Production
- "An ounce of pre-work is worth a pound of post"
- 25% of old post-production time spent on pre-research eliminated 90% of man-hours
- Result: 4x video output at HIGHER quality

### Content Introduction Formula: Proof, Promise, Plan
- PROOF: prove you know what you're talking about
- PROMISE: tell them what they'll get/learn
- PLAN: set expectations for what happens next
- For business education, usually lead with PROOF

### Context > Content Quality
- "The message is 10% of how people consume it. 90% is the context around the message"
- "Elon Musk can tweet on the toilet and get a million likes... because he's the richest man in the world"
- "Solve for the proof and then the content will take care of itself"

## CONTENT STRATEGY INSIGHTS (Deep Pass -- Batches 3-4)

### Edutainment vs Education (90-Day Experiment)
- Ran 90-day experiment doing more edutainment/entertainment content
- Views went 2-3x higher than normal
- But ad revenue DROPPED by half
- "We're actually getting the wrong people"
- Entertainment viewers want more entertainment, education viewers want more education -- they do NOT convert into each other
- Key metric switched from views to ad revenue (RPM) because it pairs quality + quantity
- Month with highest RPMs also created most book sales, opt-ins, and business applications

### Content Decision Framework (SPCL)
- S = Status (control reinforcers in a given environment)
- P = Proof (why should anyone listen to you)
- C = Content quality
- L = Likeability
- Build influence, not just views

### Audience Monetization Framework (4 Ways)
1. Affiliates: paid after sales (lowest risk for brand)
2. Sponsorships/Endorsements: paid upfront to advertise
3. Partnerships: equity in existing business (higher commitment)
4. Start your own brand: promote your own products (highest control + risk)

### Branding Playbook
- Branding = "deliberate pairing of things through an outcome"
- Good branding = deliberate pairing with stuff your ideal customers LIKE
- Bad branding = pairing with stuff they don't (Bud Light + Dylan Mulvaney = sales dropped)
- Brand measurement: influence (likelihood to change behavior) x direction (towards/away) x reach (how many)
- Nike pairs with LeBron/Tiger (winning) -> $5 t-shirt becomes $60 -> 12x premium
- Strong brand improves advertising: 6x higher click-through rate on branded vs generic
- Warren Buffett: "The single most important decision in evaluating a business is pricing power"

Put UTMs on description links and give a CTA in the video to a next step or lead magnet. That's how he sees which videos actually produce revenue on the back end.

### Sell the shortcut through your work (vid: 5rOIS7GHD5Y — "Personal brand in 2027")

No accomplishments? Volume is credibility too. Go on 100 dates and turn it into a 12-minute video: two seasons of The Bachelor in 12 minutes. Do 10,000 door knocks and document them. People pay attention to skip time they don't have. You still have to write the book to make the CliffNotes.

### Turn delivery Q&A into 30-50 posts a week (vid: 8VqSFBMMS4M — "Scale or Fail: Tina, hate selling")

Clip every question answered inside the program; an offshore or AI editor handles it. Answer community questions by green screen. The audience won't see repeats as repeats, same as Dave Ramsey. Push every platform to one conversion point. Take winners and run them as ads.

### Audience and waitlist before product (vid: EonibwnAEME — "How to catch up in life")

Attention is potential energy, even with nothing to sell. Someone who pays with time now is more likely to pay with money later. No proof yet? Do the work, document it, and show either epic proof or epic effort.

### Feed the proof machine (vid: XsWSvz-aewA — "New content in the age of AI")

Plug a transcriber into your virtual meetings, then build content from real examples instead of invented stories. Better still, build proof into how you serve customers. With only 10 customers, go to their houses or run free audits and document it. The trade: free work in exchange for footage.

### Make instructions easy, then make them believed (vid: ZTSI3DDP_4A — "Social strategy 2027, The Algorithm")

Education is the door to commerce. Two jobs: use words people understand, which lowers the cost of comprehending, and raise conviction the payoff is real, which makes the gamble worth taking. Show expertise in varied conditions against the avatars you serve.

### Every customer makes an ad (vid: j2TZMFkj71Q — "Scale or Fail: Mim, stickers")

Add a bonus unlocked when a new member posts themselves doing the thing, and put it in the first-7-days activation sequence. It raises activation and generates ads. 200 customers a launch means 200 ads. Screenshot group wins, add highlights, and you've got statics that take seconds.

### Capture the reveal (vid: uaLNfijnp-8 — "Scale or Fail: Hussle, dentistry")

Re-engineer delivery to film the moment: walk patients to a hallway mirror. Cut one result several ways: story, carousel, and doctor case review, ending with a pivot to viewers with the same problem, plus a lead-gen CTA. Hold organic until the supply constraint is fixed.

### Dry industry? Your content has no stakes (tax firm, $4M, 800 clients) (vid: A8p2HCI_23E — "Answers Your Questions")

Four years of posting tax tips got no views because tips are hypothetical. A hook only makes artificial stakes (curiosity). Real stakes come from a real situation, like a client with a $500K bill and no cash.
Prescription: write it into delivery. Each month, record 20-50 client calls as a value add. Open on last year's bill versus this year's, give 1-3 tactics, stop at about 10 minutes. Get consent; blur faces or show the numbers only. In person works easier than remote at first. No clients free? DM followers a free no-pitch call. Dave Ramsey's advice is drier than tax; the situations carry it.

### Scope your free help so asking stays light (vid: 3Cw_BR6utgc — "Advice for Hard Days")

Sales creator, 100K+ followers, service business, needs cash without souring the audience. Keep the public feed all give. When questions arrive by DM or story reply, set the boundary: you've had the free material, and if you want more than that, let's get on a call.
For the public version, use a low-key line in a comment or story: if you need help with this, I'm happy to help. Cash comes in and the goodwill stays.

### Low views can be a working strategy if the right buyer sees it (vid: X39rITUMEPA — "First Principles, Content, Brand")

Alex watches gym equipment content daily, and small metal fabricators fill his discover page with niche posts about a welded handle. One such brand, per Alex, makes multiple millions of dollars on about 6,000 followers.
Platform targeting has made niche reach the easiest it has ever been. Want whales? Make whale content and accept that it won't get big numbers. If you've outgrown your customer (fat loss coach now into competition prep), keep making what you used to care about.

### Engineer the clips while you are live (vid: R7McLmx73Xw — "30 lives in 30 days, what he learned")

Do not stream two hours and hunt for moments later. When a question will clearly become a short, phrase the answer for it: land on clear next steps or a one-two-three so the clip has a payoff.
Afterward, trim hard and start on the hook. Viewers on the replay neither know nor care it was live; they only judge value per second. Post-live views can double or triple the live count.
Volume also forced his team to cut wasted post-production: one video recently went from idea through filming and editing to posted very quickly.

### Where content monetizes, and how to pick which post becomes an ad (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

Skool attribution, per Hormozi: Instagram sends far more traffic, but YouTube converts at three or four times the percentage, so YouTube often earns more. Creators with trouble monetizing TikTok put a DM me on IG CTA in the bio. Email and podcast are middle of funnel, not discovery.
To find purchase intent, sort a year of posts by saves-to-likes ratio. List and step-by-step teaching that only did medium views tends to carry the most buying intent. Run those as ads.

### Promotions go on top of normal content (vid: k834E6OTGTM — "$100M Leads launch event")

A mistake he says took too long to figure out: when launching, people swap out the content the audience came for and only talk about the thing. Keep regular output at the same level and layer promotion over it, so launch periods need more total volume. Film long ahead: 17 shorts and 6 long-forms were on the launch calendar months before.

### Ask ceilings from media benchmarks (vid: 5cOwh-8scu8 — "Make Money Online Without Destroying Reputation")

Extends the give:ask ratio. TV: 13 minutes of ads per 47 of content. Facebook: about 1 ad per 4 posts. So 3:1 is the ceiling. Long form: integrate a tiny share of the runtime. Short form: under about 3% of posts, once or twice a month at several a day. If you need income now, he'd use 30:1. The real cost of a commercial is goodwill. Warning story: a friend's podcast ask made money, so he asked more, and listeners and revenue kept falling.

### Declaration of business: the first post (vid: 5cOwh-8scu8 — "Make Money Online Without Destroying Reputation")

If you've helped people for a while you have more goodwill than you think. Post once: you may know me from here; this is what I'm doing now; I'd love to give you something free that helps; if not, stay tuned. Hormozi's 10-year-old version: free training for a year in exchange for a $500 to $1,000 donation to the client's chosen cause, so they valued it, then a website, transformations and a few open slots. It brought his first 20 paying clients.

### Delaying the right hook: compounding favors the late ask (vid: 5cOwh-8scu8 — "Make Money Online Without Destroying Reputation")

A tequila owner refused The Rock's request for two-thirds of the company; yet the deal might have cost The Rock more than his own brand did, so waiting paid. Say yes to an endorsement today and it's cheap vs your audience in two years. He declined early mentoring DMs for this reason. Patience isn't waiting: it's deciding what you do in the meantime. Audience is the asset, content just adds to it. Content lifts demand, and you set supply, so price rises at both ends.

Under 6,000 followers, reach under about 2,800 accounts a month, one post and one story a day, a list of about 2,900, 10-plus cheap cheat-sheet sales daily and about 3 higher-ticket a week, about $1M take-home (her figures to Hormozi). ### Don't fear the empty room (vid: 0S5xsICW8qg — "5800 Followers Into $1 Million")

Very low views can mean very high revenue. A brokerage he owns does many millions with videos getting 100 to 500 views, because the viewers need that high-end insurance. Views versus sales: when Hormozi's early podcast moved from gym talk to general business, listens rose and organic sales fell to almost nothing for months. Followers make you famous; understanding business makes you rich. Measure optins, buyers, not vanity metrics.

### Whisper, Tease, Shout: three phases of promoting anything (vid: 60_7PU9JDIw — "$100M Leads affiliate guide")

He credits this to something heard at an event, source unknown. Whisper (6+ weeks out, up to years): hints only, no contents, like a creature's foot in a movie teaser; show that you are working on it, since long visible effort raises perceived worth. Tease (6 weeks to 7 days): show a sliver of the contents, a page or one idea, like a trailer. Shout (last 7 days): plain reminders. Common error: shouting for six weeks so people tire of it before it starts. Shout power is set by goodwill banked in the earlier phases.

### For educators, the Lamborghini is the proof (vid: UGEc9-7X3OQ — "Building a brand on easy mode")

Extends Proof, Promise, Plan. He watched the first 3 minutes of his top 35 videos and found three things in all: promise, proof, path. Entertainers must show what the thumbnail showed at once (say you'll crush a Lamborghini and it had better appear). An educator's equivalent is the big reason to listen. Pattern: "I sold my company for $46.2M, a big part of that was a guarantee that beat competitors, and here are the four things that made it stronger." Promise and proof earn the stay; the path shows structure, then you meet the expectation, which drives watch time.

### The room test: niche videos sell even at a third of the views (vid: UGEc9-7X3OQ — "Building a brand on easy mode")

At a workshop he asked which of his videos owners actually watched: case studies such as building a sales team and onboarding, or lessons to his younger self. The whole room chose the case studies, which sometimes got a third of the views. Wider pieces act as top of funnel; the right people then go down the longer niche ones. His mix: about three or four on-topic long videos for each wider one.

### Tweets as the source file (vid: UGEc9-7X3OQ — "Building a brand on easy mode")

He calls tweeting his highest leverage use of time. An idea takes two seconds to capture; then the team pulls those tweets to prompt recording sessions, Instagram captions and reels, and he can read one aloud as a video. One capture spot feeds every format. It replaced his Gym Launch habit of a yearly email thread to himself of lessons and failures. Any repository works if ideas land there as they come.

### Compare post counts side by side (vid: ULGT0Qpglek — "17 Life-Changing Conversations")

After going from zero to about 200K followers in six months he asked a big influencer for the blueprint. There was none: anyone selling a flow chart is selling a system. They opened each platform side by side, and his count on every one was a fraction of the influencer's (five to one on Instagram). He put out 10x the content in the next six months and grew 10x faster. Try the comparison on your own accounts against someone ahead of you.

### Value post vs valuable post (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

"Here are six things" is a value post and no one cares. A valuable post is a bargain on time or money: I did the work, took the risk or spent the money so you don't have to. Hormozi's example, a member: spent $25K on ads, here's what ran, what happened, and a short summary at the end. A Games winner's alternative, broccoli wrapped in bacon: looks dumb and funny so you read, and you've learned something by the end. Each takes him 45 to 60 minutes. Best blend is valuable and fun.

### Give until they ask, then poll (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

Extends give until they ask. His illustrative math: pitch 100 people cold and perhaps one buys. Offer the same 100 a free fix, perhaps 20 say yes; solve it, name the next problem, and half buy: 10 sales instead of one. Listen for the common request, then poll: if I built this, would you want it? A yes is permission to spend three months building it.

### Local business: content converts, it doesn't acquire (vid: h6y0nYVZgwE — "Not Unbelievably Rich Yet")

Platform reach is global, so most viewers live nowhere near you. Treat content as what people check after an ad, outreach or referral: is it legit, friendly, near me. Going from zero to one post a week so you show a heartbeat is his high-ROI minimum; more only if content is your chosen main channel, then start at two a day.

### Proof ladder for when you have no proof (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

Best: I helped people like you do this. Next: I did the thing you want. Next: I did the work you won't, here's the result. Bottom rung: observe someone big whose metrics are easy to verify and connect the dots; a member started on Marcus Aurelius because no one cared what he himself said. Education is a deal on time, so show the time and money you spent to learn it (a member's "I spent this much on ads in 30 days, here's what I found"). The profile then says in one snippet who you are and why listen; on X, pin a long tweet that frames everything after it.

### Treat a ten-year audience like a first date (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

He grew the most, by percent, when he assumed nobody knew him and every second had to be earned. So he dropped branded content ("Alex's diet"): if you don't know who I am, who cares. Lead with what was done, not who did it. He has never run content buckets either; he talks about what's on his mind and variety follows. A former roommate's strategy, from a book he names as Too Good to Fail: make it so good that one viewer shares it (Hormozi says that roommate now runs a $100M company).

### Platform memory: how soon you can reuse a winner (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

Extends reposting your best. Shortest memory and most volume: X, where a six-month-old tweet can crush again. TikTok sits in the middle. Instagram remembers longest and takes the least, maxing at two to three reels a day for him; a photo or carousel goes almost only to existing followers, so treat it like email and put CTAs there. YouTube long form: don't repost. Four ways to remix: another platform, another format, re-shoot it, repost the old clip. His stack at the time: read winning tweets to camera for clips, string 20 clips into a YouTube video, use another top tweet as the caption.

### Platform scorecard: where to spend your content time (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

Score each platform on six things: who (share of households with money who are there), how many (share of the country), growth (can a new account get discovered), depth (time spent per piece), conversion (DMs, links, shopping on the platform), then results in your own CRM. His tiers for selling to business owners: S YouTube and Instagram; A X, Facebook, email; B LinkedIn, podcast; F TikTok. Trigger for the audit: a Skool Games winner (his story) had 800K on TikTok, 200K on Instagram, and 90% of revenue from Instagram. The audit moved Facebook up from nothing to A for him. Your avatar changes the answer: an art dealer got 90% of her business from Pinterest.

### LinkedIn: treat a big account's comment as a collab (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

A large account's comment shows your post to their followers, so write something substantial about them or their best piece, and a real reply follows; the longer the reply, the more reach. He did this for a small account by commenting. Starting move: get to 500 connections from your high school, college and employers, since the profile just reads 500+ after that. His hidden ROI there is recruiting: DMs to top talent get better response than any other platform.

### Platform mechanics he learned by testing (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

Instagram: tried 8, 6 and 4 posts a day, and settled on two sessions, morning and evening, because over-posting got penalized; TikTok and X did not penalize volume. The head of Instagram says shares drive it, above all a link sent off platform. Shorts no longer travel: a winner on one platform often fails on another. YouTube has no DMs, so stack your asks: top of the description above the fold, pinned first comment, a short in-video ask (long ones hurt watch time), and the community tab (2.5M impressions in 30 days against 18M for his shorts). His reason for starting YouTube: a podcast guest with about 300K subscribers got about 3,000 applications a month.

### Copy skills, never identity (vid: zNJ5JzEJgyo — "Why You Shouldn't Copy Me")

Replicate before you iterate applies to skills: sit beside the best caller or door knocker. It doesn't apply to story. Editing, captions and look can be cloned, not what you lived. Everyone can spot the real estate starter kit (fade, glasses, BMW, watch pic); once people can predict you they stop paying attention, because category filed means done. No story to tell? Go do the thing worth telling that others won't.
