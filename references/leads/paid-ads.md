# Paid Ads
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## What Matters Most
- Efficiency > creativity. All advertising works. The question is HOW WELL.
- Goal: find ads good enough to scale, then scale the hell out of them.
- **Benchmark: 3:1 LTGP-to-CAC ratio minimum** (lifetime gross profit vs cost to acquire customer). Hormozi targets 10:1 minimum for portfolio companies. Best ones do 20+:1. His personal lifetime average is 36:1.

---

## Three Phases of Ad Spending
1. **Track Money**: Get tracking in place first. Without tracking, you're flying blind at a casino.
2. **Lose Money**: You MUST lose money to make money. Out of 300 ads, maybe 295 won't work, 5 will be ROI-positive, 1 will be a home run. Most people quit at this phase.
3. **Print Money**: Find the winner, scale it. If you put $1 in and get $10 out, your budget = as many dollars as you can fit into the machine.

## "If someone says 'I have a $10K/month marketing budget,' I already know they don't understand paid advertising."
- If ads are working, there is no budget. You spend as much as possible.
- Constraint is never ad budget -- it's delivery capacity.
- Reverse engineer: if you can handle 20 new customers/month and CAC is $100, budget = $2,000/month. Focus on deconstraining delivery, then crank ads back up.

## Key Metrics

### LTGP (Lifetime Gross Profit)
- LTGP = (Price - Cost) x Number of purchases. NOT total revenue.
- Example: $15/month product, $5 cost = $10 gross profit. Customer stays 10 months = $100 LTGP.

### CAC (Cost to Acquire Customer)
- CAC = all-in cost including ad spend, commissions, software, team.

### Ratio Targets
- If LTGP/CAC > 3: scale aggressively
- If LTGP/CAC > 1 but < 3: improve either side
- Common mistake: confusing revenue with gross profit. A food company spending $100 to acquire a customer who pays $500 but at 10% margins = spending $100 to make $50.

---

## Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)
- The goal: break even or profit within 30 days so you can recycle the cash.
- Example: $15/month membership, $10 gross profit, $30 CAC = 3 months to break even. Too slow.
- **Fix**: Add a $100 one-time upsell. 20% take rate = $20 average per customer. $10 (first month) + $20 (upsell) = $30 = breaks even day one.
- Now you can reinvest immediately. Compounding: 2x -> 4x -> 8x -> 16x -> 32x in 5 months.
- Easiest upsells: sell MORE of what they bought, sell BETTER of what they bought, sell NEW/complementary.

---

## The 3 Numbers You Must Know
1. **CAC** (Cost to Acquire Customer) -- include everything
2. **LTV as Gross Profit** (not revenue) -- gross margin x lifetime purchases
3. **30-Day Cash** -- how much gross profit collected in first 30 days per customer
- Target: LTV/CAC > 3:1
- Target: 30-Day Cash/CAC > 1:1 (break even in 30 days = use credit cards to finance growth)

## How to Fix Ads That Aren't Working
1. Don't confuse sales problems with advertising problems. If right people are showing up and not buying = sales problem, not ad problem.
2. Your best free content makes the best ads. Videos not intended to be ads that went viral = murder as ads because the platform already validated them.
3. User-generated content (UGC) creates a flywheel: customers make videos -> run as ads -> more customers -> more videos.
4. Don't say "I'm not techy." Hormozi said it for 4 years, reversed it with 4 hours of effort.

---

## Ad Creation: The Elements
Every ad has:
1. **Call out**: Who is this for? ("Hey gym owners" or "If you're a mom over 35...")
2. **Value element**: What's in it for them? (The 3 W's: What you get, Who it's for, When/how)
3. **Call to action**: What do you do next? Clear, simple, direct.

---

## Targeting
- **Audience targeting**: Demographics, interests, behaviors, lookalikes
- **Creative targeting**: The content of the ad itself IS the targeting. The algorithm knows what you're talking about and shows it to the right people.
- "The content IS the targeting" -- make content for your avatar and the algorithm finds them.

---

## Paid Ads Checklist
- Who: You or your advertiser
- What: Your offer or lead magnet
- Where: Any platform you can buy access to your audience
- To whom: Target audience + lookalike audiences with filters/interests
- When: Every day, 7 days/week
- How much: Learning budget (track -> lose -> print), or reverse engineer from sales capacity
- How many variations: 30 hooks x 10 valuable content pieces = 300 ad variations to test
- How long: As long as it takes (sometimes 3-6 months to crack)

## META ADS CREATIVE MACHINE (Batch 7)

### Andromeda System
- Meta's new ad delivery system: feed it tons of ads, it picks top ~10% winners
- Makes advertising more efficient but requires massive creative volume
- Need ~100 ads; Andromeda picks top 5-7 winners. Old winners decay -- must constantly feed fresh creative
- Targeting is now embedded in creative itself: avatar in ad determines who sees it
- Demographic targeting less important than creative-based targeting

### Creative Production Playbook
- Life cycle ads: get 2-3 ads from each new customer (testimonials, transformations)
- Repurpose best organic content as ads + 5-second CTA cut-in
- Banner overlays for specific avatar callout (e.g., "Occupational Therapist, Physical Therapist, Speech Therapist")
- Andromeda finds people who match the avatar IN the ad -- make sure creative shows ideal customer
- Top-of-funnel hooks: ask the qualifying question in the ad ("Do you have a green card?")

### Specific Benchmarks from Hotline Calls
- Dental (high-ticket): expect $50/lead on Meta, 1-2% conversion rate at $8K+ ticket = $2,500-$5,000 CAC
- PPC dental: $5K-$8K/month minimum spend
- Creative volume: need ~100 ads, Andromeda picks top 10%, need to constantly feed fresh creative

## PAID ADS ADDITIONS (Batch 5-6)

### Facebook Slap Recovery Story
- Lead cost went up 5x overnight from Facebook algorithm change
- Was losing money on marketing daily
- Solution: attached phone to funnel, sold high-ticket on front end instead of trials
- Went from losing 2.5:1 to making 10:1 return on front end
- Could now spend up to $100/lead profitably vs breaking even at $10/lead before

### Three Levels of Advertising (CFA Framework)
- **Level 1**: Lifetime gross profit > CAC (minimum 3x to be a business)
- **Level 2**: 30-day gross profit > CAC (never spend your own money to grow -- credit card float)
- **Level 3**: 30-day gross profit > 2x CAC (unlimited scale -- one customer funds the next)
- Level 3 math: Month 1=1, Month 2=2, Month 3=4... Month 12=4,096 total customers
- Credit card = 30-day interest-free money -- that's why 30 days is the benchmark

### Core Four Advertising Framework
- 2 audiences (warm/cold) x 2 communication types = 4 methods
- Warm: people who know you (unidirectional -- they know you)
- Cold: people who didn't give permission ("other people's audiences")

### Workshop funnel for high-trust sales (vid: B-ogfFiQpXg — "Scale or Fail: Dr. Zad, concierge medicine")

Meta ads to a one-day event with scan value baked in, ideally $99 to filter tire kickers and get a card on file. Pitch one on site, book a second, VSL in between. In-person workshops beat nearly everything for high-trust offers, and you trade more sifting for a bigger ocean.

### Launch spend cadence (vid: j2TZMFkj71Q — "Scale or Fail: Mim, stickers")

Early spend, 5 to 10% of budget, is only to test creative. Stair-step through middle weeks (10%, 15%), and expect about half to go in the final stretch. He reached $500K a day at the end of a launch. Don't cap total spend if ads perform. Optimize on purchases, track EPC. Transcript has gaps.

### Think in hundreds of creatives (vid: j2TZMFkj71Q — "Scale or Fail: Mim, stickers")

Two images and two videos isn't a test. Reshoot your 4 best with 20 backgrounds and you've got 80. AI statics take seconds. If CPA jumps when you scale, your ads only speak to product and offer aware people. Add pain-based hooks up the funnel. Mim's next game: 800 ads, $100K spend.

### 100 ponds, no oceans (vid: j2TZMFkj71Q — "Scale or Fail: Mim, stickers")

You can't convert everyone with the same message. Add hook call-outs per sub-avatar, such as mothers vs grandmothers. To scale you go narrower, which is counterintuitive. Drain many small ponds.

### Half your leads come at the end (vid: xRxVpjeHmeA — "Scale or Fail: Paul, adventure park")

In a countdown campaign, a third to a half of leads arrive in the last 48 hours, however long it runs. The middle stretch feels like failure and costs climb. Keep spending anyway.

### Let customers pay their continuity in creative (vid: A8p2HCI_23E — "Answers Your Questions")

Women's weight loss program, $4.1M, 50/50 Meta and organic. $10K anchor, $5K downsell, average ticket $5,087, back-end community at $50-$150 a month. Her Andromeda ads win for days and then burn out, because she writes every ad alone.
Alex: creative is the limit, and fixing it takes her to well past the $12M goal. Offer customers a choice: pay $100 a month, or make several pieces a week from a list of proven hooks. Get 100 people on five a week and that's 500 pieces, each person matching a sub-segment. Same words from different faces is fine. Log winners in a sheet that feeds the AI. His own operation puts out about 500 a day.

### When to add paid: let organic find them, let ads ask (vid: X39rITUMEPA — "First Principles, Content, Brand")

Paid is close to a one-way door for services because it demands phone teams and other infrastructure; ecommerce to a checkout is easier. Organic struggles with CTAs, which kill reach. Ads can make the direct ask and reach is guaranteed. Buyers don't separate the two, so together they are your brand.
He keeps more organic than paid, retargets those warmed-up viewers, and calls it significantly higher ROAS than cold. Go too far and you burn the audience, then you must rebuild your selling for cold traffic. That has upside, but it's a whole new machine.

### Local is fastest to cash, but never swap a funnel that just hit 4x (vid: lidFtb1Ga0o — "He Makes $1.5M and Keeps 1.3%")

Hormozi's survival play for a solo weight loss coach: local lead gen, call them, send a video, in-person close in a gym office rented for a couple hundred a month, walls covered in testimonials. Weak creative still wins locally, and he says 10x to 30x returns are far more common there, though it's harder to scale.
Then he learned the caller had 4x on $6K a month with statics to an Instagram DM funnel, and reversed himself: lock the local idea away. Double the spend and add DM setters; that path can reach $100K a month.
Lesson: ask what's working before prescribing. New information beats the good plan.

### A hard ceiling on ad spend is a hook problem, not a bidding problem (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

Agency owner at $600K/month, $150K/month ad spend, 25% margins: her Meta ads broke going from $5K to $10K a day and she blamed campaign structure. His read: bidding and campaign setup are maybe 5% differences. Her hooks only speak to people already aware of the solution, so Facebook runs out of people to show them to.
Fix: map hooks to the five awareness levels, write wider and less aware hooks (how to double AOV, six ways to lower CPA), and swap the straight book-a-demo ask for a lead magnet. Expect a slower breakeven and a longer education path.
At a $6K/month price, give every secret away in the ads. Those buyers want it done for them anyway.

### Hire the agency, keep the mouse (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

How he learned national-level Facebook ads: he paid an agency its full fee but cut the deal down to live working sessions. He clicked, they told him what to do and why, he recorded the screen and asked questions. Within six weeks he no longer needed them.
Prescribed to an insole clinic owner ($54K/month, stuck at $3K/month Google spend because his specialist said it could not go higher): stop leaving a core part of the business in someone else's hands. Spend a set monthly budget yourself alongside the current vendor. You will be bad at first, then quickly beat them because it is your business.

### Pay more per lead: Money Models launch and the One Year Later campaign (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

Money Models launch, two campaigns. Optimized for opt-ins: leads at about $4-5, worth about $20, so 4-5 to 1. Optimized for pre-orders: leads at $17, worth $189, about 11 to 1. The expensive leads won.
Gym Launch's One Year Later campaign (we fix the model, not one method; where are their one-year results?) had a far higher cost per call and nearly got killed. Then sales asked for more of those leads. A year on they paid most, stayed longest, upgraded and referred most.
Lessons: expect lead cost to rise when you add qualifiers, judge on LTV to CAC, and run marketing and sales as one acquisition loop.

### First ads for a brand-new service business (vid: ehmBlLNGgsA — "Focus is subtraction live Q&A")

1) Do the service free for 5-10 people in exchange for using video of it. 2) Those clips become your first creative. 3) Local call-out (town + avatar) wrapped in an attraction offer with the word free, such as a giveaway, a buy-one-get-one, or a money-back win. 4) Run to Meta instant forms, so no landing page. 5) Automate leads into a Google Sheet, with a phone alert carrying a tap-to-dial number. Set the automation to check every minute; the extra cost pays for itself in speed. 6) Call in 15 seconds if possible, 5 minutes at most.

### Cold traffic in a warm funnel: the $99 workshop fix (vid: _ooj0V4hpgw — "Hormozi Hotline caller breakdowns")

Poker coach at a $4M pace, 200K subscribers, closes 40%+ of warm pitches into a $5K program, but cold funnels only break even. A free cold webinar got 25% show and 5% booking, and most could not afford it.
Prescription: charge $99 for a 3-hour workshop, stack bonuses until the price feels absurd, sell at the end, accept a loss on the front end. He expects a paid step to take unqualified from about 90% to about 40%, and buyers buy more. Add the top qualifying signal to the form (average game buy-in). Judge qualified conversion, not webinar conversion: closing 50% of qualified is a traffic issue.

### Low spend feels like volatility: spend at least one CAC a day (vid: _ooj0V4hpgw — "Hormozi Hotline caller breakdowns")

Physique coaching, $150K MRR, $15K offer, $3,500 CAC, $1,300 a day, saying results were inconsistent. That spend buys a sale every three days, so a normal gap looks like failure. Raise daily spend to at least CAC ($3,600) for a customer a day.
That exposes the next limit: creative. Go from 5 ads a week per founder to about 100 (his portfolio ships 250 a day). Walk and talk winning hooks off a teleprompter for 20-30 ads an hour, then turn winners into statics. With too few closes to train on, fire the pixel on a qualified-only application thank-you page; he wants an event you can hit a thousand of.

### Creative and page audit from the HVAC teardown (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Text each ad image to your phone before approving, since that is the real size; fine print and logos vanish. In direct response skip the logo, make the offer the headline, then run many colour variations (he said 40). Send ads to a freestanding page with no nav and the offer above the fold, not a homepage where a button leads to a second page: he says that extra step usually loses about half. Point all site buttons to the new page too.

### Spend problem at high ROAS: uncapped until it stops (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Corey had about 13:1 up front on about $5K a month in Google spend (his figures) with an unoptimized page. Rule: if each dollar returns several, the budget is as many dollars as you can get, watching ROAS and CAC month over month. If you are not sure of the return, you have an attribution problem, so fix that first. Fix the funnel before you add spend, then check you have crew for the leads.

### Buy your own name on search (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Anyone who advertises or gets word of mouth creates searches for their name. Bid on every version of it so competitors cannot sit on top of your customers. Paying for clicks you might have got free is a small problem. If someone outbids you on your own name, you can always win, because those clicks are worth more to you than to them.

### Creative volume gut check (vid: 2tHlHWgDRdQ — "The reason you are not successful")

An agency owner stuck on lead flow was proud of 5 to 10 creatives a week. Hormozi screen-shared his own department: 30 pieces tested every 3 days. Compare your output to someone winning before diagnosing anything else.

### Testimonial interview grid: internal/external x before/during/after (vid: 5MjjpB8SPMo — "Make Everyone Want to Buy Your Stuff")

Teachable format for life cycle ads. Before: how life felt, and observable stats. During: what made them act, what worried them most, why go anyway. After: external and internal results. The concern question is the key since a prospect is talking a prospect past the same fear. Cut to 60 to 90 seconds; one interview yields content, bonus and ad.

### Starting local ads from zero: vendor for PPC, in-house for Meta (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

For a caller's garage door company doing about $7M with one marketing lead a month. Local PPC and SEO agencies run a repeatable playbook at fair prices, so start with a vendor. Local Meta differs: creative tires in 2-3 weeks in a small market, so you need new ads about every 21 days, and few vendors keep up unless they work at scale. Hire a young, tech-savvy local ads person in-house. Local SEO is far easier to win than national.

### Raise LTV before adding an ad platform (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

Caller spent about $10K a month on Meta at roughly 3:1 with weak lead quality, and wanted to switch platforms. Hormozi: never turn off a channel that makes money, and test on top of it. First add the performance component, taking a customer from $3K to $10K-$12K. At 12:1, diminishing returns from more spend stop hurting. Then the Meta limit becomes creative volume.

### Ads skim, organic plants (vid: BYpTRiRqS1Y — "4 Educational Business Owners")

To a founder who grew on organic alone: turning on ads typically gives a one-time 3x to 5x off the baseline, not a promise. Picture a pyramid: ads reach down and skim the people closest to buying; more organic volume widens the base. Run both. Warning: the warm sales motion will likely break on cold traffic and funnel economics shift, so build ads and sales together, since either one alone being bad fails.

### When the ad dashboard is implausible (vid: HGZOxBfnF-E — "$1M Business for a Stranger")

Luis's agency showed LTV below CAC while the business made money, on spend that had risen with sales. So either the report is wrong or the agency adds little and referral traffic is the source. A post-purchase survey alone can't settle it. A bolder test Hormozi floated: shut off the ads and see how sales change. Past $3M, "more spend, more sales" stops being enough, because colder traffic needs real targeting data.

### Treat spare profit as a keyword hunting budget (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

Custom game-table maker at about $2.5M revenue and 20% margin spent $15K-$20K a month on Google, near 10:1, and said more spend brought worse leads. Hormozi called it a media buying problem. Accept burning perhaps $200K a year on bad keywords to find six more you can scale to $100K a month. Whatever you do not need to live on is experiment budget, not lost money.

### Colder keywords need a bridge page (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

Extends the Schwartz awareness levels. Product-aware keywords run out; broader ones can hold many times the clicks but need more steps. Send them to an advertorial, then the sales page. Problem-aware example for a pool table seller: is your house boring, or do you have a spare room with no purpose? They had no idea a pool table was coming. Start from where they are and walk them along.

### Paid can burn through a B2B niche fast (vid: FKWEybUIda4 — "I quit advertising")

Fastest way to turn on business, hardest at scale in B2B and B2B2C, since you can exhaust a whole marketplace. Then scaling has to come from outside the ad account.

### You're bored of the ad; the customer hasn't seen it (vid: TIH1w-KuATk — "7 Secrets Behind Chick-fil-A")

Henry Ford story, as Hormozi tells it: months into a campaign he asked when they'd stop running it, since he was sick of it. It hadn't launched yet. You live your product all day; the buyer gets it maybe once a month. A change every six months feels slow to you and like constant churn to them, and they pay for consistency.

### All give in the feed, all ask in the ads (vid: UGEc9-7X3OQ — "Building a brand on easy mode")

Extends let ads ask. The give to ask ratio from his leads book has an advanced form: organic content only gives, ads carry every CTA. In one person's feed it still nets out at three pieces of content for one ad, and the brand isn't spent because the two run separately. Starting organic also lifts existing paid right away: people who see the ad check your content, then take the next step, show up and close at a higher rate. Treat early content as lead nurture, and it becomes acquisition as it grows. Don't worry about views yet.

### Own your branded terms (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

Competitors buy your name while you don't. Bid on every variation of your brand and send it to a simple opt-in page. He reports 20:1 to 40:1 returns, and about 10 minutes to set up. Often the biggest easy pile of money in the business.

### Lead gen in an hour, and a slush fund for ads (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

What third-party lead companies do, by his account, for five times the cost: a Facebook lead ad, Zapier into a sheet, a lookalike of your customer list, something free for a phone number. Then extend the slush fund idea: pick what you can blow monthly (he starts people at $100 a day) and always spend it. The learning beats another course, and your ego stays out of it.

### Optimize the buyer to cost per sale, not cost per lead (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Buyers chasing cheapest leads and appointments drifted a 25-35 employed target down to 18-24. Reps then cancelled 75% of appointments by hand just to reach a 49% show rate. A low show rate can be a targeting problem, not a nurture one. Switch the objective to who actually buys and spend moves to where sales come from.

### The shadow funnel: global retargeting first (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

First campaign for anyone already getting traffic: install every platform's pixel and retarget all site visitors on Google Display, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube. You already paid for those visits, so he says it is almost always positive ROAS. He prescribed this before any new channel, for a podcast-led coaching brand and for a roofer on PPC.

### The friction rule (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

Too many bad leads: add friction. Too few leads: remove it. Almost no friction and still bad leads: targeting is off. Friction must filter. A slow page is expensive and no better; a VSL on the page costs more per lead and raises quality.

### Reskin winners: 80% of resources, 70/20/10 of effort (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

He says his team spends 80% of creative resources reskinning winners. Pull the top 5-10% of past ads, slice the first three seconds and paste them onto 100+ others. One testimonial from his later GM got about 100 versions: filters, reordered sections, new hooks, headlines, fonts, copy. Split effort 70% on the main ad's versions, 20% adjacent ideas, 10% wild ideas, and do the versions first.

### Compliance audit firms for shut-down ads (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

Health, wealth and relationship claims get flagged even when clean. Specialist firms audit sites and creative, then help you resubmit. Ask their post-audit approval rate: he says good ones run 90%+ at about $5-6K. Told to a $10M health company stuck under 1% of revenue on YouTube at a reported 10:1.

### Local: offer-driven, low front end, card on file (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

To a $600K acupuncturist. Local ads don't need brand or value first because people already know the service; lead with an offer. He'd bet on $19-29 for the first visit, sold on the thank-you page, then phone every lead. If you can, pitch the plan before treating, while they still hurt, then relieve them and book the next visit. Card already on file makes the close one question.

### Automated ad loops from existing content and wins (vid: fr78adfAnuA — "How to Use AI in Your Business in 2026")

Yesterday's content gets a CTA overlaid and auto-launches in a campaign each day. Community wins tab: every new win runs through six proven static templates and goes live as ads to his paid community. No new creative work, just plumbing.

### Keep an album of ads you like (vid: h6y0nYVZgwE — "Not Unbelievably Rich Yet")

He refuses premium so he sees ads, and screenshots any he likes. Before making ads, review your all-time best (words, hooks, settings, pains), then this week's interesting ones, merge elements, and reshoot winners word for word. Treat the daily ad budget as how the business eats: paid before you eat, plus about four hours a day on creative at small scale.

### Founder ad volume versus the portfolio, and the cross-industry swipe (vid: m5ordaa7NN4 — "This Idea Will Make Your Business Unstoppable")

Founders spending $5K a day complain they can't scale on five to seven new ads recorded monthly. At the time his team made about 30 new core ads a week, swapped hooks to reach about 100 pieces, and said a one-in-a-hundred ad is what reaches colder audiences. Weak creative only works on the hottest people. Prep before ad days: hooks written, past winners reviewed, and a phone swipe file of ads from other fields (a hotel ad that would work for the software company, a dry cleaning ad for a gym).

### Whitelisting: the sponsor pays to advertise you (vid: oRqnTOT9ZG8 — "Monetize An Audience in 2026")

A creator can't post about a sponsor over and over without souring the audience. So record one "I just partnered with them" ad and let the business run it as paid to people far beyond your following. Business gets better conversion from a known face and better delivery; creator gets paid and grows off someone else's spend. Priced off CPMs. Who has leverage depends on relative size.

### Choosing an agency after three bad ones (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

Roofing caller burned by three agencies had compared two each time. Talk to 10. Litmus: you are ready when you stop learning new things on the calls; for him it takes about 15 expert calls before he can talk their lingo. Filter: a good agency offers best practices unasked (the funnel, the offer, call within five minutes, the script, how to raise shows), and some work leads for you, since owners tend to be bad at it. Terms: no results in 30 days and I'm gone, results and I pay for a long time.
