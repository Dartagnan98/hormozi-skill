# The Core Four Framework
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## Core Definitions

### What Is a Lead
- **Lead**: A person you can contact. That is all. Anyone whose contact info you have (email, phone, DM, address) is a lead.
- **Engaged Lead**: A person you can contact WHO HAS SHOWN INTEREST in the stuff you sell. This is the true output of advertising.
- Someone follows you on social media and you can DM them = lead. They reply to your message or opt in = engaged lead.

### What Is Advertising
- **Advertising**: The process of making known. Ideally to people who don't know you yet.
- If you're not getting enough leads, you're not advertising enough. Simple.
- When all else is equal: double your leads = double your business. 10x your leads = 10x your business.

### Two Types of Audiences
1. **Warm Audience**: People who gave you permission to contact them. Friends, family, followers, past/current customers, contacts. They need to know YOU (unidirectional -- they know you, you don't necessarily know them).
2. **Cold Audience**: People who did NOT give you permission to contact them. Strangers. Other people's audiences/lists/eyeballs that belong to platforms.

### Two Types of Communication
1. **One-to-One** (private): DMs, calls, emails, texts
2. **One-to-Many** (public): Posts, ads, content, broadcasts

## The Core Four: Only 4 Ways to Get Leads

The 2x2 matrix of audience type x communication type creates the ONLY four ways one person can let other people know about anything:

| | Warm (People Who Know You) | Cold (Strangers) |
|---|---|---|
| **One-to-One (Private)** | 1. Warm Outreach | 3. Cold Outreach |
| **One-to-Many (Public)** | 2. Post Content | 4. Run Paid Ads |

These are 100% in your control. If you're not getting enough leads, it's because you're not doing enough of the Core Four.

## The Rule of 100
Every day, do 100 primary actions:
- **Warm/Cold Outreach**: 100 reach-outs per day (calls, DMs, emails)
- **Content**: 100 minutes of content per day
- **Paid Ads**: $100/day in ad spend
- Do this for 100 days straight. You WILL have customers.

## Method Overview

### 1. Warm Outreach (One-to-One, Warm)
- Reach out to people who already know you
- Use ACA framework (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask)
- Ask if they know anyone, not selling them directly
- Start free, raise prices as referrals come in
- Average 9 follow-up attempts needed

### 2. Post Content (One-to-Many, Warm)
- Content unit: Hook -> Retain -> Reward
- Give/Ask ratio: at minimum 4:1, ideally way beyond
- "Give in public, ask in private"
- Content is a massive multiplier on ALL other advertising methods
- 78% of Gym Launch customers consumed 2+ long-form pieces before buying

### 3. Cold Outreach (One-to-One, Cold)
- Reach out to strangers via calls, emails, DMs
- Three problems to solve: How to contact, What to say, Getting enough chances
- 100 cold calls/day -> ~4 engaged leads
- Competition can't see what you're doing (everything private)
- Incredibly reliable -- numbers work like clockwork

### 4. Paid Ads (One-to-Many, Cold)
- Three phases: Track Money -> Lose Money -> Print Money
- Benchmark: 3:1 LTGP-to-CAC ratio minimum
- If ads are working, there is no budget -- you spend as much as possible
- Constraint is never ad budget -- it's delivery capacity
- Out of 300 ads, maybe 5 will be ROI-positive, 1 will be a home run

### Sell through someone else's event (vid: mRlSb0O5QNU — "Scale or Fail: Joey, event planning")

Buy a booth at conferences full of your buyers, trade concessions for a 15-minute slot. Teach something like 20 mistakes buyers make, with proof, and offer the slides by QR. The opt-in asks whether they run events and how many: a survey close, no pitch. $15K booth, $500K deal in the pipe.

### Sell affiliates instead of customers: same work (vid: k834E6OTGTM — "$100M Leads launch event")

Same effort, 10 sales a month. Sell 10 customers at $10K: $100K a month, $1.2M in a year, flat. Sell 10 affiliates who each send one $10K customer monthly: revenue stacks by $100K every month, $7.8M in 12 months, and it keeps climbing.
Proof he cites: Prestige Labs did $429K in week one and $1.7M in its first full month on 100% affiliates, on his second attempt after a failed first launch two years earlier; $75M total affiliate sales across the portfolio. Reward top affiliates with value for their audience, not a Lamborghini.

### V x S x T: throughput is volume times skill times time (vid: 2tHlHWgDRdQ — "The reason you are not successful")

Each term feeds the others and they multiply rather than add: more reps build skill, skill makes you do it longer, duration compounds. It all starts with doing. A beginner has low S and near zero T, so the only available lever is extreme V. Blaming the channel is usually a volume miscount.

### Test size story: 300 flyers vs 100,000 (vid: 2tHlHWgDRdQ — "The reason you are not successful")

Hormozi's first gym tried 300 flyers total and called flyers dead. A multi-location friend wouldn't judge a test under far larger numbers and ran 100,000 a month at a half to one percent response, around 30 walk-ins a day. Before calling a channel dead, ask what volume its working operators run.

### Picking a first channel: overlap with what you do well (demolition, $1M) (vid: 0EqJD2o-Mnk — "Helping 6 Business Owners Scale")

Any channel can work, so choose the highest overlap with existing team skill. Owner's strength is in-person networking, a few events a week, so the risk-adjusted move is several a day before adding cold email or LinkedIn. Fund the next channel with the cash that frees up.

### Two-tier unpaid affiliate contest (vid: 60_7PU9JDIw — "$100M Leads affiliate guide")

Tier 1 suits big, status-driven promoters: the top 10 win a live AMA with him for their audience. Tier 2 stops small accounts from opting out: anyone bringing 10 people gets two bonus chapters left out of the book. Scarcity at the top, a tiny hurdle for everyone else. No cash paid. His rule: prizes should reward the affiliate's audience too. "Help our community win a Q&A" costs no trust; "help me win a contest" withdraws it. Cash alone aligns you and the affiliate but leaves out the audience.

### Affiliates are a second customer: sign-up friction and upkeep (vid: 60_7PU9JDIw — "$100M Leads affiliate guide")

Friction is a dial: easy sign-up for volume, harder for quality. He would qualify harder in B2B or services; his book uses name, email and phone, about 30 seconds. Put the custom link on the thank-you page and also email it. Changing the banner above the form lifted affiliate sign-ups 60%. Getting sign-ups is the easy part; getting them to keep promoting is the job. He set the cadence up front: two emails a week, one with new content, one with their standing. Hold back part of the assets for a second drop about three weeks in so their ads stay fresh. Affiliates will also judge how you treat their audience harshly, which forces your messaging up.

### Good advertising can be bad branding (vid: 9unucIBuNio — "10x Your Pricing")

Advertising is how widely you make something known; branding is what you make known. Bud Light's campaign was strong at the first and poor at the second for its core buyer, and the same pairing could be good on both counts for a different who. Judge them separately.

### Four levers before you call a market capped (vid: 9gVdCR7W8o8 — "The REAL Reason")

Layers: method (ads, content, outreach, referrals, affiliates), platform (Facebook, YouTube, phone, email), media within the platform (Instagram DMs, stories, posts, reels), and volume in each. Pull them in order: another method, another platform, more media, more volume. To size a niche, add up the ad spend of every agency selling to it. Think like an investor: if you hold under 1% of a $60B industry, you are not saturated.

### Second channel: hire a person to build it, judge progress not sales (vid: HKbFUWJwEG0 — "Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business")

Gym Launch ran only Facebook and Instagram paid. A rep from a rival he'd never heard of said they did about $10M a month, all outbound with 30+ reps (the rep's figure). Hormozi hired him to start a department of one; 12 months later outbound was 50% of revenue. Rules: exhaust more and better on the main channel first (he says one offer, one avatar, one channel until $1M a year), let someone else own the new one so the main doesn't drop, copy where same-size competitors acquire, and score it stage by stage: clicks, opt-ins, booked, shows, right people, sales. It can take months to first sale.

### Small player winning shelf space from a distributor (vid: Lc8DNduiwKA — "13 Ways to DESTROY Your Competition")

The distributor can pick a bigger brand, so pay in perks the big brand will not bother with: a lower unit cost, a retail kit that moves more product, free on-site staff training. In return you ride all the marketing the retailer already does.

### Six ways sorted: platforms and people (vid: FKWEybUIda4 — "I quit advertising")

Platforms: paid, earned, owned media. People: manual outbound, affiliates, referrals. All are linear except referrals, the one that compounds. Non-consumer giants mostly grew on the people side; consumer brands act like media companies. Master one first, then add. Shifting to people lowered his risk and made the business more sellable.

### Your friends list already gave permission (vid: FKWEybUIda4 — "I quit advertising")

Owned media includes every social connection. Message them with no opt-in. Founders ask how to get a first customer with thousands of people they have never messaged.

### 160 reach-outs in eight weeks is not the rule of 100 (vid: sSEm3qJUh9s — "Poor and weak")

A reader said he tried it. He was about 9,000 short: it's 100 a day. He'd take 70% intensity for ten years over 120% for a month. Sustained volume beats bursts; persistence itself gets built by sticking with things.

### Rising CAC may be word of mouth, not the platform (vid: WsYgWC7NmO8 — "Top 10 Fast Business Lessons")

Brand is keeping promises beyond what you made. It works as an invisible hand: people have heard of you before the ad, so returns climb. Overpromise and it reverses: negative word of mouth spreads harder than positive, and CAC rises past what ad costs explain while owners blame pixels and platforms.

### Amazon as a traffic source, not just a store (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

Extends platform trust lifts conversion. His book page converts about 30% of viewers against around 2% he cites for Shopify. His first book outsold his email list when he had roughly 10K followers, and his books sell almost 1,000 a day: that many new customers daily, who find him only because of the CTAs and QR codes inside the product. The only way to grow there is a good product, so he puts his best work on it even though it is free elsewhere. For creators with nothing to sell: a small Amazon affiliate cut on a 15x conversion can beat 20-30% on a 2% page, and it reads as a recommendation, not an ask.
