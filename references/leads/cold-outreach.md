# Cold Outreach
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## Three Problems Strangers Create (and Solutions)

### Problem 1: How do I contact them?
Build a list 3 ways:
1. **Scrape**: Subscribe to lead scraping/outbound softwares. Search criteria, test a sample of a few hundred, buy more if good.
2. **Buy from Brokers**: List brokers buy and sell contact lists. Ask for sample, test, scale winners.
3. **Elbow Grease**: Join groups/communities where your audience hangs out. Check directories for contact info. Reach out outside the group to avoid getting kicked out.
- **Interest groups are the warmest cold audience** -- problem-aware people concentrated in one place.

### Problem 2: What do I say?
Personalize + give big fast value:
- **Personalize**: Act like you know them. Match area code, use their name, reference something about them/their business. "Make cold outreach feel like warm outreach."
- **Give BIG FAST value**: Not tickling interest -- blowing their minds in 30 seconds. Your lead magnet must be EXCEPTIONAL. "So good people feel stupid saying no."
- **Write at below 3rd grade reading level**: 50% response bump from rewriting templates at this level. Run all scripts through a free reading level app.
- Scripts are never more than 1-2 pages (phone/chat) or half a page (email). First message may be 2-3 sentences.

### Problem 3: Not getting enough chances.
Increase volume 3 ways:
1. **Automate Delivery**: Live (highest response, lowest scale) vs Recorded (lower response, highest scale). Voice memos, handwritten cards = high response. Templated emails, pre-recorded voicemails = high scale.
2. **Automate Distribution**: Manual clicking send vs robot blasting thousands at once.
3. **Follow Up More Times in More Ways**: More ways (email + DM + call + text) = more likely to reach them. More times = shows you're serious.

## Voicemail Script (Using Third-Party Trust)
"Hey John, it's Alex. I'm calling in reference to [their competitor/neighbor]. Please give me a call back at [number]."
- Short: no overthinking needed
- Relevant: mentions a company they know
- Opens a loop: curiosity about what it's about

## What NOT to Do on Calls
- Don't say "This is [first name]..." (too formal, too salesy)
- Don't mention your company name
- Don't fail to follow up
- DO pause after your name (implies they should search memory for you)

## Cold Outreach Benchmarks
- 100 cold calls/day -> 25% pickup rate -> 25 pickups -> ~4 become engaged leads = 1 engaged lead per hour
- Cost = $0 (just your time/food)
- Pro tip: Give each rep explicit number of leads per week (~70 if capacity is 100). "No Leads Left Behind."
- 70 leads -> expect 7 appointments back. That's the quota.

## Scaling Example
- One virtual assistant sends 2,000 emails/day using multiple softwares
- Generates 40 engaged leads/day
- 10% close = 4 customers/day
- Customer LTV = $20,000+
- Unlocked cold outreach to pass $1M/month

## Why Cold Outreach Is Worth It (Pros)
1. Don't need lots of content or ads
2. Competition can't see what you're doing (everything private)
3. Incredibly reliable -- numbers work like clockwork
4. Fewer platform changes (cold call laws barely changed in decades)
5. Less compliance hassle (only legal, not platform rules)
6. Creates a more sellable business (team of 20 callers works without owner)
7. Hard to copy (private = no one knows your scripts/offers)

---

## Pro Tips
- New rep? Copy the best rep exactly, do TWICE the volume. Don't change anything until you beat their numbers.
- After contacting list, wait 3-6 months, start from top again (they didn't see it, couldn't respond, or circumstances changed).
- Response rate dropped with automation but volume made up for it -- always calculate: reach-outs x response rate = output.

## COLD OUTREACH ADDITIONS (Batch 5-6)

### AI in Cold Outreach Arms Race
- "As AI becomes crazier and crazier, there will be a continuous arms race between people sending and people receiving"
- Question will be: "Is this legitimate or is this AI that said my name?"
- AI enables perfect combination: max personalization + max volume
- "Every day data becomes more accessible to find personal data"
- Softwares now exist that can scrape personalization data from groups automatically

### AI personalizes the first text (vid: zzleYxkf39k — "Scale or Fail: Naz, moving")

Feed the agent list to AI, research each, output a personalized first message in a sheet, skim, and send. It may raise replies and cut spam flags; test against generic. Consider following them, then DMing anyone who follows back, plus blue-bubble iMessage.

### Laid-off bookkeeper diagnosis: 10,000 reach-outs, not a better service (vid: 3Cw_BR6utgc — "Advice for Hard Days")

Solo bookkeeping and CFO services founder, back at a day job after three years. Diagnosis: the skill was fine, promotion was missing. Alex guessed he ran on a few referral clients and never did four hours of promotion a day.
Prescription: 4 hours nights and weekends, one post a day plus outreach, message everyone who engages, 10,000 people total. A commoditized service is a gift: demand is proven, and few owners love their bookkeeper. Faster: pitch 1,000 B2B firms with adjacent audiences (HR, recruiting) on referral swaps or kickbacks. Early content is nurture for those who check your page, not lead gen.

### Cold call stuck on I am busy: shrink the ask, enlarge the reason (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Busy means the perceived cost beats the perceived benefit. Fix both sides. Do not ask for 15 minutes, which sounds huge to a stranger; ask for 60 or 120 seconds, and open with a big offer that earns them.
He cites a street mentalist's opener: asking strangers for just 30 seconds. Once they repeated it back, he knew they were hooked, and it turned into 10 minutes.
Then keep dialing: expect plenty of misses.

### Robotic cotton candy: when 100 emails closes a deal, you owe the volume (vid: TLpLmhWqKEU — "Key man risk and caller breakdowns")

Machine costs about $15,000. Cold email: 100 sends gets 2 to 3 replies and most close (Hormozi models a conservative 1 per 100). Four closers, one setter, reps hunting their own leads. His only action: go from 100 emails to 5,000 a day.
Automate it, reps do not send. Warm the domains, one sending name per closer plus backups, AI personalization on the front. Each closer gets about 10 leads a day, first come first served, and keeps working the phones. His own closers still do two hours of outbound daily to stay sharp and value handed leads.
Also: Meta not working means your ads and conversion process did not, not the channel.

### Punching above your case studies: sink price and work to zero (vid: WeH2E1fGgaw — "Daily Q&A: selling, comp and failure")

People refuse because of price, work or risk. A small-client provider chasing bigger clients asks them to carry all three. Take price and work to zero, so only outcome risk remains.
How Hormozi's shorts editor got him: I see your YouTube videos, I will clip them, you do nothing, no pay, just permission to post or I send them to you. Hormozi said send some and he would look.
Good clips earned posting rights, better results earned original shorts, and it grew to him flying in quarterly to record 100 shorts in a day. Do a lot with a little and you get given more. Premium prices come after the track record.

### Fitness coach outbound fix: one better post, way more free service (vid: 9OsUfwTTslc — "Goal setting 1/0 live Q&A")

Online coach doing about 75 outreaches a day plus 2-4 posts a day, 4 customers in 5-6 weeks. Prescription: cut to one good post a day, put the saved time into a stronger give.
In fitness, free PDFs won't move anyone. Give time: a FaceTime walk through their kitchen, or a guided grocery run. After an hour they ask how to get more, and you book tomorrow's sales talk. Rename strategy call, since people read it as a sales call.
Opening message and offer are the biggest outbound levers. At one customer every 10 days, one doubling gets every 5, two doublings every other day. Cold needs an insanely good reason to reply; warm just needs trust.

### Highest-converting cold call offer he has seen: result first, bill later (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

A pure cold-call team sold automated map listings: I will put you in the top five on Google Maps in seven days, call you back then, and if you want to keep it we start billing. Owners saw it working and didn't want to lose it. He kept them at $300-400 a month.
Grinding or killing an outbound motion (roofer caller at zero sales): not getting the right people on the phone means you can't judge it yet, so keep going. On the phone with no movement is an offer problem, so lead with big fast free value that others charge for.

### 100 outreaches a day for years and broke: run the math on yourself (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Caller claims years of daily 100s, courses and no pub, still broke, asks if it's a waiting game. Hormozi's math: 100 a day for three years is about 100,000 people. He refuses to believe that many turned down free work.
His prescription: you went straight for the sale on something nobody wanted. Offer the work free, fill the client load, then charge once you're full. Non-buyers still give testimonials or referrals.
Saying you incorporated the feedback doesn't make it true; if you had iterated you would be better. Believing you are working may be what stops the real work. Doing unreasonable volume with no result is a signal to change the thing, not wait for luck.

### What an outbound ramp really looks like (vid: k834E6OTGTM — "$100M Leads launch event")

Gym Launch, started during COVID when ads failed and 30% of gyms closed for good. Sales by month as he listed them: Sept 0, Oct 2, Dec 4, Jan 6, Feb 10, Mar 14, Apr 20, May 30, reaching $480K monthly revenue. Hired one rep who claimed he'd be profitable in 90 days. Outbound later passed 50% of sales, and the team has spoken to 25,000 gym owners out of about 150,000, one in six.
Expect a first month of zero. Its other edge: competitors can't see it. He didn't know a $10M-a-month outbound company existed in his own space.

### Personal iMessage and voice note, even at scale (vid: H_ZLs1-jOKQ — "Stop Being Broke. Get Busy.")

A green text from a chat tool reads as automation, so response falls. Companies of his doing tens and hundreds of millions still have reps send personalized voice notes and videos via iMessage from a real iPhone. Buy the reps phones when you can; until then, use what you have. Efficiency per lead matters most when you're small.

### Zero-start partner play: show up to the influencer with the work done (vid: IFpHasZ7jN4 — "54.1% First Dollar Online")

What he'd do at zero with no money or name. Pick 10 mid-size accounts you already follow, with real engagement, not giants. DM commenters, record 5 interviews about what they'd want, and build a deck with rollout phases and a 1% audience conversion estimate. On the call: I only want one partner, you get all of me, not a rep. If they pass, the deck goes in front of the next one as portfolio, so the work is never wasted. Take a small share (20% is fine) and get paid to learn. Two operators with no presence did this, editing and running everything for a creator from 50K to 3M followers and adding about $40K in recurring revenue their first month.

### Lead source bake-off (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

Extends the scrape-and-sample step. Scraping is usually cheapest, so start there. Run the same avatar search in the top four or five tools, work a sample of 100 to 500 from each, and double down on whichever gets the most responses. Compare lead sources on replies, not list size.

### Fund outbound at 10-20% of ad spend (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

He puts 10-20% of ad spend into outbound with no ROI expected for two months. It took about a quarter to pay, and he reports about 20:1 since. First call your own leads, then just start: he says $50K of outbound programs went unused, since each niche differs. He gives anyone six months at 100 reach-outs a day.

### Three-tier outbound machine with the math (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

Gym Launch org chart, shaped like a Christmas tree: dialers (BDRs) feed half as many value-call specialists (SDSs), who feed half as many closers. VAs in the Philippines scrape and verify the list. Daily floor: 100 dials, about 20 pickups, 1-2 booked calls that actually show. Dialers are paid only on shows, $50 each, and about 10% of shown SDS calls close, so about $500 of dialer cost per deal against $40K LTV. His floor for this model is a ticket of about $15K. About 75% of results came from phones, and he says local-area-code wrapping is non-negotiable for pickup. Month 14: about 60% of sales volume.

### Sell four-hour dialing shifts, not eight (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

His sales manager's experience: pay eight hours and you get about five and a half of real calling, so pay four and get three and a half. Same 100-dial expectation. Side benefit: good salespeople can moonlight the shift. Dialers ramp in about two days, so he stopped screening hard for them; his team had been screening too much when the fix was to let everyone in and let results sort them, since a trial costs almost nothing.

### Value call, then switch hats (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

The cold call offers a real piece of the paid product, not a lead magnet (for gyms: how to set up the sales room and the script). Second call: 45 minutes, about 30 delivering it and 15 left for the turn. Staffed almost entirely by ex-clients so domain expertise shows. The turn: ask permission to switch hats, say what stood out, call what you just showed a tiny piece of the upside, and only then ask if they know what the company actually does. It is the first mention of the company in two calls.

### Book the sales call by putting the closer on live (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

SDSs could set close appointments but prospects wouldn't show, and three-way texts did not fix it (about two months of testing). What worked: call the closer on speakerphone mid-Zoom, with a scripted "I am slammed, is he big enough for us?" exchange the prospect overhears. The closer joins for a few minutes only to build rapport and states plainly that the next call covers pricing and fit, and is a sales call. A face seen once shows up.

### X and LinkedIn: compliment first, and tiny followings can pay (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

X DMs: never open with an ask. Name one specific thread and say why it was good; respond only if they thank you, since someone who ignores a compliment was never going to engage. Example he reports: a CRO agency serving e-commerce brands over $10M a year did $5M a year off 8,000 X followers, using split test threads and a calendar link in the bio. LinkedIn is his outbound pick: Sales Navigator targets by role, location and age, so the smallest following can make the most per follower.
