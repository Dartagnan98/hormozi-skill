# Lead Magnets
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## Definition
A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem** given away at a significant discount or free to attract ideal customers.

## Why They Work
- People who pay with time now are more likely to pay with money later.
- Lead magnets get 3-5x more people to engage than pitching core offer alone.
- Costco food samples: giving away free food drives more purchases than no samples.
- The solved narrow problem reveals the next bigger problem (which your core offer solves).

---

## 3 Types of Lead Magnets (How to Solve)

### 1. Reveal a Problem (Diagnosis/Assessment)
- Works great for problems that get worse while you wait (termite inspections, posture analysis, website speed tests, financial audits)
- Creates implied urgency because deprivation increases with every second after they find out
- Example: "4 reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency" (Hormozi's highest-converting ad ever for Allen)
- Bonus: not only reveal the problem but show what it COULD look like if solved, and the delta

### 2. Free Trial / Sample (Taste Test)
- Limit by: number of uses, quantity, time, or combination
- The Costco sampler, try-before-you-buy
- Give solution then REMOVE it to create deprivation that drives purchase
- Example: Gym Launch gave agencies services free for 4 months, then charged after they got used to having leads

### 3. One Step of Many (Give away first step of a multi-step process)
- Particularly effective for complex products/services
- Example: First coat of paint (need 3 more), first laser hair removal session (need 6-8), first 2 videos of a comprehensive course
- Example: Hormozi's books solve "what to sell" and "how to get leads" but not the full scaling picture

---

## 4 Delivery Mechanisms

### 1. Software/Tools
- Spreadsheets, calculators, dashboards, assessment tools, small software
- Example: Neil Patel's URL analyzer tool

### 2. Information
- Courses, lessons, interviews, keynotes, tips, templates, guides
- Infinitely scalable, costs nothing, provides tremendous value

### 3. Services
- Do work for free. People SLEEP on this.
- Creates massive goodwill. People know it costs you something.
- High interface time = soft sell opportunities
- Example: Hormozi ran gym owners' ads free for 30 days -> they made money -> wanted to keep paying
- Math: 1 hour of labor ($25 cost), worth $250+ to market, 1 in 4 converts = $100 CAC. Not bad.
- Only give to QUALIFIED prospects (have budget, authority, need, timing)

### 4. Physical Products
- Books, supplements, branded items, t-shirts, samples
- Example: T-shirt that says "CEO" at a conference -> only given to verified CEOs -> qualified lead list

## Naming Your Lead Magnet

**This is the most important part.** How you name it determines engagement more than anything else.

- Hormozi split-tested 6 different headlines for $100M Leads book
- One change (first 5 seconds of a video) took it from 4,000 views to 850,000 (200x)
- David Ogilvy: "After you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar."
- Test names by: running small ad tests, polling audience, posting A/B on stories, DMing contacts

### Naming Formulas That Work
- **Number + Outcome + Timeframe**: "3 emails that turn cold leads into clients in 24 hours"
- **How to YAY without BOO if GREATEST INSECURITY**: "How to build a funnel without hiring a copywriter even if you've never done it before"
- **Adjective + Type + Good Thing**: "The lazy funnel template that converts like crazy"
- **X Mistakes**: "5 ad copy mistakes that are killing your conversion rate"

---

## Making It Easy to Consume
- Offer multiple formats: ebook, audio, video, physical (Hormozi has nearly perfect split across all 4)
- Software: accessible on phone and computer, multiple devices
- Services: available more times, more ways, more days
- Physical products: simple to open, simple to use

---

## Making It Good
- Zero fluff. Give away the secrets, sell the implementation.
- If you only have one valuable thing, learn another and give the first away free.
- The market judges you on your FREE stuff, not your paid stuff. 80%+ of people will never buy but will have opinions.
- Bad free stuff DISCOURAGES people who otherwise would have bought.
- People make purchasing decisions based on prediction of future value. Best predictor = value already received.

---

## The Call to Action
- Clear, direct language. Not clever. "If you're doing X, go to [URL]."
- Include a reason to act NOW (urgency, scarcity, or any reason at all)
- **Fraternity Party Planner principle**: Any reason is better than no reason. "Timmy got his wisdom teeth out -- kegger!" Your business anniversary, your dog's birthday, National Dog Day -- it doesn't matter.
- Weave in scarcity (limited quantity) and/or urgency (limited time)
- Service businesses inherently have capacity limits -- USE THEM. "I can only take 4 more clients."

## FROM $100M LOST CHAPTERS

### Free Presentations (Full Chapter)

**Three reasons free presentations work:**
1. **Captive audience** -- they chose to attend, they're sitting there, they're paying attention
2. **Long enough time** -- 45-90 minutes gives you enough time to break beliefs and build trust
3. **Offer at peak motivation** -- by the end, they're at maximum desire and minimum skepticism

**Presentation Benchmarks (Conversion Rates):**
| Format | Close Rate | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Dinner seminar | 25% to lower offer / 8% to higher | Most intimate, highest cost per attendee |
| Masterclass (live/virtual) | 10% | Scalable, good for mid-ticket |
| Challenge (multi-day) | 2-5% | Lowest close rate but largest audience, best for low-ticket or list building |
| Summit/conference | 10% | Multiple speakers, social proof environment |
| Strategy call (1-on-1) | 25% | Highest close rate, least scalable |

**Key stat:** Gym Launch found that **78% of customers consumed 2+ long-form content pieces** before buying. Free presentations serve as that long-form content.

### BANT Qualification for Strategy Calls
Use BANT to qualify who gets a strategy call (don't waste calls on unqualified leads):
- **B**udget: Do they have the money or access to it?
- **A**uthority: Are they the decision maker?
- **N**eed: Do they actually have the problem you solve?
- **T**iming: Are they ready to act now (not "someday")?

### Three Core Limiting Beliefs (to Address in Presentations)
Every prospect has three beliefs preventing purchase:
1. **"Results must come easy"** -- If it takes work, the product must be bad
2. **"I must get the best results possible"** -- Perfectionism prevents starting
3. **"Results must lead to attention/approval"** -- Only values externally validated outcomes

**How to break each:**
- Belief 1: Tell stories of people who did the work and got massive results. Reframe effort as investment.
- Belief 2: Show that "good enough" results still transform their life. Perfect is the enemy of done.
- Belief 3: Show internal transformation stories -- confidence, peace, freedom -- not just external status.

### The name does the selling, because nobody has read it yet (vid: lidFtb1Ga0o — "He Makes $1.5M and Keeps 1.3%")

Same caller had put huge effort into a free book, audiobook and course. Hormozi: when the lead decides, the title is the only part they've consumed, so packaging is the biggest lever on all that work.
Test titles with cheap ads and read CTR, then test on the landing page. Mismatched covers are harmless since it's free. Value only builds trust if they consume it, so also chop the magnet into hundreds of content pieces that each point back to the whole.

### Lighten the ask: rotate magnets, then triage into the sale (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

Pitching every post tanks reach because the CTA kills view-through. Keep the CTA to 3 seconds or less, run only about two direct book-a-call posts a week, and make the rest lighter lead magnet posts (seven tips, cheat sheets) in rotation.
The DM triage line turns the free ask into a sales conversation: ask whether they came for the free stuff or want the problem solved for good.
His own data: the scaling roadmap has lower immediate ROAS than direct-to-workshop ads, but beats it over a 60 to 90 day window with about 10x the reach. Direct asks only reach people already warm. Also: selling high ticket from TikTok, make the CTA a DM on Instagram.

### The missed lead magnet step, plus a 10-business test (vid: k834E6OTGTM — "$100M Leads launch event")

Of his seven lead magnet steps, the last gets skipped most and explains why giveaways produce no buyers: give them a simple way to raise a hand for the next thing. The other common miss is solving a problem that doesn't lead to the purchase.
Test on 10 businesses (chiro, dental, PT, home services, float tanks, more): added monthly revenue averaged $6K, then $19K, then $34,972 by month three, including those who did nothing. Also note 99% will never buy; a free solution is what builds your reputation with them.

### Paid lead magnet math: massage example (vid: 7NMH1oAkgLY — "Giving Away Free Stuff")

Illustrative: pitch a $100 massage and perhaps one person says yes. Pitch $10 and 20 say yes, and about a third then buy at full price, so six customers instead of one. If each trial costs $20 to deliver you lose $10 each, $200 to land six customers perhaps worth $2,000. Even a money-losing magnet should lower total cost per customer. Freebies that cost you real money usually get valued more than a PDF.

### Solve step one fully so step three is for sale (vid: 7NMH1oAkgLY — "Giving Away Free Stuff")

Extends One Step of Many. Map before, during and after for the customer's journey, then give away the early steps complete. Weight loss: a pre-loaded Instacart list by weight, goal and sex is one-time work for a hundred types of people. That creates the next problem: what to do with all this food. Then storage and portioning. Each solved step predicts value from the next. If your magnet could be your competitor's core offer, you become hard to beat.

### Split-test the title in rounds (vid: 7NMH1oAkgLY — "Giving Away Free Stuff")

Extends "the name does the selling." Round one: Advertising beat Promotions 69 to 31. Round two: Leads beat Advertising 58 to 42. Then the subhead and cover image. He also tells of a dating book that tanked, then sold over 10M copies after a new cover and title with nothing else changed.

### Look at what everyone charges for, make it better, make it free (vid: IFpHasZ7jN4 — "54.1% First Dollar Online")

His go-to customer-getting move, used on the Skool Games: rivals charge for calls, courses, community and tools, so give those away for a limited window, 14 free days inside a 30-day game, with a top-10 trip as the prize. Set the bar low: three members in your group unlocks the bonus downloads. Peers starting at zero, coaches and alumni all add accountability.

### Challenges fake a brand: microwave the prospect (vid: OpeN4O5myIg — "This Email Campaign Generates Sales")

Direct response uses challenges because it has no brand. A 7-day challenge crams months of relationship into a week, as a marriage reality show does, warming fast where branding slow cooks. Pull one piece that already works out of the product, with a mini outcome, say a 7-day cleanse before a long weight loss goal. It scores on all four value drivers, and they arrive at the offer having acted and been rewarded.

### Free group to paid group, with the webinar disguised as a course (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

A Skool Games winner's funnel: content and profile visits go to a link in bio, then a free group. Joining fires an auto DM; a pinned post points to a "course" that is a webinar cut into modules (intro, three core stories, then the offer), so people don't feel pitched; then a call. His figures: about 400 to 450 free members, 53 sales into a $999 a month group, and the same process took a client from $500 to $10,900 a month on short form. The free group looks dead because the engaged people moved up, which Hormozi reads as the funnel working. Hormozi's simple version: low ticket, add a personalized onboarding call and sell on it; high ticket, put the VSL or webinar in onboarding.

### Free thing as a work problem: the tall ladder (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

A caller couldn't work out what free offer would stand out. His diagnosis: she wasn't used to the work level required. He put 200 hours into one 90-minute free talk, and six hours a morning for two years into a book. The best fruit is in plain view on a tall ladder. Block four hours a day for a month, make it better rather than longer, and document the making so people see it wasn't a PDF whipped up in a day.

### Four ways to set calls from a free group (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

1) Free onboarding call as a joining bonus, which turns into a pitch if they fit. 2) Five-video series as a split VSL: opportunity and story, then three belief breakers, then stack and CTA. 3) Weekly value webinar with setters in the chat pulling engaged people out by DM. 4) DM setters work anyone who likes, comments or posts. He says group funnels are taking cost per call from about $250 to $40; good ones convert about 10% of members, from a third getting on calls and 30-50% closing.

### Representative case studies as the freebie (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

For a vacation rental manager recruiting owners with idle second homes: five to ten case studies of different home types so each owner spots one like theirs and gets a rough earnings idea, then FAQ answers (trashing, security, cleaning), proof, and a personal estimate as the call offer.

### Comment keyword into funnel, and offer custom not more (members' methods) (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

From community members on a call. Post or go live with free value, ask for a comment or DM keyword, ManyChat sends the link to a lead magnet or an opt-in that captures name, email and phone, then the community. It reads warm because it looks personally sent; nudge after five minutes if no reply. Hormozi's guess: comment triggers likely lift reach and cost less friction than a DM. Live version: review a viewer's profile on air, then offer the same review weekly inside. On the CTA: "want more" fails because the feed has infinite more; "custom recipes for your macros" can only come through your thing.
