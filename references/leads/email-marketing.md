# Email Marketing
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## Why Email
- Average ROI: $35-$45 return per $1 spent
- You already paid to acquire the contact. Email is just follow-up.
- 61% check email multiple times/day, 20% check multiple times/hour
- Uniform across ALL age groups (15-24, 25-44, 45-64, 65+)
- You control delivery (unlike algorithm-dependent social posts)

---

## Hormozi's Email Structure (Mosy Money Minute)
1. **Subject line**: Brand name + topic (building brand recognition)
2. **Opening quote/tweet**: Immediate reward for opening. Value in one glance.
3. **Meat**: Under 200 words (~1 minute read). ONE concept. One clear tactic.
4. **CTA**: Contextual to the content topic (beginners -> school.com, advanced -> acquisition.com/workshops)
5. **PS statement**: Meme, joke, or secondary CTA. "Not having a PS is PS stupid."

---

## Key Email Tactics

1. **Make unsubscribing easy**: Don't keep people captive. Dormant subscribers hurt deliverability worse than unsubscribes. First email will have higher unsubscribe (shaking the tree). Hormozi's: 1% first email -> 0.042% ongoing.

2. **Segment your list**: HubSpot research shows segmented lists get way higher returns. Send right content to right people (beginner vs advanced).

3. **Mobile-optimized design**: 58-60% of traffic is mobile. Check both desktop and mobile display.

4. **Consistent layout/template**: Figure out what works, repeat. People expect McDonald's consistency.

5. **Plain text, minimal links**: Emails should look like normal emails you'd exchange. No Vegas Strip design. Fewer images = better deliverability. Less money language = stays out of promo tab.

6. **Optimize preview text**: 24%+ of people check preview text before opening. Don't let it default to "Hey John..."

7. **Clear, contextual CTA**: Bridge the CTA to the content topic. Don't copy-paste the same generic CTA.

8. **AB test subject lines**: Most platforms can test a small segment, then send winner to full list.

9. **Get replies**: When someone replies, it signals the email server that you're legitimate. Ask a question that requires "yes" reply to unlock bonus content.

10. **Speed to contact**: Harvard Business Review: calling leads within 60 seconds of opt-in = 391% increase in sales (4x).

---

## Email Cadence
- Perfect cadence: ~3x/week (but not a rule)
- Start with once/month if you haven't been emailing
- Block out 12-24 emails at once (half a day of work = year of emails)
- More often you email = more you convert, as long as quality stays high
- Never commit to a cadence that forces low-quality output

---

## Email Benchmarks
- Hormozi's open rate: ~35.7% (last 90 days)
- Hormozi's CTR: 8.5% (very strong)
- B2B: 16.99% of marketing emails don't even reach inbox
- Best days: B2C = Monday/Tuesday, B2B = Wednesday
- Best times: 10am-noon and 1pm-3pm

### A soft PS is your CTA (vid: B-ogfFiQpXg — "Scale or Fail: Dr. Zad, concierge medicine")

Newsletters with no call to action leave money behind. Keep it low pressure: PS, this quarter we're running a drawing for a year of premium service, enter anyone you know. They do a friend a favor, and a quarterly giveaway to the list may produce a lot of business on its own.

### Reactivation as mini-campaigns (extends I owe you money) (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Run one email a month to the old list, but organize it as angle plus proof: a stat email, then a follow-up case study about one customer with that issue. Test frames: I owe you a free checkup or $175 of value; we made a mistake on your account. Bold the at-no-cost part because people scan. Angles for Corey: billing savings, allergies, environment.

### Pre-event show-up stack (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Four moves between registration and a weekly webinar: 9 emails rotating angles (pain, speed, ease, stuck) and alternating with proof; SMS added; behavior trigger, so anyone who clicks or opens gets behind-the-scenes material; voicemail drops. Forced engagement in emails also protects deliverability. One reminder a day is fine. The three that must go by SMS: 24 hours before, morning of, one hour before. He bets SMS did the most.

### Ration your asks so each one lands (vid: 60_7PU9JDIw — "$100M Leads affiliate guide")

He said he emailed his list only 3-4 times in a year while publishing about 250 pieces of content a week, and only for things he was sure would over-deliver. Two emails to that list asking people to help promote brought about 9,000 affiliate registrants. Limit recruitment asks to 2-3 across a campaign, and write them knowing customers will read them too, so no talk of sales targets.

### Low-repeat products: two emails a week forever (vid: HGZOxBfnF-E — "$1M Business for a Stranger")

No continuity fit, so stay first in mind for their next project. A 10,000-person list on a $2.5M business is likely valuable. Mail the whole list twice weekly, rotating before-and-afters with FAQs from the 20 questions buyers always ask. Structure: subject, immediate reward such as a picture or quote, short point, CTA, friendly PS. For home-obsessed buyers this is entertainment, so they open it.

### The quarterly cleanup: a challenge sent to the dead list (vid: OpeN4O5myIg — "This Email Campaign Generates Sales")

Portfolio company, nine figures on paid ads, ignoring its old list until ads slipped. Sent a 7-day challenge to about 15% of the list, 464,000 people, many non-buyers. 10,000 registered, 800 bought at $2,000 ($1.6M), 20% of those ascended for another $800K: $2.4M, about $5 an email by the math (he rounds to six). A repeat next month on another segment did $2.2M. Do it once a quarter on 10% first, testing subject, CTA and page, before the whole list. His figures.

### Rewrap the same promo four times, then stop inventing (vid: OpeN4O5myIg — "This Email Campaign Generates Sales")

To a non-buyer, a new name is a new offer. His gym versions of one eat less, move more program: 42-day fix, six-week booty blast, Slim for Santa, Lean by Halloween. Change the name, page and subject, and hang it on a season or life event. This matters most for local businesses, where small audiences tire faster. Once you have four tested promos, the annual calendar just repeats. Between them, send value to earn the next ask.

### Ads are the paycheck, email is the 401(k) (vid: OpeN4O5myIg — "This Email Campaign Generates Sales")

Ads pay you back directly for work done; a list is an account that compounds. Two reasons it lifts enterprise value: acquisition cost on a list you already paid for is zero, so sales drop straight to the bottom line that buyers apply a multiple to; and list size cannot collapse overnight the way ads can, so it smooths volatility.

### Ten-day launch cadence (vid: 4PX-hE6LTSU — "#1 mistake gyms make")

Whisper a month out in warm groups: countdown graphic, no copy. Start the real push about 10 days out; earlier crescendos too soon. Email days 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, text on the off days, one bullet each. Each email takes one pain; recycle them, since not everyone reads all. On launch day he sends about six, sometimes subject line only, just to sit at the top of the inbox.

### You can market your face off and still be missed (vid: 4PX-hE6LTSU — "#1 mistake gyms make")

He mailed food to friends who had followed him forever. One had no idea it was his launch. Repeating yourself feels loud to you, and much of your list still hasn't seen it.

### Newsletter subscribers are customers, followers are not (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

Caller: a creator losing a six-figure brand deal. A 10M following no longer guarantees views, so content is a top-of-funnel skill judged per post. A newsletter list is an owned asset. A friend's subscriber data, per Hormozi: hyper-niche, higher-priced newsletters get the best LTV. Flow: content feeds the list, then sell ads or your own product.

### Quarterly four-day push (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

Most lists are never really squeezed. Between giving value and plain statements of what you do, once a quarter promote hard for four days and close with real scarcity. On his due-diligence checklist for any company.

### Nine-word email (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

He credits Dean Jackson. Send the list only this, as he gives it: "Do you still need help getting that problem solved?" Name the problem, add nothing else, and replies come back.

### Email and podcast are nurture buckets, not growth channels (vid: wR8KoE8u1p0 — "Tier List, Making Money Online")

A great email or episode rarely grows either one; big podcasters mostly grow on YouTube. Feed both from discovery platforms and use them to deepen. Podcast edge: a listener keeps three to five shows, against thousands of accounts fighting for a scroll, so holding the slot means keeping that person. Podcast weakness: trust is high, but they are driving or lifting and must type a URL later, which most won't. Email is the reverse: least compliance risk, near-free delivery, the 36:1 average return he cites. His own confession: he under-sends because he is too particular about the words.
