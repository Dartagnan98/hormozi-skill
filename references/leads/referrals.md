# Referral Systems & Employees as Lead Getters
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## Core Principle
If you solve someone's problem well, they become a walking advertisement. Referrals are the highest-leverage lead getting method.

## Getting More Referrals
- Ask at the point of peak satisfaction (right after they get a result)
- Ask specifically: "Who do you know that's also dealing with [problem]?"
- Make it easy to refer (give them a link, a card, a script)
- Reward referrals (discounts, bonuses, cash, recognition)

## Employees as Lead Getters

### Why Employees Make You Wealthy
- Same business, same $2M income:
  - Owner does everything = net worth is cash in bank
  - Owner works 10-20 hrs/week with team = net worth is cash + $10M+ in equity
- A business that runs without you is sellable. One that requires you is a high-paying job.

### Wrong Beliefs to Replace
- "If you want it done right, do it yourself" -> "Get someone to spend ALL their time on it"
- "No one can do it like me" -> "If I can do it, someone can do it BETTER"
- "I'm irreplaceable" -> "Everyone is replaceable, especially me"

### The Internal Core Four (Getting Employee Leads)
Same framework, different nomenclature:
- Warm Outreach = Asking your network for referrals
- Cold Outreach = Headhunting/recruiting
- Posting Content = Job postings on personal/business pages
- Paid Ads = Promoted job postings

### Lead Getters for Employees
- Customer referrals = Employee referrals
- Affiliates = Community owners (editors, salespeople)
- Employees getting leads = In-house recruiters
- Agencies = Staffing firms, paid headhunters

### Training: Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate
1. **Document**: Do the thing yourself, make a checklist (video yourself doing it)
2. **Demonstrate**: Do it in front of new employee
3. **Duplicate**: They do it in front of you, you give feedback
- Short windows to prove themselves. Below expectations at 3 weeks = cut them.
- Give the short window for entry-level; look for GRIT as the meta-skill.

### Calculating Employee ROI
- Total payroll / total engaged leads = cost per engaged lead
- Example: $100K/month payroll, 1,000 engaged leads = $100/lead
- 10 leads per customer = $1,000 CAC
- If LTV is 4:1 on that = profitable
- If CAC is 3x+ above industry average, diagnose: Are engaged leads qualified? (No = advertising problem) Are they qualified but not buying? (Sales problem)

### A-Player Arbitrage
- Pay 25% above market = get 5x the output
- One A-player is worth five B-players
- "Great lawyers are expensive. Cheap lawyers cost a fortune."

## REFERRALS ARE EXPONENTIAL, NOT LINEAR (Deep Pass Batch 2)

- Two bring four, four bring eight -- compounds
- Book example: one post with 20K followers -> still sells more copies every month than the month before
- 25,000 copies/month, ~1,000/day, no marketing spend, 14,000 5-star reviews
- "People obsessed with their product are the ones that get the compounding"
- This is why product quality is the ultimate growth lever: referrals compound naturally when the product is exceptional

### Who are you bringing next time? (vid: B-ogfFiQpXg — "Scale or Fail: Dr. Zad, concierge medicine")

Not asking for referrals; assuming it. Each scheduled visit ends with booking the next and asking who they'll bring, since doing health with others works better. Give members a set number of plus-ones worth real money, one per person. Marginal cost is close to nothing. Partners sign up each other.

### Give treatment, not credit (vid: B-ogfFiQpXg — "Scale or Fail: Dr. Zad, concierge medicine")

A $500 credit means nothing to rich clients and makes a referral feel neutral. Make it social capital positive: a high-value treatment or year of care as the prize. Everyone entered gets a treatment anyway, and the big prize states the price, avoiding sticker shock. Quarterly drawing, mention via newsletter PS.

### Refer-to-win mechanic (vid: xRxVpjeHmeA — "Scale or Fail: Paul, adventure park")

Thank-you page: copy a link, get another entry per friend. If your friend wins, you win too. Then call the winner and the referrer on a three-way line. Giving something real away also yields good media.

### Brokerage-level deal, not agent by agent (vid: zzleYxkf39k — "Scale or Fail: Naz, moving")

Realtors don't care about $250. Give their clients a free VIP upgrade so they look connected. Then go to the broker with donuts and a happy agent: be first in the client packet. Each brokerage might mean five deals a month, 60 a year. Adding one a week stacks, and you'll need a relationship manager.

### Reward the crew for reviews (vid: zzleYxkf39k — "Scale or Fail: Naz, moving")

Have the foreman tell the customer that ownership gives the guys a bonus for five-star reviews, so it would mean a lot to them. Give $100 to the monthly leader. Customers help a person faster than an owner. Have him film before and after at both sites, ending on the review.

### Niche offer: recruit the audience owner, not generic referrers (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

Mortgage broker serving airline and military pilots used realtors as affiliates: outbound took real time, and few of their referrals were pilots since realtors know people generally. Better targets: owners of pilot communities and pilot merch businesses.
Also turn the existing course into a lead magnet that drops people into a free pilots-only group with a free onboarding call. The group nurtures those not ready yet and generates referrals. Take winning pilot content, add a CTA, run it as the ad. Interest-based ad delivery finds pilots if the content is about pilots.

### Wedding photographer: let the planner keep 100% of the first shoots (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

For a one-time local purchase, buyers ask around, so recruit the people they ask. Reach out to wedding planners and offer to shoot one or two of their clients at whatever price the planner can sell it for, with the planner keeping all of the money.
They now have a cash reason to push you, and you get to prove your quality. Then strike a standing deal and repeat planner by planner until each one is a distribution node feeding you customers.

### Saturated B2B lead gen: hitch to adjacent businesses, then show up (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Lead gen for MSPs. Speed order: ads are the fast button, then outbound, and content is usually slowest. The hidden fourth door: find about five non-competing adjacent businesses and pay them when they send you business. Their whole marketing and sales machine now works for you, and it's cash flow to them with no delivery, because most can't sell that lead anything anyway.
That gives you room for heavier outreach. Lumpy mail: an old direct response trick puts a crumpled sales letter in a tiny trash can, joking you'd have tossed it anyway. Follow up as the trash can guy. Say you'll be in the area next week; people grant the meeting, then fly out for a few hundred bucks if the deal justifies it.

### How he found the referral channel by accident (vid: k834E6OTGTM — "$100M Leads launch event")

Gym Launch's ads were shut off for two weeks and sales kept coming, which revealed $300K a week from referrals. He then interviewed the best businesses in his network for six months, found six shared factors, and tripled how many stayed and how many they sent.
Math to run: at 4:1 return, one referral from each customer makes it 8:1 before second-order referrals. Track referral percentage against churn percentage; grow forever by having more customers refer than exit.

### Stay narrow so high-ticket providers feed you (Corey, HVAC cleaning) (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Corey deliberately limits his services so he never competes with the replacement companies around him. He sends them the big jobs and they send him leads; one partner sends about $30K a month (his figures). Promising to refer back does not scale, though: with 20 partners you cannot send each one enough volume, so you need an offer that pays them without reciprocity.

### Partner offer: let them keep all the lead magnet money (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Hormozi's fix: partners sell your $175 inspection as an add-on and keep the whole fee. You do the work at cost and upsell the core job. His math: if a visit costs $25 and 1 in 3 buys, you pay $75 per customer. Even at $200 of cost per customer it is fine on a job that big. Referrals are something everyone promises and nobody delivers until the partner has a reason.

### Local affiliates: two offers, no payment (vid: 60_7PU9JDIw — "$100M Leads affiliate guide")

Every affiliate deal has an offer to the partner and one the partner makes on your behalf. Massage example: give a company hiring weekly two free massages per new hire for its onboarding packet. The employer looks caring, so no fee is needed, and it never sees you as competition. Or offer a chiropractor's patients a free massage. Refer business back to the partner and the loop feeds itself.

### CAC outrunning CPM reveals bad word of mouth (vid: EUW3rMp-Uvg — "Past $30M mistake")

Check: if cost per impression is up 10% and your acquisition cost is up 40%, the gap is likely reputation; people see the ad, recall something bad, and pass. Unhappy buyers also spread it far wider than happy ones (he says 10 times), and neutral isn't enough at scale. Positive word of mouth does the reverse, warming cold markets before your ad lands.

### Launch the book with the book's own methods (vid: Ktwv_uEW-uA — "What now?")

His rule for each book: prove the concept by using it on the launch itself. A leads book got ads he did not need, affiliates and customer referrals. The referral hook: bring 10 people and get two bonus chapters that are not in the published book.

### Full integration, not a stack of business cards (vid: FKWEybUIda4 — "I quit advertising")

Dropped cards get tossed. Map the partner's customer journey and put yourself in it. Dry cleaner plus fancy clothing store: a free first cleaning stapled to the bag, three set words at the counter, a fee per redemption. The store now offers more than the shop down the street, at no cost. Then repeat with 200 stores.

### Product is quadratic, marketing is linear (vid: ULGT0Qpglek — "17 Life-Changing Conversations")

Crediting Naval: good marketing or sales relates linearly to your audience; an exceptional product relates quadratically because each buyer tells two, who tell four. So don't launch a new product until the first is right, even if that takes years. $100M Offers still sells more copies each month with zero ad spend. Building the better thing is less total work than building a poor one and selling it forever.

### Who's your Michael Jordan: put the whole partner budget on one (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Nike took the money it spread across 5 to 10 second-tier players and handed it all to one rookie, then ate the category. Ask which single partner move makes the most money in the least time, then put your time there. A big audience is also an insurance policy: with a million people reached for free, the question shifts from whether a product makes money to which one makes the most, and even a poor fit still sells. His own proof: over $20M paid to Prestige Labs influencers, $22.8M to ALAN's, 23,000 affiliates on his last book.

### Whose brand goes on it: compare audience size (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Your audience bigger than theirs: they promote your brand (Nike, with a Jordan line because he was bigger inside basketball). Theirs bigger: you build and deliver a product under their name and stay invisible (Congo behind Logan Paul's Prime). Many small 100K partners take longer but you keep more ownership, since you are always the bigger party. One super influencer is faster and easier to manage but costs more of the pie. He now leans to the second for speed and brand.

### Contact circles for reaching an influencer (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Work outward: people you know with a bigger following than yours, then ask your network, offering a cut or cash bounty for an intro that lands, then people you follow on every channel, then agents. Hunt the quiet inbox: one of a thousand DMs on Instagram might be one of a few on LinkedIn. Others: direct mail (no unsubscribe) and the support email on their own site. His read on price: bigger business owners can often get real stars for a couple hundred grand a year, far below what people assume.

### Be worth answering: the influencer DM kit (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Pay for the check mark (he claims 5 to 10x reply rates), a close bright face photo, a bio that names the partner work you are pitching, a few real posts. Message shape: long-time fan first-time messenger; what we do with influencers; your likely monthly income from it given your following; screenshots of others this month; you keep the majority, we do the majority of the work; 15 minutes? Add cost you bear: "I'm out that way all the time" so a flight reads as no burden. A gift should carry their logo, not yours, and show effort. Sharing their stories gives them free content and creates a small debt they notice in the inbox.

### Pitch an influencer: promise, proof, plan, then offer (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Bring mockups of their product and a "you now vs you with me" graph built from posts per week, assumed reach and conversion. Promise: what they get. Proof: your track record, or stats from people like them, plus a demo. Plan: next steps, with your side visibly heavier. Then an offer that feels stupid to refuse. Bargaining chip: a mid-size influencer may want growth over money, so trade less economics for staff and content; if they get 10x bigger, so do you. Extends the deck-as-portfolio idea: show decks built for other names and they may ask for that structure, which starts the negotiation.

### Every position has an advantage: two scripts (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Told "easy for you, you're the 800-pound gorilla"? Small player's pitch: to him you're a number with an account rep; I want one partner, you have my cell and my full attention. Gorilla's pitch: best practices from thousands of accounts, specialists in every function, support that holds when it blows up; do you trust your brand to someone who may be gone in six months? Sell whichever is true of you.

### Influencer partner case: a sharper number (vid: Z2tgZC_XkT4 — "10x with Influencers")

Extends the zero-start partner play. Two 20-year-olds with no following reached a roughly 50K former pro bodybuilder, Eddie Abbew, who only wanted to make content. They became his only partner and did filming, editing, stories and community for a cut, reaching $44K a month in recurring revenue in 30 days, per Hormozi. A paid community is the one-size product when you have none: fans follow for access, so sell more of it.

### Full integration: solve the partner's activation problem (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

A webinar swap gives a blip; he wants a place in their onboarding. Example: trainer software can't retain trainers without clients, so offer to get them their first five. Prestige Labs sold inside each gym's nutrition consult 48 hours after signup, partly why it outgrew his gym company. For a PT consultant: offer brace and band makers to teach 10 of their PT accounts to sell their product. For the top 10 partners, spend real money to get the meeting: a TV, a watch.

### Become their affiliate first (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

If you don't send too much away, selling someone else's product for 40-50% of top line beats running it, because they do all the fulfillment on traffic you already paid for. Sell theirs first, then ask them to send people back. It works if done regularly. He cites about 4,000 Prestige Labs affiliates and about 400 agencies white-labeling Allan.

### Ask at the activation moment (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

Extends the favor gap. Gratitude peaks just after the favor and fades, while your sense of what you did grows. So place testimonial and referral asks at the first magic moment in onboarding. To find it, see what your longest customers did early. If you can't point to where the ask sits in the process, it doesn't happen.

### Skill value runs product, marketing, sales; most learn it backward (vid: qLM5G7N3l3I — "3 Ways to Do What You Love")

Extends product is quadratic. Sales is fastest to learn (a couple of weeks), and marketing is sales spread across media and many people. Marketing scales anything to a point, then the next customer costs more and more; without referrals from users, the company plateaus. So the highest leverage is a product others promote for you.

### "Who else" numbers, and asking on every deal (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

Extends the who-question. A new portfolio-company rep beat a veteran team's records with no better close rate: 30-50% more sales because he asked who else on every close, and about one in three gave one or two names. It was probably already in the SOP; reps skip it for fear of scaring the sale. Hormozi's benchmark: about 30% of business from referrals across his companies once you actually ask. Write referral names under the signature line of the contract so reps see the blank and each name traces to its source.

### Trade the discount for three names (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

When a buyer asks for a discount: sure, give me three friends, and we introduce them right now. He never discounts without a change in terms. Size it on CAC: if one in three referrals converts, three names is worth up to 1x normal CAC as a discount. Better version: offer extra service in place of price. $200 off costs $200; $400 of extra service at about 20% delivery cost runs about $80, feels like more, and gets them using it.

### "Want to know how to get this for free?" (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

Say it right after they pay; everyone leans in. Each friend who signs earns a cash check, $100 in his gyms, paid only on the friend's sale. Reference: about 30% of sales on each promotion came this way, and one woman, with a network of nurses or teachers, sent him 10 customers.

### Selfie text at the moment of success (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

At the result (house painted, door installed, dog trained, weight lost): capture the testimonial, tell them they're amazing, take a photo on their phone with you and the outcome, ask who to send it to, then text from their phone with a joke intro. Now a friend sees the proof, you, and a personal vouch at once. Needs rapport or don't try it. He used to write "you should meet Alex, the coolest guy" so they'd laugh.

### Handwritten event invite with a PS (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

Quarterly member appreciation night, cheap is fine, potluck works. Handwritten thanks and invite, then a PS: photograph this and text it to a friend, they eat and drink free, three-way text me first. Every guest hears loyal customers praise you while you work the room like the mayor. Don't sell: just set appointments, soon, since interest fades in days, and work those as normal leads with reminders.

### Spouse request and the campaign-wide bring-a-friend line (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

Anything two people can do together: ask if the spouse supports this, cite a stat on results (people who join with a spouse do X times better), and strongly push without saying mandatory, playfully excepting only hospital-grade reasons. Also a friend's million-plus customer challenges (his figures): adding "bring a friend, you'll do better together" to every ad, post and message put 25-30% of signups in word of mouth, from almost none before.

### Gift card play: sizing details (vid: qpQvdBFW_yI — "7 Ways To Get Customers for Free")

Extends the gift card play. Face value equals your cheapest full unit ($200 on a $1,000 ladder; about $2,000 for an SEO agency), the thing you'd give away free anyway; 90% off or more. Any reason works: holiday, birthday, business anniversary. 30-day expiry forces the visit, and you text the named friend. Then credit it against a pricier package, so $200 on $2,000 is 10% off. Rough math at 200 customers: 30 buy two is 60 paid leads.

### Referral partners when the margin is too tight for outbound (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

Roofing and windows caller at about $8K per customer: pay about $1,000 to crews of adjacent home services (landscaping, carpet cleaning) who see the roof, and he says they will beg, borrow and steal. Lender caller on $7K loans with $1,500-$2,000 gross profit, too thin for outbound: spend outreach on recruiting partners instead, such as credit repair firms. His illustration: $10K to land one who sends 20 customers a month is about $40K monthly. Convert their leads better than anyone and you can outpay rivals.

### The card is the ticket: pair events with handwritten cards (vid: sPkMHh8zTMI — "7 Ways to Get People to Buy More Times")

Run a themed member event about every six weeks, which he found matches a good card cadence. Card's PS: photograph this and text it to a friend in a three-way text with us; that photo gets them in. The customer gets credit for the invite, you get a warm lead and an offer at the door, and sales more than paid for events. Trap: value gives people a reason to leave, so always announce the next event before this one ends.
