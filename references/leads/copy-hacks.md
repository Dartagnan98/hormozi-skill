# 12 Internal Advertising / Copy Hacks
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads + 602 video transcripts)

## The 12 Hacks

### 1. Headlines Come First
- 80 cents of your ad dollar is in the headline. Curiosity is king, different is ideal.
- A banger headline can 3-5x a control.
- Test headlines from organic content and competitors. Steal from different industries.
- Formula: "How to [good thing] without [bad thing] even if [biggest fear]"

### 2. Say What Only You Can Say
- Proof beats promise. Competitors can copy your offer but not your proof. Document everything.

### 3. Always Call Out Who You're Looking For (and Who You're NOT)
- "Only for people trying to lose 30+ lbs." Polarize. You advertise to ONE person. Specificity creates compelling copy.
- **Levels of awareness**: Unaware -> Problem Aware -> Solution Aware -> Product Aware -> Most Aware (know you and your product)

### 4. Always Have a Reason Why
- The word "because" is one of the most persuasive words. Any reason > no reason.
- Fraternity party planner: any excuse works.

### 5. Damaging Admissions
- Admit flaws upfront. "Statement A, BUT statement B" -- people believe what comes after "but."
- "Our gym has terrible parking and the AC is on and off, BUT our sessions are more fun than everyone else's." Now they believe you.
- Eminem strategy: own all your flaws so opponents have nothing left.
- "What you sacrifice in promise, you gain in trust. Trust trumps promise."

### 6. Show Don't Tell
- Don't say "get more sales." Describe: "You're running out of order forms because customers are reading credit cards to you over the phone too fast."
- Describe the MOMENTS of the outcome.

### 7. Tie Benefits to Status
- Not just "fast and easy cooking" but "so fast your friends will wonder how you found time to be fit AND cook for your family."
- Think: Who gives them status? Family, friends, colleagues, rivals? Past, present, future.

### 8. Use Urgency and Scarcity
- Scarcity = quantity (only 100 spots). Urgency = time (ends midnight).
- Make it LEGITIMATE. Say no when sold out so next time they buy first.

### 9. Implied Authority
- "As seen on...", "Best [X] in [City]", "We've helped 5,000 gyms over 7 years."
- Longevity itself is authority. Awards matter even if obscure.

### 10. Always Have a PS Statement
- 2nd most-read part of any written copy (after headline). PPS = 3rd most-read.
- Use for: joke/reward, CTA, recap of main points, or "who this is NOT for" disclaimer.

### 11. Clear CTA
- Tell them exactly what to do next and what will happen. "Click the button. On the next page, fill your info. Select a time."
- Make promises, keep promises = gain influence (Martha Stewart effect).
- Hormozi named his company acquisition.com so the name IS the CTA.

### 12. 3rd Grade Reading Level
- Most copy doesn't convert because people pause to understand.
- Fancy = friction, simple = sales. Short sentences, small words, big promises.
- Fewer adverbs, better verbs. Run through a free reading level tool.

### Bonus #13: Use Humor Whenever Possible
- Entertainment goes broad, education goes deep.
- Old Spice = highest-converting ad of all time.
- Test humor like comedians test material.

## Data-Driven Marketing (Slicing Statistics for Campaigns)

### The Three Wheels
1. **What percentage of clients** achieve the outcome (top %, bottom %, median, average, "1 in X", fractions)
2. **What is the outcome** (revenue increase, absolute $, percentage increase, relative increase like "pays off 9 months of rent")
3. **Over what time duration** (first 30 days, first year, every X days/weeks)

### Ways to Express the Same Stat
- Percentage: "87% success rate"
- Fraction: "1 in 5.5 gyms is a million-dollar gym"
- Inverted: "4.5 out of 5.5 gyms hit X"
- Frequency: "Every 7.7 days we create another seven-figure gym"
- Absolute: "Average gym adds $15,500 in first 2 weeks"
- Relative: "Average gym pays off 9 months of rent in first 14 days"

### Why This Works
- Data has absolute conviction (it's just true)
- Most competitors don't track their own data
- You can base ENTIRE campaigns around a single compelling statistic
- Mix and match the three wheels to find the most compelling angle

## Landing Page Optimization

### The One Process
- Every week: one split test. Force prioritization to the single highest-impact change.
- 52 tests/year = massive compounding improvements.

### What to Test (Priority Order)
1. **Headline** (above the fold -- biggest impact)
2. **Image/visual** under the headline
3. **Sub-headline**
- 90% of all tests should be headline or image. That's it.
- Everything above the fold is where you're selling them on staying. Biggest returns happen there.

### Process
- Monday marketing meeting: What was last week's test? What won? Did we beat the control? If not, try another test.
- Google "statistical significance calculator" to validate results.

## More Better New Framework

### More (Do More of What Already Works)
- Ask: "What's stopping us from doing more?" Answer is often: nothing.
- If doing 100 cold calls/day, why not 1,000?
- If spending $1K/day on ads, why not $10K?
- For businesses under $1M profit: just do MORE first. Not enough volume for percentage improvements to matter.

### Better (Improve What You're Doing)
- The only way to get better: TESTING.
- **Test one thing per week per platform.** Four reasons:
  1. Multiple simultaneous tests = you never know what worked
  2. Split tests affect each other (confounding variables)
  3. Constraining to one forces you to prioritize the highest-impact test
  4. Must run long enough for statistical significance
- 52 improvements per year from weekly testing.
- **Prioritize front to back**: Headlines, first 5 seconds, CTRs (front of funnel) have the biggest percentage improvement potential.
- The constraint = the step with the largest return for the smallest improvement.

### Example of Better
- 30% opt-in -> 5% apply -> 50% schedule
- Adding 5% to each: 35% opt-in (16% lift), 10% apply (100% lift!), 55% schedule (10% lift)
- Attack the 5% apply rate first -- doubling it would 2x throughput
- Is it easier to go from 5% to 20% apply rate than to get 4x more traffic? Probably yes.

### New (Expand to New Places/Methods)
- Only after exhausting More and Better.
- Order of "new" from easiest to hardest:
  1. **New placement** on same platform (Stories if already doing Feed)
  2. **New platform** with similar media type (short video on TikTok -> short video on Reels)
  3. **New platform** with different media type (YouTube videos -> Twitter text)
  4. **New Core Four method** entirely (content -> cold outreach team)
- Change as little as possible. Half steps, not pendulum swings.
- For businesses over $1M: flip to Better -> More -> New (improvements have big impact at scale).

### Sell like they've never heard of you (vid: 8VqSFBMMS4M — "Scale or Fail: Tina, hate selling")

Pitch to cold and it converts cold and warm. Pitch to warm and only warm buys. Most owners running ads to people who already know them mistake that for scale.

### Imposter syndrome is shame for lying (vid: ZTSI3DDP_4A — "Social strategy 2027, The Algorithm")

An outbound seller for agencies felt like a fraud because his own agency failed. He was pretending. The honest story sells better: I was bad at running an agency but great at getting clients, so I do that for good agencies. State the facts, tell the truth, including where you look bad.

### Pattern break earns attention (vid: SCi464zfAUM — "X-factor, Dolly Parton")

People judge a trucker-hat guy's truck, coffee, and politics, and will never stop, since pattern recognition is how we think. Say he's a vegan hacker and violinist with a Tesla, and now you want to know him. How much you break earns attention; whether they like the break makes it positive.

### Put trust next to the button (vid: xRxVpjeHmeA — "Scale or Fail: Paul, adventure park")

On a page with a scary question (will my kid get hurt), cut competing banners, put reviews and an obvious safety stat by the CTA, add a day-one money-back, and add video. The VSL was the best thing on Paul's page and became his best ad.

### Write what you want them to do, then cut everything else (vid: X39rITUMEPA — "First Principles, Content, Brand")

Communication exists to change behavior. To get clear: write what should happen in the real world because of your words, say that, and delete the rant it was hidden in. If a sentence can't turn into something done with hands or body, it goes. That's how Leads and Offers got cut to a fraction of their drafts.
The prospect has four screens open and no time. Say what to do, what they'll get, and why it's worth it. Clear beats clever. If you drift into jargon, zoom out with an example.

### The Why People Buy document (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

Feed recorded sales calls into AI and ask for the recurring problems people state before buying. Put only those pain points into the VSL, then build the sales script from the same list. Hormozi says his team did this and it doubled acquisition.
The script is also how a non-expert sells: founders close on authority, so a question-based framework lets a rep without the expertise match or beat the founder's rate.

### One or two seasonal pains cover the whole year: pre, during, post (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

A seasonal pain sells before it, during it, and after you missed it. Gym cycle: start now for New Year, keep your resolution, you missed it, start now for summer, you look bad this summer, back to New Year. HVAC version: allergy season then mildew smell when heat kicks on. Give the marketing lead one theme for two months across email, ads and PPC.

### Collect the stat that turns a cost into a payback (vid: OQf2Ba-Lp_4 — "$2.5M Business for a Stranger")

Ask existing customers for 12 months of utility bills before and after the job. A real saving reframes a four-figure spend as something that pays itself back, and supports a home-value angle. Hormozi rated this Corey's most compelling angle. Send only the exceptional cases, by name.

### Editing pass: drop from 12th grade to 3rd (extends 3rd grade reading level) (vid: -TOYJHax5x8 — "$0 Bestseller writing process")

Goal is readers spend effort on the concept, not on translating words. Hormozi's tool: Hemingway app, because the instant colour change is the feedback that trains you. Rules: if a paragraph means the same without a sentence, delete it; replace adverb plus verb with the right verb (walked quickly becomes ran); simple tenses. Trouble making it simple means you do not understand it yet.

### Webinar opening and booking frenzy (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Find the drop in attendance data and fix the opening first; in another portfolio company rescripting the first five minutes of a three-hour presentation lifted closes 20%. Open with proof, promise, plan. If live, prove it: date, time, weather, current events. Then state who it's not for, and real limited slots ("typically book out within X" only if true over half the time). Ask for "booked" in chat, call out names, and announce times as sold out, chunked by morning or afternoon so small inventory still sells out visibly.

### You get zero credit for what you don't say (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Benefits, scarcity and urgency only count as perceived. An unannounced deadline isn't a deadline; unmentioned capacity limits aren't scarcity; an unmentioned live webinar performs like a recording while you still pay the cost of going live.

### Dean Graziosi reshot a set for two sentences (vid: QQGHCG8d1So — "Emailed myself my failures")

Graziosi's infomercial with Larry King looked perfect and bombed. He compared it with his winners, decided the first two sentences of the hour-long segment were the problem, rebuilt the whole set at his own cost, re-recorded only those lines, and it took off. Hormozi's read: the frame set at the start changes how everything after it is received, so hooks and headlines deserve far more of the time budget.

### Proof strength checklist: how hard is it to fake (vid: fSbqaTlWaYI — "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes")

Runs from a faceless anonymous quote to someone who looks just like you on stage telling your exact story. Grade each proof: in person over virtual, live over recorded, like me over unlike me, the result I want over one nearby. He says about 80% of his ads are proof and he stopped appearing in Gym Launch ads, since owners want owners like them. Extends proof beats promise: promises are all alike, so your edge is the mechanism and how believable the proof makes it.

### The marketing law: state the facts, tell the truth (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

His team's big rule, used in ads and content alike. Under it: say what no one else can say, show what no one else can show. Every brand has something only it can show and most never show it. Starting small in gyms against older names, he just put his numbers up: here's the model everyone teaches, here's mine, mine makes more. Copy fix: "six neat tricks" from a five-follower profile is noise; "six ways I helped this person" can only come from you.
