# The Conviction Framework
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## Core Principle
"Conviction will correct your tone."

If you believe in what you sell, you will say it the right way. You don't need all the sales skills. You'll do it right because you genuinely want to help the person.

## The Belief Continuum
Belief is NOT binary (believe / don't believe). It's a continuum: "To what extent do you believe?"

- Would you bet $1,000 on this product?
- Would you bet $10,000?
- Would you sell your mother this product?

## The Sales Equation
**Closing Percentage = Skill of Sales (1-10) x Conviction (1-100)**

Conviction counts DOUBLE because:
1. It increases closing percentage
2. It increases work ethic (follow-up speed, persistence, number of attempts)

**Total Deals = (Skill x Conviction) x Work Ethic**

A salesperson with skill level 2 and conviction level 100 will outperform a salesperson with skill level 8 and conviction level 10.

## Proof: Religion
"Every religious movement sells something incredibly hard to sell -- a completely new worldview. They don't train missionaries in the skill of sales. They get them to believe wholeheartedly."

## The Mortgage Leads Story (Conviction in Action)
Alex consulted for a mortgage leads company. Salesman John couldn't sell leads. Instead of training scripts, Alex said:

"If you actually believed the leads were good, you'd say: 'Dude, these leads are unbelievable. I'm studying for my real estate exam right now so I can get in on these leads. My aunt is a realtor and she has more business than she can handle. I'm sending her traffic. I'm trying to get my brother to do it with me. I'm not sure how long I'm going to work here because these leads are killing it.'"

## Why Salespeople Burn Out
"They burn out not because they can't handle 'no' but because they can't handle how they feel about themselves."

If you don't believe in the product, you develop cognitive dissonance: you believe you're a good person but you need to make money, and those two things are at odds.

## Hot Streaks
"When you are hot, those are the videos you should watch. That's the game tape footage you should go over and over again. When you're hot, you say things differently. You pause at different points."

"Great salesmen never get cold. They are always on a hot streak because they know how to recreate success."

## Two Mental Hacks for Conviction
1. "I will never have a perfect product, but I can have perfect intentions."
2. "The prospect will be better served working with me than with anyone else." -- You may not be perfect, but you're the best shot they have.

## Conviction-Building Tactics for Teams
1. **Re-read testimonials out loud daily** in front of your sales team. Fresh ones if possible. "They get beat up every day with nos. They need to remember why they're still doing it."
2. **Fix everything you can about the product.** Never blame a customer for lack of success.
3. **Never stop improving the product** so you always know what's up to date.
4. **Bring head of customer support to sales meeting:** "Erica, tell these guys what you do for our customers." Sales team hears the delivery process and gets re-convicted.
5. **Watch completion/success footage on days you close:** Close rates spike when the team watches success stories from finishing cohorts.

## The "Caring Most" Framework

### Core Principle
"The person who cares the most about the buyer's interests is the one who wins the sale."

A sale is always made -- either they sell you on why they can't, or you sell them on why they should. The winner is whoever cares more.

### Commission Breath
"People can smell whether you're in it for your agenda or their agenda."

### The Gym Owner Story
Went 0 for 25 with the same script. Then realized he was selling for HIS agenda, not theirs. Switched to genuinely caring about the prospect's outcome. Went 5 for 9 immediately. Same script.

### How to Apply
"I care more about your best interest than you do. You're going to throw things in my way, but I care about you enough to get us through them."

"The moment you're able to get someone to be real with you in the sale, you've already won."

### Why "Unskilled" Salespeople Close
"Some of our gyms who honestly aren't the most skilled salespeople, but they kill it. They're genuinely kind-hearted, caring people that you can tell from their vibe really love helping people. Those people get away with so much lack of skill because the people believe they are acting in their best interest."

### Delivery and pitches stay separate (vid: 8VqSFBMMS4M — "Scale or Fail: Tina, hate selling")

A marathon that mixes teaching and selling does both badly. Asking parents to wait hours to hear how you can help does them a disservice, and half leave anyway. Teaching is content; pitches are pitches. Afraid of feeling salesy? Everything you bought last month was sold well by somebody.

### Guilt about making money usually means you're misrepresenting something (vid: Uj5XKwW0Vbs — "End of life discussion")

Money is an IOU for a surplus you created, in a voluntary exchange. If it feels off, look for the deception: promising too hard, or implying experience you lack to borrow authority.
Fix it in reverse: work out the most compelling narrative, make that narrative true, then tell it.

### Every outreach attempt is a free scratch-off (vid: 3Cw_BR6utgc — "Advice for Hard Days")

Having nothing makes you a dangerous competitor, since people who have something stop taking gambles. Each call, door, or cold email is a ticket handed to you at no cost: lose and you're where you started; hit one and your life can change.
People want the yes without paying for it. Accept the price of a hundred nos and yeses become available on demand.

### Testimonial ladder for rep conviction (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

Repetitive selling starts to feel like hunting, and that's when it goes cold. Rebuild the belief from best to weakest: former clients join a team call and describe what changed; watch testimonial videos in the morning huddle; read them aloud. If reading, the rep who sold that client reads it, which doubles the effect on them.

### "Here's my card, bill me" as the brand test (vid: 9unucIBuNio — "10x Your Pricing")

When he launched the early beta of his software on a webinar, gym owners who missed it called in saying they had no idea what he was selling but wanted to be charged so they could get in line. Only a long record of over-delivering creates that, and at that point direct response tricks stop mattering.

### Stack calls on results day (vid: AGCtZmgJ1JA — "How I learned to SELL")

Conviction is a dial, not yes or no: the question is how far the rep believes. Drills are the basics, but what lifts a team is hearing last week's buyers talk about their changed lives. At his in-person gyms he packed sales appointments into weigh-out day, while finishers cried in the lobby, and says they closed nearly everyone. Easier to arrange real belief than to fake the tone.

### Help vs manipulation: the full-information test (vid: 6ySRKgXBcO0 — "Day In The Life Ep 002")

Both change behavior, and intent is the only difference. Test: if the prospect knew everything you know, would they still choose to buy? If not, you are deceiving them. Most buyers already want to believe you; your job is giving their logical brain a way to justify it.

### The half-measure pitch: his biggest flop on stage (vid: Tu6YDG0AZ5k — "Billion Dollar Decision")

Extends keeping teaching and pitching apart. During Gym Launch he spoke at a personal trainer conference, gave over 3 hours of value, then rushed his roughly 90-minute pitch into about 15 minutes and barely said the price, assuming the goodwill would carry it. Heavy pushback from the crowd and maybe two sales. A soft toss is not a middle ground: either give value or sell, and don't blend them.

### Weekly CS and sales meeting, and the hot rep's slump (vid: bHnVItVGJPA — "$100M Salesman Persuasion Hack")

Rep starts hot, then fades: skills don't vanish, so training won't fix it. They usually heard something bad from someone they sold, and prospects pour in doubt daily. System: both teams meet weekly, CS tells client wins long-form, and sales learns what buyers misunderstand and should stop saying. Best case, a client tells the team their story live.

### What conviction sounds like: the Bitcoin time traveler (vid: WrCt0R3FBFs — "1.2M follower audience in 6 months")

Imagine going back 10 years in a stranger's body to convince your old self to buy Bitcoin and sell on a set date. You wouldn't let them shrug it off. Have reps sell like that. A rep who was hot and goes cold knows how to sell, so skip the tactics and raise belief; a real prior client beats a pep talk.
