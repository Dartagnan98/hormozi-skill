# Negotiation Tactics
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## 5 Brutally Effective Negotiation Tactics

### Tactic 1: BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)
From Harvard Business School via Chiron Cervata.

"Negotiators who know their alternatives set higher aspirations, make more aggressive first offers, and negotiate better outcomes." (London Business School study)

- Before sitting down: secure other offers/alternatives
- "You only want to sell when you don't want to sell. You want to buy when you don't want to buy."
- Employee: get another job offer before negotiating
- Vendor: get multiple bids
- Deals: have multiple interested parties

### Tactic 2: Anchoring
First number set in a negotiation becomes the anchor. Everyone adjusts from it.

"I'm a big advocate of getting the gasp. Put the big number out there."

**Pro tip -- Anchor the increments too:**
- If house is listed at $20M, offer $15M
- They counter at $17M (moved $3M toward you)
- You counter at $15.25M (moved $250K)
- This signals: "I'm not moving much." They learn to move in small increments too.

**Post-deal comfort:** "You're probably wondering if I would have done it for less. I wouldn't have sold it for a penny less." (Classy move that puts buyer at ease)

### Tactic 3: MESO (Multiple Equivalent Simultaneous Offers)
Present 2-3 offers with different price/term structures that all work for you.

Embeds reciprocity: "I'm being reasonable. Which works best for you?"

Teases out the other party's priorities without them having to reveal them directly.

**Counter-MESO (from Chiron):** "I like the best part of this one and the best part of this one. Let's do option D."

### Tactic 4: Reciprocity in Concessions
Break your offer into as many variables as possible so you can trade more times.

Variables to play with (Value Equation):
- Price
- Speed (faster/slower delivery)
- Risk (who bears more risk)
- Ease (how convenient)
- Financing terms
- Inclusions/exclusions

"Have 80 different things you can change about a deal so you have arrows in your quiver."

Trade small concessions that matter to them but not to you. Keep the reciprocity going while barely moving on price.

**The Front Desk Story:** Contractor nicked a corner. Partner asks: "How much would it cost to replace?" Contractor: "$1,500." Partner: "How about you just discount us $1,500?" Get them to price their own mistake, then flip it.

### Tactic 5: Framing
Position your offer as an investment, not a cost. Position their offer as overhead, not value.

"For a $5,000 investment, you can see XYZ return" vs "This is going to cost you $5,000."

Pool example: "Your house is worth $1M. Houses selling at $1.2M all have pools. Pool costs $100K. You're not spending $100K -- you're adding $200K in value and getting to enjoy the pool."

### Commodity price matching without bleeding margin (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

Commercial refrigeration caller, tickets up to $100K: refused to match last year and had fewer sales at margins about 10 points higher; matched this year and margins fell about 10 points.
Prescriptions: match only where the economics hold, and tell the team exactly when. Ask for the rival quote to compare scope. If matching, match year one and reset to market in year two, or attach a multi-year warranty that only starts in year two, since a delayed charge stings less.
To avoid matching, price in the competitor's later cost using industry breakdown and repair stats (save a dollar now, lose four later). Buyers compare the one number they understand, so show the ones they don't.
Busy GCs won't take a call: send a 1-2 minute screen recording of the comparison, or turn the PDF into a linked interactive chart.

### Nobody sells a company to sell a company (vid: 7IPgbO7-O2M — "Birthday stream on priorities")

Son in a $30M family aerospace manufacturer, one parent wants to sell. Morally, he says, it's their call. Tactically, selling is painful and long; they want something they think the sale delivers. Ask what they want, then offer another path: buy them out, bring a lender, an ESOP, or give them their time back.
Find the outcome behind any stated position. Most people reject a price the other side never named, paying it in their heads from their own wallet of time, money, or embarrassment.

### Simplest law of negotiation: most options wins (vid: -Pz0NpcILOY — "Inverse thinking on pay")

Leverage is built before the meeting: other customers, other offers, other jobs. Same reason a public company CEO can get a huge bonus: the pool able to do that job is tiny. Asking for more while 20 people would do it for less gets you nowhere.

### Hurricane charter: quote shopping under a deadline (vid: 6ySRKgXBcO0 — "Day In The Life Ep 002")

Before Hurricane Maria hit Puerto Rico, with flights gone, the first charter quote was $70K. He called other operators and played each new number off the last: 50, 40, then $28K, leaving in two hours. Even at the last minute, more calls beat accepting the first price. His wife had read $70K as "we go", he as "we stay", so agree the decision rule first.

### "Can you do it for less?" "I can do it for more." (vid: m5ordaa7NN4 — "This Idea Will Make Your Business Unstoppable")

Asked to consult for a day, he said $50K. The buyer offered $35K. He answered $75K. Buyer: $50K is fine. His claim is that this overcome has worked every time he used it.
