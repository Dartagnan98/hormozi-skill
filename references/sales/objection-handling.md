# Objection Handling
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## The 3A Reframing Framework

The skill that separates the best from the rest. Used across $100 to $1M+ deals, in person, phone, Zoom, stage.

### Why Reframing Matters
"The most productive salespeople ask for the sale more times than anyone else."

Problem: asking too many times the wrong way makes prospects hate you.

Solution: Reframe between each ask so rapport is maintained, allowing unlimited asks.

### The 3 A's:

#### A1 -- Acknowledge
Say what they said back to them.
- Shows you're actively listening
- Buys you time to think
- Example: "So you're curious about what our certifications are..."

#### A2 -- Associate
Connect their question/objection to the behavior of successful customers.

Three association strategies:
1. **Recency association:** "It's so funny you ask that -- I had someone just earlier today who asked the same question. Can I share what I shared with them?" (Now you can be brutal to the "other person" without insulting the prospect)
2. **Successful customer association:** "That actually makes you just like Sarah who had a similar business and crushed it with us."
3. **Appeal to authority:** "That's so funny you ask that -- Alex just told me this thing earlier today. Can I share it with you?" (Messenger, not the authority)

The association gives the prospect a label to live up to. Later in the close, bring back that label: "You said you were a family man... because you're a family man, this makes sense."

#### A3 -- Ask
Ask your next question after reframing. Redirect back to the sale.

### Five Rules for Using Reframing

**Rule 1: A prospect believes almost nothing you say and almost everything they say.** Goal is to get THEM to say "I'm a good fit" through leading questions.

NEVER ask "Do you have any questions?" -- This is literally asking them to have objections and take control.

**Rule 2: No one can disagree with a question.** "Be like smoke." You can't catch smoke. Always sidestep with questions. Never disagree with a prospect. "You never win a sale by winning an argument."

**Rule 3: Tell them what their question means.** When they take a step away from buying, associate their question with successful behavior: "90% of our best clients asked that same question."

**Rule 4: Use Straw Men for tough truths.** Three types:
1. "I had someone earlier today who asked the same question..." (attack the straw man, not the prospect)
2. Reference a successful past customer who was similar
3. Appeal to the CEO's authority: "Alex told me this earlier..."

**Rule 5: Retain childlike curiosity.** Physical posture affects tone. Say "Huh" -- it buys 2-3 seconds and resets your frame. "Seek to understand, not to win."

Sticky note above camera: **"Keep the human #1"**

### Universal Response
"What are you afraid of?" / "What are you most afraid of having happen?"

Cuts to the heart of the issue fastest.

- First objection: "What's your main concern?" / "What are the variables you're using to make the decision?"
- Deeper in the sale: "What are you afraid of, man?"
- If they need more time: "What would make this a no?"

## The Three Core Objections

### Price Objection
Core belief to instill in sales team: "If we were giving away Ferraris for $5K, do you think they'd find the money? Yes. So they don't understand the value."

It is ALWAYS the salesperson's fault if someone can't afford the program. This reframe gets the team to take ownership.

### Decision Maker Objection
Three-step process: Circumvent -> Past Agreements -> Ask Forgiveness

**Step 1 - Circumvent:** "What if they say no?" (1 out of 3 times they say "I'd probably do it anyway" -- close immediately)

**Step 2 - If they say they wouldn't do it:** "Well, what do you think their biggest objection would be?" Now they're using the absent partner as a foil and you can attack the actual obstacle.

**Step 3 - Past Agreements:** "Does your partner know that you're struggling with [problem]? Do they approve of that? No? Then why would they be against something that remedies a problem they already don't approve of?"

**Step 4 - Ask Forgiveness:** "Sometimes it's better to ask for forgiveness than permission."

**Step 5 - Delayed Close:** "Let's get your card down. We'll delay the payment to Friday. If between now and then your partner says 'Baby, I want you to keep struggling and never make money,' then call me back and I'll tear up the contract."

### Stall Objection ("I need to think about it")
People are afraid of making mistakes. They don't like making decisions because it's easier to let life decide for them.

**Response:** "Totally. What's your main concern? Let's think about it together."

Walk them through a decision-making framework:
1. "Do you feel like what we're doing can meet your needs and solve the problem? Yes or no?"
2. "Do you want to work with us as a company? Do you like our values?"
3. "Do you have access to funds or know someone who does?"

"Those are the only three things you need to make this decision. Do you like the product, do you like us, do you have the money?"

**Fear-based close:** "What are you most afraid of happening? You're afraid I'm just going to take your money and you get nothing? Let me tell you how that's not going to happen."

## ADDITIONAL DETAILS (Deep Pass)

### "What Are You Most Afraid Of?" Escalation
- Universal escalation for deep objections: main concern -> variables -> what would make this a no -> what would it take to say yes
- Walked through sales floor, heard weak close attempt, grabbed phone: "Dude, what are you afraid of?"
- Gets to the heart of the issue faster than any other question

### 3A Framework Usage Examples (Expanded)
- "I need to think about it" -> "Totally understand, what are the main things you're considering?"
- "I need to talk to my spouse" -> "Totally understand, what parts do you think they wouldn't like?"
- "Timing is off" -> "Totally understand. Our biggest success stories are people who felt the same way. What would make it a good time?"

### Labels Created During Association Can Be Reused at Close
- "You said you're a family man... family men buy this because..."
- Pro tip: bring back the label they accepted in A2 during the final close

### The Mechanic/Surgeon Analogy
- For handling technical questions: "You're asking me what's wrong with your car and it would be unethical for me to answer without looking under the hood first."
- Prevents premature diagnosis that loses the sale

### Three Beliefs to Break in Every Sale
1. **Circumstances**: "I don't have money/time" -> teach resourcefulness/prioritization
2. **Other People**: "Spouse/kids won't let me" -> reframe how it helps them
3. **Self**: "I can't do it / what if I fail?" -> confront decision, show it's not rushed

### Stop answering DMs one by one (vid: 8VqSFBMMS4M — "Scale or Fail: Tina, hate selling")

Sort your DM history into question buckets, rank by share (top one maybe 40%, next 25%, then the long tail), and answer them in order in the pitch FAQ. No new questions exist. Keep going as long as people stay. That's how hours of one-on-one sales go away.

### Never price match; beat on value (vid: zzleYxkf39k — "Scale or Fail: Naz, moving")

Say that if they find another shop with these things included, you'll match it. They won't. Make them see it's apples to oranges, and that a cheaper bid is fair because it includes less. Customers go to price when you give them nothing else to compare.

### Money and hesitation reframes that were used to close Hormozi (vid: 6_CCutkM11g — "$100M Money Models Live Launch")

Lines he says moved him as a buyer, usable on a stalling prospect:
Three-question decision: Does this move you toward your goal? Does it get you there faster than going alone? Can you access the money? Three yeses make it logical.
Time or money: I won't push you to spend what you lack. Slow growth means you are already paying for this lesson in time, and only money comes back.
Already spent: over the next year that budget goes somewhere anyway, on flopped ads, wasted leads, unclosed deals, weak hires. The only choice is where it lands.
Burned before: a past bad buy that blocks a good one burns you twice. You hesitate because you can price the loss but not the gain.

### You're more expensive: reframe local to global (vid: TLgSkSQUql4 — "Answering your sales questions live")

Having to overcome price after you present it means the motion is the problem, not the script. Frame earlier, ideally with a VSL before the call.
The reframe: value is more than sticker price. Speed, rework, delays and purchase risk all count. If all providers cost the same, which would you pick? Us, and that is exactly why we are not the same price.
Locally, today or on this part of the project, a cheaper vendor saves money. Globally, across the whole project or home, it costs more in rework, delays and opportunity cost. Measured over the longer period, your return is better.

### Entrenched buyers: sell the outcome, carry the risk (vid: TLgSkSQUql4 — "Answering your sales questions live")

Drone spraying sold to farmers, a total market of only 300 people, who resist change. Likely mistake: pitching how you do it. Nobody cares about the drones, same as nobody cares that an editor uses AI. They care about the result.
Technology is one of the rare advances that can be better, faster and cheaper together, so win on several: speed, targeting, price.
Still stuck? Do it free or at hard cost and eat the loss if they hate it. Take the risk onto yourself so it comes off them, and put it in front of every farmer you can.

### Same objection, several phrasings, one answer (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

People only hear an objection stated the way they say it to themselves. So voice the stall 4-5 ways (lot going on, super busy, let me see how current projects work out) and overcome them all with one answer. Pull the wording from recorded calls.
Tried it before: the only thing worse than one bad buy is to "let the bad investment burn you twice" by skipping a good one.
Not sure about the cost: you pay in time or in money, and only money comes back. Can't spare it: it's already leaving on flopped ads and missed sales, so the choice is where it goes.
Hesitation: you can count what you might lose but not what you might gain.
Three-question close from his first mentor: Does this move you closer to your goal? Better odds than going alone? Do you have the money? Three yeses make it logical.

### Let me think about it: qualify, future-pace pain, then own the information (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Diagnose first: not qualified on budget, authority, need, timing means let them go. If qualified, it is an education gap, so add pre-call material.
In the call, sales locally raises one outcome's importance, carrots and sticks. Most reps future-pace the dream, few future-pace the pain: if nothing changes, what does a year from now look like?
At the moment: treat it as a smoke screen, not a reason to hang up. Thinking takes information, and you are the one on the call with all of it, so what else do they need to know? Backing off quickly reads as low conviction.

### In-store sizing, then buying online: de-commoditize the unit (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Retailer whose shoppers get fitted then order online. You cannot beat the incentive on a commodity, so make this piece a one of one: manufacturing varies, sizes are not created equal, and the one on your body that fits may not match what ships. If it fits the way you like, take this one.
Layla's addition: stock the full size run. If the size is missing or ships in a week versus 48 hours online, they will buy online anyway, and sellers often skip XL.

### Beating big consulting brands: take off the brand wrapper (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

Finance consultant loses to firms charging three times more. Hormozi's read: they don't believe you, and your low price may be part of why, so raise it first.
Then show what they really buy: not the famous name but a partner at 5% of his time, a lead at 50%, and two analysts fresh from a 12-week Excel boot camp. Versus you: my cell phone, the equivalent of their top people, good enough to go out alone.
Expect the counter: 80 years of track record, hundreds of customers in your market, against some guy in a basement. Every market position has an angle, and whoever sells theirs better wins.

### Spouse objection by curiosity, plus slow breathing (vid: AGCtZmgJ1JA — "How I learned to SELL")

Extends childlike curiosity. "My husband won't approve": ask why not, whether he wants what's best for her, whether he knows she's struggling, whether he'd be happier if the struggle continued. Then ask if she'd be opposed to moving forward today. No fight anywhere in that. Like a sparring novice hyperventilating, reps speed up when adrenaline hits; the skill is slowing down enough to sound interested.

### "I left my wallet": ask for the ID, then trade (vid: Ul87yrDKZ78 — "I left my wallet at home")

Nobody actually leaves home with no way to pay, so set the scene before it comes up. At the close ask if they have their ID on them. It lives next to the cards, so the wallet, purse or phone case comes out and you can see the payment. Take the ID (or a photo on Zoom), note the details, joke about where they're from. Then hold it out: "Let me trade you for the card you want to use." In person, wag the ID toward the wallet. An ID is far easier to ask for than a card, and handing it back builds trust. He puts this at about 80% of cases handled.

### "I don't have the card I want to use": the fallback ladder (vid: Ul87yrDKZ78 — "I left my wallet at home")

Once cards are visible, that is the only line left. In order: 1) Phone out, banking app, statements, any month: the full account number is there, take it as ACH. Set ACH up with your processor if you can't, since it opens another way to sell. 2) Apple Wallet. 3) Put a card they don't want to use on file ("that's policy"), delay the charge 24 hours, both set phone alarms, and you call when they're home to swap. Deal is closed on that card and the contract. If the real issue is money, ask what day they get paid and date the charge. If it reads as not wanting the program, drop the payment talk, ask what they came in for, and resell the outcome.

### Asking permission when you need support (vid: WrCt0R3FBFs — "1.2M follower audience in 6 months")

For the "my spouse" layer. Run the clock two years out: who gets blamed for the life you have, you or them? That's unfair to the relationship, so own the decision and ask for their support. Ask too: would you stop them if the roles were reversed? And people find money for the IRS, the landlord or a broken car; only an investment in themselves gets refused.

### Kill zombies far away: decision maker check before the appointment (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Internal name for handling objections early: a zombie is easier to shoot at a distance than on top of you. Script addition: before the appointment ask if anyone else is needed for the decision; if yes, move the slot so they attend.

### "This feels fast" loop (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Ask what makes it feel fast, then how long they have wanted this problem solved. A long answer means the decision was made ages ago and today is only acting on it. Then ask again.

### Best case, worst case (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

He says this closed more sales for him than any other close. Ask what they fear, then say let's play it out. Worst case they land back where they have already been and survived; best case their life changes. Lays the bet out and takes the emotion off it. His story: a woman budgeting cash in envelopes feared losing grocery money; said with a smile, not affording food on a weight loss plan was a win either way, then he asked what she had once bought without knowing she could afford it.

### Layers of the onion: "I need to think" is progress (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

Extends the three objection types. Prospects usually start outside (time, money, fit), move to other people, then themselves. Reaching "I need to think about it" means you got past two layers and now hold the person who decides. On asking "what would you do if the spouse says no", he reports about one in three say they would do it anyway. For seasonal time objections: start when busy, because that's when you get the most support and it lasts past the busy period.
