# The CLOSER Framework
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## C.L.O.S.E.R. (Core Sales Script Structure)

### C -- Clarify Why They Are There
Questions to ask:
- "What made you come in today?"
- "What made you reach out?"
- "What's your goal right now?"
- "Why is that important to you?"
- "Why that number specifically? What does it resonate for you?"
- "What would it look like 12 months from now if this ideal outcome were achieved?"

If prospect says "I just wanted to find out more information":
- "Cool, but why? What did you want the information to solve?"
- "I'm sure you don't just go around collecting information all day. There's something you're trying to get out of this. What's the problem?"

Key rule: You MUST hit this milestone. If you don't know why they're there, do NOT move forward.

### L -- Label Them with a Problem
- "So just so I'm hearing you right, it sounds like [their goal] is where you want to be, correct?"
- "So what I'm hearing is you've done X, Y, and Z, and the missing link has been X. Is that right?"

They must acknowledge they have a problem. They must say it and own it. "I can't cure cancer until you admit that you have it."

### O -- Overview Past Pain (The Pain Cycle)
Four-step cycle, repeated until exhausted:
1. "What have you tried so far to accomplish this?"
2. "How long did you do it for?"
3. "How long ago?"
4. "How did that work for you?"

Then: "What else have you tried?" -- Repeat the cycle.

Continue until: "That's it. I've tried everything under the sun."

Then explain how it's not their fault: "You were so close. You were six inches away. You had this one piece missing, which is what we provide."

Transition: "Do you want to hear about how the program works? I think we might be able to solve that for you."

### S -- Sell the Vacation (The Pitch)

**Critical rule: Pitch must be UNDER 3 MINUTES. Tops.**

The pitch has exactly 3 pillars. Always 3. The human brain can't take more than 3.

For each pillar:
1. State the pillar (5 seconds)
2. Get micro-commitment: "Can you do that? / Does that make sense?"
3. If hesitation: tell a memorized 30-second anecdotal story

**"Sell the vacation, not the plane flight."**

What NOT to sell (the plane flight): modules, meal plans, macros, workouts, support team, URLs, domains, funnels, targeting, ads, periodization, calorie counting, software features.

What TO sell (Maui / the destination): the result, the experience, the transformation.

**Maui Analogy for tiered pricing:**
- Swim to Maui = DIY, sharks, could drown (lowest tier)
- Boat to Maui = slower, no wifi, but safer (mid tier)
- Normal flight to Maui = faster, still smells bad, security (standard tier)
- Private jet to Maui = direct, champagne, suitcase packed on arrival (premium tier)

"Everyone gets to Maui. Variables are the speed and quality of the journey. Likelihood of arrival is assumed."

**Example stories:**

Accountability story: "When you were a kid, did your parents tell you to brush your teeth? You hated it. But you did it because they told you to. Now you're an adult -- you brush your teeth on your own. That's external accountability turning into an internal habit. That's what we do here."

Motivation story: "Do you have a favorite TV show? Game of Thrones? Do you feel like you need to get motivated to watch it? 'Oh man, I wanted to watch it but I just couldn't sit on the couch.' Of course not. You look forward to it. We're going to make [your program] something you look forward to. When you look forward to it, you don't need motivation."

### E -- Explain Away Their Concerns (Objection Handling)

Only 3 real objections exist:
1. **Price** (value discrepancy)
2. **Decision Maker** ("I need to talk to my partner/spouse")
3. **Stall** ("I need to think about it")

(See objection-handling.md for full breakdown of each)

### R -- Reinforce the Decision
Added later to the original framework. Critical for reducing churn, refunds, chargebacks.

Actions within first 48 hours:
- Personalized video from the CEO/founder by name: "Hey Johnny, saw you just signed up. Super pumped to have you."
- Handwritten card
- Welcome gift/swag
- Introduction to their point of contact: "You're going to be meeting with Heather tomorrow."

"People make their decision about whether they believe in you as a company within the first 48 hours after the sale."

"The sale continues throughout the entire customer relationship. You close them at signup, you close them again at onboarding, you close them again at their first check-in."

## The Three Things on a Sales Call

Only three types of statements should ever come out of your mouth:

1. **Questions** -- leading questions that guide prospect to the conclusion they should work with you
2. **Restatements** -- "So what I'm hearing is [X]. Does that sound about right?"
3. **Anecdotal Stories** -- 30-second stories used to break beliefs

NO declarative statements of fact. NO teaching. NO technobabble. NO paragraphs about the product.

"The difference between teaching and coaching: teaching provides tactics (only 10% of people benefit). Coaching breaks beliefs (100% of people benefit)."

"Everyone gets benefits from breaking beliefs. Only 10% of people get benefits from tactics." (attributed to a friend)

## Discovery / The Pull Teeth Technique

### When Prospects Give One-Word Answers
Alternate between two phrases:
- "Can you give me an example of that?"
- "Can you be more specific?"

Bounce back and forth until you get something meaty:
- "Business isn't growing" -> "Can you give me an example?" -> "This year is the same as last year" -> "Can you be specific about what's different?" -> "I have the same amount of leads as last year"

### Chunking Up
Once you get specific information, chunk up to what you sell:
- "So it sounds like you have a marketing issue. Is that fair to say?"
- "So it sounds like you have a pricing and packaging problem."

Then: "So if we solved this marketing and pricing issue, do you think it would help grow the business?"

They say yes. They've already accepted the sale before the pitch.

### Transition to Pitch
"Now that you've agreed [what I'm about to sell] would help, can I tell you about our thing? I think it might help given what you just told me."

Match each problem to a specific solution/expert.

### Rule: Don't Pitch Until They're Ready
"You do not pitch the program until you believe the person will say yes."

"Lawyers do not ask questions they don't already know the answers to. No guy proposes without knowing she's going to say yes."

If you don't feel ready: stay in the question/restate cycle. Don't move to the pitch.

"If someone says 'tell me how the program works' and you're not ready: 'No, I don't think we're ready for that. I need to understand more about your business otherwise it's not going to make sense.'"

### The Mechanic Analogy (When Pressed to Answer Questions Too Early)
"You're asking me what's wrong with your car and it would be unethical for me to answer without looking under the hood first."

"If anyone gives you an answer on the first call without already having looked under the hood... run the other way. It means they're just telling you whatever they can to get you to buy."

## SELLING WITH LOGIC FRAMEWORK (Deep Pass -- Batches 3-4)

### Core Principle
- Charlie Munger: "We have a high moral responsibility to be rational"
- Emotional buyers buy fast, cancel fast. Logical foundation makes decisions stick.
- "When the emotions fade, the logic will continue"

### Three Pillars Rule
- Any solution can only have 3 pillars (brain can't handle more)
- Fitness example: exercise, nutrition, accountability
- Leads example: timely, qualified, exclusive
- Use stories, not jargon to explain each pillar
- Tooth brushing analogy for accountability: "Your parents made you brush your teeth. You hated it. Now you do it on your own."

### Pre-Call Tactics
- Voice memo, video, personalized reach out BEFORE the call
- "What size shirt/boxing gloves do you want?" -- tiny thing, huge effect on show rates
- Follow up before AND after appointments

### Referral Hack at End of Every Call
- Ask for referrals at end of every call (not just closes)
- 1 in 3-4 people refer when asked
- Referral close rate: 80-90%
- Cuts CAC in half

## CLOSER FRAMEWORK EXPANDED (Batch 5-6)

### Detailed CLOSER with Three Decision Points
- **C** = Clarify why they're there: "What made you come in? What's your goal? Why is that important?"
- **L** = Label them with a problem: "So what I'm hearing is you're struggling with X, correct?"
- **O** = Overview past paid attempts: "What have you tried? How long? How'd it work?" (Pain cycle, exhaust all options)
- **S** = Sell the vacation (not the plane flight): 3 pillars max, use anecdotal stories, never jargon. Pitch under 3 minutes.
- **E** = Explain away concerns: Price (past agreements), Decision maker (forgiveness > permission), Stalls ("What's your main concern?")
- **R** = Reinforce decision: Personalized video, handwritten card, gift. "First 48 hours after the sale determines everything."

**Only 3 things come out of your mouth:** Questions, Restatements, Anecdotal stories.

**Three decision points:** Does this meet your needs? Do you want to work with us? Do you have access to funds?

### Pitch what the investor wants (vid: eq1bnI0JoXU — "8 entrepreneurs compete for $100K")

Both finalists talked about themselves and missed the first rule: start with what the prospect wants. For an investor that's fast growth, high profit, defensibility. Entrepreneurs never stop selling: investors, employees, customers, ads. Tell the truth, too: the bigger miss for one finalist was mentioning a night out before pitching.

### Sell better, not harder (vid: TLgSkSQUql4 — "Answering your sales questions live")

Someone afraid of being pushy dislikes bad salesmanship, not selling. You happily bought plenty last month from people who planned for you to buy.
Pushy feelings usually mean underqualified or un-preframed prospects. Most sales close at the ask when they are qualified, preframed and the script ran properly, so a lost close usually traces to the intro and discovery.
After an objection, agree and ask a follow-up. Add to their logic; nobody buys from the person who proved them wrong.
"It is what happens when sales go wrong." That is his line on objection handling: a last resort, not sales.

### Enterprise sales is the SMB call repeated up the chain (vid: 0RjIw9M1j28 — "Walk Away From $1.4M Business?")

The conversation is not very different. You sell person A to reach B with the same pitch, B to reach C, until you reach whoever controls the budget. A long sales cycle really means more people to sell on the same thing.
So outbound until someone bites, make that person your internal champion, and expect to run the same call around 10 times. The jargon about gatekeepers and stakeholders is mostly smoke and mirrors.

### Pitch is a statement plus an analogy; everything else is questions (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Reps who come off aggressive are usually talking too much. People believe what they say themselves, and no one can disagree with a question.
Keep statements to three uses: praising a conclusion they reached, answering logistics (do I have mediums, am I free that day), and the pitch itself.
Even there, one line on the feature then an analogy for it. Describing all you will do sounds like either your work, which they do not care about, or their work, which they do not want. Mention your thing only as far as it builds belief you can solve their problem.

### Phone CLOSER for a commodity service: close every door, then give three needs (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

Swim school owner taking inbound calls. Overview: ask what they would do instead, then test each answer (pool access? three times a week?) until they agree there is no reasonable alternative.
Then name three things every winner has: right environment, right expertise, right process. Each one kills an option they just raised (home, off-duty lifeguard, winging it).
Anchor on time: this should have begun two months back, so you are already behind. Ask how soon this week, then which card.
When they start reading the number, stop them, text a payment link, and stay on the line until it clears.

### Ping-pong discovery, then demo only the one thing they care about (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

After what brought you in and how long, alternate two prompts: tell me more, and give me an example. Vague business isn't growing becomes lead quality, big houses versus small, in a few volleys. They are also commands, which quietly position you as the authority. Describing their pain in vivid detail makes them believe you can fix it, so discovery should run far longer than the pitch.
"Most buyers are single issue voters." Ads must list five reasons to catch a crowd; one-on-one you find the one. Tesla buyer with kids: skip range and the environment, demo getting kids in and out, ask if that solves it. A demo exists to prove the outcome is easy, never to tour features.

### Boil the whole call to one stated decision (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

In portfolio sales reviews the gap was no core decision articulated. Work out the smallest unit of the choice before the call and hand it to the prospect, so they never have to figure out what they're deciding. B2B version: will you make more on your own than with us, past what we cost? The call is then why are you here, what's the problem, what have you tried, here's how we'd help, so can we help more than you could alone, yes or no.

### Open by qualifying them for something (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Swap small talk and "got a pen?" for: name, company, relevant background, tie to the person they saw on the webinar, then ask what resonated most. Then: the real purpose here is seeing if you qualify for our guarantee, and not everyone does, based on three factors (budget, authority, need, timing in disguise). A bonus or start date works in place of a guarantee. They lean in and try to earn it.

### Pitch shape follows delivery model (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

DIY or done-with-you: make it dead simple, three pillars, so they believe they can do it. Done-for-you: show every step, 11 or 27 of them, so they believe it works and don't want to do it. The stuck company ran a feature stack on a do-it-yourself product, which overwhelmed people and then told them they'd be the ones doing it. Before pitching, recap fit and ask where they want to go from here, so they ask for it.

### Baton pass congruence (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Ad, webinar headline, first lines of webinar, sales call, onboarding should each echo the last. Simple to grasp, hard to run since several parties must stay aligned, and he says it pays heavily.

### Reps getting cute in discovery (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

The common slide: a rep ramps fast, then numbers sag as they play consultant, chase detail and answer prospects' questions. Whoever answers has lost control. Stay high level on the few things tied to biggest ROI.

### The diagnostic sale: feels custom, delivers the same product (vid: 1UhvBSQFy6A — "10x Revenue with 1 New Sales Process")

Move a transactional pitch toward a custom one without changing delivery. Steps: pre-sale questionnaire that asks about the problem several ways (how long, cost, five years from now) to arm the rep and raise awareness; set up the account profile including the card before anything is sold; current state; desired state; obstacle, in their words; present the path and tie the price to the goal; incentive to prepay. Kill objections before price, since after price you are a salesperson, not an expert.

### Diagnostic sale for fitness: price the gap, not the package (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

Extends the diagnostic sale. A caller offered 3, 6 and 12 month packages. Hormozi's fix: drop the menu. Take the gap (200 lb to 160 lb is 40 lb), apply a safe rate (a pound a week), and the length and price follow. Now you sell the goal. Add a promise to keep working with them until they hit it if they do their part, and his gym experience was that buyers then handed over the card.

### Enterprise selling: BAMF and hours, not months (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

Book a meeting from a meeting: "waiting to hear back" with no next date counts as a lost deal. Reframe the timeline: a 12-month deal is really hours of work, so 48 one-hour meetings can stretch over two years or finish in three months. Pitch each stakeholder on getting promoted with no risk to their job. Gated white papers still work: take the name from anywhere in the org, ask what the download is for and who else is involved, and pull the thread.

### Hire the account (vid: aBPWCdaJJqA — "I Helped 6 Business Owners")

A friend selling to the Fortune 100 hires people from the exact departments he targets, per Hormozi. They bring know-how and sway, land the account, and most revenue comes from cross-selling that client's other regions.

### "Sell me this pen" is a trap for product pitching (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Extends educate on the prospect, not the product. Pocket the pen and ask how it's going and what brought them in. You have to travel from where they are to wanting a pen, and that road has nothing to do with the pen.

### Prep rule: 10% for repeats, the reverse for rare (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

Something you do constantly gets about 10% of its length in prep: six minutes for a 60-minute call. Something rare flips: six hours for a one-hour quarterly presentation. New players win on prep because they have no reputation; he studied gym owners' photos and came to calls with a floor layout fix. Rereading past notes at the start proves your advice fits them, which answers "will it work for me" before it's asked.

### Commands break the autopilot opener (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

"How's it going" gets an automatic reply. "Tell me about your day" makes them actually present. Mix commands with questions so the script doesn't sound like interrogation.

### Selling up the org: drop the story, make data a condition (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

The more the buyer earns, the less they care for narrative and the more they want the math on their return. Caller's student-retention firm lost about 20% a year (his figure) because staff loved the training but never gave data to prove it worked. Fix: make data sharing a condition of working with you, not a request, and sell it as how the product tailors itself over time, plus what you'll do to make it easy. Whether it's training or a tool, the buyer only wants renewals to stay up; product components are your weeds.
