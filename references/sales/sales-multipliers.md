# Sales Multipliers
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## Sell 7 Days Per Week
- 52 weeks x 2 extra days = 104 additional selling days/year = 29% increase
- Off-hours (5pm onwards) and weekends are when most consumers are available
- Skipping weekends causes Monday stack-up: high show rate expectations but lower actual shows due to 48+ hour gap

## Speed to Lead
- Call within 5 minutes of lead coming in
- Double dial every lead (call twice back-to-back)
- Morning-of nurture: remind prospects of appointments booked days ago

## Follow-Up Framework
- Work leads same day, next day
- If ghosted at any point: send a meme
- Don't skip follow-up days -- consistency compounds

## Show Rate Optimization
- Book appointments no more than 24-48 hours out when possible
- Morning-of text/call reminders
- Reduce gap between booking and appointment

## Payment Step-Downs

### Order of Presentation
1. **Prepay** (not "paid in full" -- that's a salesman term, not a customer benefit). Say "prepayment discount"
2. **Half down + payments** (in-house financing or credit card)
3. **Third-party financing** (every industry has financing solutions)
4. **Pay-as-you-go** (lowest barrier)

### Price Anchoring Trick
Always present the highest price first. Then step down. Each step down feels like a concession.

"You get to be the good guy for getting them a lower price rather than the bad guy for them not being able to afford it."

## The STAR System (Getting People to Do What You Want)

Five reasons someone doesn't do what you asked:

1. **S -- Set (Did they know?)** Did they know you wanted them to do it? Solution: Write it down. "If it isn't written, it never happened."

2. **T -- Tell (Did they know WHAT?)** Did they know exactly what you wanted? Define in terms of behaviors or outcomes, not vague labels. Don't say "be less creepy" -- specify the exact behavior to change.

3. **A -- Train (Did they know HOW?)** Do they know how to do it? Everything is trainable. Break it down: which specific sub-step don't they know? Document -> Demonstrate -> Duplicate.

4. **R -- Required (Did they know WHEN?)** Did they have a deadline? Default to "end of day" -- an end-of-day organization moves 7x faster than end-of-week, 30x faster than end-of-month. Always ask: "How long will the actual work take?" then "What are you doing between now and then?"

5. **S(tuff) -- Something blocking them?** Are there legitimate obstacles? Go through 1-4 first to eliminate excuses. If nothing else, it's a motivation issue.

**The elegance:** By asking 1-4 first, you give multiple opportunities for it NOT to be a personal attack. Then if you reach 5 (motivation), both parties know it.

## Communication & Speaking Frameworks

### Only Three Things Out of Your Mouth
1. Questions
2. Restatements
3. Short anecdotal stories (30 seconds max)

### The Person Who Speaks Most Loses
"The person who speaks the most in the sale loses."
"Sales is in your ears, not from your mouth."

### Closers Ask Hard Questions
"Questions that challenge their current paradigm. If they're on the phone to prove nothing will work: 'It sounds like there's no way for you to succeed. Does that sound about right?' Then they sell YOU on why you're wrong -- and by doing so, take the contrarian stance that proves there IS a way."

### State Shifts
If you can tell within 5-10 minutes you're not going to close, you need a state shift. Ask challenging questions to shift the frame.

### Advanced Salesmen = Never Skip Basics
"Advanced salesmen are advanced because they never don't do the basics. People are defined as advanced because of their outcomes, not because of their tactics. Advanced lifters aren't doing different exercises. They just lift more weight consistently."

### One VSL per conversation (vid: mRlSb0O5QNU — "Scale or Fail: Joey, event planning")

Each sales touch gets a VSL, built on its own objections: pre-call one, post-event before rebooking, and one for the decision maker not on the call. Add a CTA like text me your headcount to check it was watched and raise show rates. Calls shrink from two to one, and you close instead of yapping.

### In-person VSL has no downside (vid: uaLNfijnp-8 — "Scale or Fail: Hussle, dentistry")

Two jobs before a sale: qualify and educate. Educating costs you people in most funnels, but not when they're already in your office. Hand them an iPad during the wait. More sold, fewer questions, shorter talks, more prepayment.

### Fix show rate by phoning the no-shows, then selling the appointment (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

Ketamine clinic, $2M revenue, $27K/mo on ads, a VA just schedules, roughly 40% show. Hormozi's prescription: treat it as a one-time project and personally reach the last 100-200 no-shows until 3-4 reasons surface. Reasons differ by avatar (his gym example: overweight women too embarrassed to walk in).
Lever order: a live conversation first, then a VSL. A stranger with no stakes is easy to cancel on, so the slot must be sold, not merely scheduled. He thinks that alone could flip 40/60 to 60/40.
Integrity tie-down on the booking call: ask what could stop them attending, name the usual reasons, get them to commit past each one.
Co-host's add: rep rings 24 hours early, apologises for the wrong day, confirms tomorrow and mentions the video. Doubles as a confirmation touch without pressure. He says use it ethically.

### Four levels of no-show friction (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Practice with 13 no-shows, about 25% of slots. Owner feared deposits would scare off a small town. Choose a level:
1. No card: after agreeing the time, get a verbal commitment. Are you someone who keeps commitments? A missed slot costs us real money.
2. Card on file, not charged, with a $200 fee inside 24 hours, framed as respect for the doctor's time and demand for slots.
3. Charge for the consult, credited toward anything they buy.
4. Charge for the consult regardless.
Hotels hold a card, so patients already accept it. He calls it very unlikely this lowers income.

### Adding qualification almost never makes less money (vid: TLpLmhWqKEU — "Key man risk and caller breakdowns")

Wholesaling lead and sales service: $5,000 CAC, about $35,000 LTV, close rate super low. Hormozi can barely recall a time qualification friction lost him money. Cost per call goes up; CAC may fall and LTV rises, and reps stop being beaten down by nos.
A vague willing-to-invest question only tests for a pulse and a credit card. Build real qualifiers from your best customers: longest stay and most spend, then their demographics, quantifiables, geography, and what they consumed before buying.
Weave that avatar into ad call-outs, landing page dropdowns and VSL hooks. The thank-you page routes best leads to sales; the rest get a self-liquidating automated offer. He did this on a free launch opt-in by asking business revenue.

### Qualification vs education: the two dials of any sales motion (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

Diagnosis: right people but they feel cold means more education. Wrong people but they feel hot means more qualification. Selling expensive things to consumers is roughly 80/20 qualification to education.
Pack education where it costs least. Before opt-in is expensive; after is nearly free. Home services sequence: video on the page, another on the thank-you page, speed to lead, confirm they watched before the appointment, tour on site, then hand them a 7-8 minute proof video while you go work up the quote.
Never email quotes. Present in person and close on the spot. If you can't, you under-educated or the decision makers weren't there.

### Proof when wins sit behind NDAs: anonymize, then pull the result forward (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Marketer to aerospace suppliers and machine shops can't name clients. Use Acme Corp: keep the before and after and the specifics, change the name, and say so. That also unlocks more testimonials from shy customers.
Then use present proof. Play a prospect a real inbound call your ads generated, and ask if they could handle 10-15 of those a week. It approximates life as a customer before they pay.
The question is how many post-purchase moments you can move in front of the sale. The perfect form is a free trial: don't trust me, I'll do it, pay after. He wouldn't drop a good client over no case study unless a discount was traded for it.

### Consult funnel for a custom product (vid: HGZOxBfnF-E — "$1M Business for a Stranger")

Hormozi's flow: sticky "custom designs" banner, form, calendar, post-opt-in VSL with hook, proof, promise, plan and price ranges, then texts confirming budget, spouse on the call, and timeline. Say plainly they can buy on the call and no second one is needed, with about $200 off for deciding there since it saves admin. Let them dream with 3 or 4 wild ideas, then ask card or financing. His guess: 50% to 60% close.

### Master sheet of best content, mapped to sales questions (vid: MD5-HByRxoA — "My Content Marketing Strategy REVEALED")

For an owner whose customers come from ads, affiliates or outbound, organic content is nurture: prospects check your profile, consume a few pieces and confirm you are alive and competent. Keep one sheet of your top-converting pieces plus the FAQs from sales calls. Reps send the matching piece before a call or between call one and two. Those FAQs also tell marketing what to make next.

### Find them the money and more will buy (vid: QQGHCG8d1So — "Emailed myself my failures")

Financing is underrated: hand people a way to pay and they make it work. His example: most of the education industry exists on the back of easy government student lending. Practical rule: industries with easier lending sell more people at higher prices, so weigh that when picking a market.

### Pull up every appointment: the rep who sold 4x (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

A solar rep outsold the second best by about four times. Whenever an appointment booked, he called at once to qualify. Time now? He just ran the close. No time? He offered an opening later that day. Either way talk time rises and the later slot reopens for someone else. Same-day appointments show and close better. Reverse move: if today's prospect has not confirmed, push them to tomorrow and fill the slot with someone responsive.

### Availability beat every other variable (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

At Allen (4,000+ appointments a day), analysts checked click-to-close data including text timing and length; total open time slots was the strongest driver of sales. With few slots people still book but at a bad time, so shows drop, or they show rushed and don't buy. A prospect with a free Sunday afternoon is in a buying mood. Example: his wife called nail salons until one took her same day; the two that said no lost her.

### The kill list (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

Top reps keep their few whales in the CRM and also somewhere they see all day, so those deals get daily extra attention and never go quiet.

### Funny GIF for churned members too (vid: kloJJeiysxg — "4 Steps to Unf*** Your Business")

Extends sending a meme when ghosted. Members on the call report a funny GIF in a DM with a "leaving so soon" vibe gets churned people to come back. Hormozi's team uses a Kevin Hart meme on ghosted leads and says it gets higher response rates than any written message.

### Prep is the pitch (vid: m5ordaa7NN4 — "This Idea Will Make Your Business Unstoppable")

Five minutes on the profile gets you the city, the team they follow, the spouse's name, the VP on the call; use them in the first 60 seconds and they know you prepared. He woke at 5 for four hours of review before that $50K consulting day, arrived with four or five pages of fixes, and the buyer said no one had ever come that prepared. Vendors with 10 to 20 hours of visible prep barely have to sell him. Even starting a call five minutes late to review notes beats starting on time unprepared. Build the prep file so surprises have a place: two extra stakeholders, turn to page two.
