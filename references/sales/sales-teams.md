# Building & Scaling Sales Teams
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## The 6 C's for Scaling Sales Teams

### C1 -- Closer Sequence
Question-based framework following the CLOSER acronym. Script should be ONLY questions. No paragraphs, no statements.

"If you get lost, you can find your way back again. Just ask the next question."

### C2 -- Consistent Daily Training
**This is the #1 thing besides conviction.**

Schedule: 60 minutes/day, 5 days/week.

Split into two skill sets:
- **Talking (25 min):** Read the script out loud with correct tonality. If they mess up, start again.
- **Obstacle Drilling (5 min):** Flash cards -- "I need to think about it." "I need to talk to my partner." "This is expensive." Draw the objection, deliver the overcome.
- **Listening (30 min):** Listen to a recorded call. Three questions:
  1. What went right?
  2. What went wrong?
  3. What are you going to do next time?

Then drill the specific improvement and move on.

### C3 -- Call Recordings
Record every call. Tools: Gong (for Zoom -- "the f***ing best").

Gong capabilities: talk time ratios, question counts, engagement tracking, AI analysis.

"How are you going to study game film if you don't record? Every NBA team plays the game and watches the footage."

### C4 -- Communication Cycles & Feedback
- **Daily huddles:** Share testimonials, pump up, kiss on forehead good night
- **Weekly one-on-ones** (new reps) or bi-weekly (experienced)
- **Monthly reviews**

One-on-ones: Have them tag 3 calls (best, worst, average). Review where they clarified pain, asked for sale, overcame objections.

**The Golf Swing Rule:** Only give ONE thing to work on at a time. Don't try to fix 8 things at once.

"If he just said 'take another 20 swings and put one thumb under the other,' I could probably get better."

### C5 -- Cut the Fat
"Cut it fast. If guys can't close in the first week to two weeks, they ain't closing."

"It's much easier to take a 6 and get them to a 9 than to take a 2 and get them to a 5."

"If you have a sales person you've been waiting for to turn the corner for 6 months -- they're not going to turn the corner."

Cut bottom 10% on a regular basis. Persistent 30% performance jump from cutting bottom performers.

### C6 -- Competition & Career Path
**Competition cycles:** 6 weeks works best. Long enough to believe they can accomplish something, short enough to feel urgent.

Structure: 3-person teams (not individual). Adjusted draft (best with worst). Creates competitiveness + camaraderie.

Prize: $1,500/person for a 2-3 day weekend (e.g., Vegas). "Not expensive, but worth every dollar."

Must have a published leaderboard updated daily. Bell ring (in person) or GIF thread (remote) for every sale.

**Career path:** Minor milestones every 50 deals closed. Go from $500/deal to $525 to $550. "Just the fact that something is changing on the outside relieves anxiety."

## Hiring Principles
- Never hire seasoned closers
- Find people and educate them about the PROSPECT, not the product
- "If your sales training is focused on your product, you're doing it wrong"
- All closers under 27 years old (one exception at 40)
- Sink or swim in first 1-2 weeks

## Team Structure
- Separate outbound and inbound
- Setters and closers for BOTH teams (4 roles total)
- Outbound setters -> outbound closers
- Inbound setters -> inbound closers
- Different prospect types = different specialists

## Compensation Benchmarks
- 10-15% of deal value works well as group goal
- Per-deal bonus with career path escalation
- Competitions: $1,500/person for 2-3 day trips

## ADDITIONAL INSIGHTS (Deep Pass)

### Train on Prospect, Not Product
- "When you're training sales people, you don't train them on the product. You train them on the prospect."
- "He might know the product perfectly but he just said the guy's problem was not the problem"
- "The product doesn't matter. If you know a niche really well you'll know their pains."

### Duplicating Sales Skill > Having It
- "It's one level of skill to learn to sell. Another level to teach to sell."
- Teaching is "a more valuable skill"
- "If you can duplicate your skill in someone else, that's how you make 10x, 100x more money"

### Sales Comp: Always Off Cash Collected Today
- Aligns salesman interest with business interest
- Back-end payments are success of delivery team, not sales team

### The Wealth Management Reframe (Used ON Hormozi)
- Was getting pitched private wealth management, wanted out fast
- Said "I already have a wealth advisor"
- Guy: "90% of the clients we have that shift over already have a wealth advisor. So it means you already know how a lot of these things work."
- Hormozi: "What just happened? I thought I was getting out of this and now I'm more like the customers that ultimately buy"
- Applied this reframe to all his selling

### Setters feed the owner while training (vid: mRlSb0O5QNU — "Scale or Fail: Joey, event planning")

Owner closes 40% and reps close far less? Flip reps to setting. Going from one appointment a week to eight quadruples the business, and calendars show problems fast. Reps earn more that way too. Check in first thing and end of day; a weekly audit isn't enough when demand is the constraint.

### Weekly one-on-one order (vid: mRlSb0O5QNU — "Scale or Fail: Joey, event planning")

Personal first: if something big happened, deal with it, since they aren't thinking about quotas. Then feedback if they miss, growth if they're crushing, like shadowing your calls. Then role play, forever. Telling people changes nothing; you need feedback loops where they try it in front of you.

### Sell the rep on the math per door (vid: o64cI6tebnU — "Scale or Fail: Caleb, roofing")

Make earnings straightforward: at a set close ratio, each knock is worth about $10, so how much do you want to make today? As you scale, treat salespeople as your new avatar. High-volume repair work also gives reps more reps, on shorter cycles.

### A sales team is a one-time pull, not market expansion (vid: 6_CCutkM11g — "$100M Money Models Live Launch")

Murphy door e-commerce brand, growing 60% a year, added phone reps working abandoned carts and leads. Average ticket went from about $2,200 to $2,800 on those sales. The owner then asked whether to build a separate contractor sales channel.
Hormozi drew a triangle as the market: a sales team drags your monetization line lower so you convert more of the same people. It raises earnings per click, but keep adding reps and you run out of leads to call.
Cash the gain in as more ad spend, which feeds more reps, then repeat. Only open a new channel if a proven star can run it without you. Do more before doing new: he would rather double a business doing one thing than double it doing four.

### Why the founder closes 80% and the team closes 30% (vid: 6_CCutkM11g — "$100M Money Models Live Launch")

Hormozi's example gap: the founder sells consultatively, and that works because prospects already see the founder as the expert. Perceived expertise hands over status, leverage and influence. A new rep has none of it, so copying the founder's style fails.
Build the script for someone with zero authority. His view of sales has simplified: be clear, speak up, ask several times, and phrase each ask so it does not offend.
His fix is a short set of memorized closes (he counts seven, details kept for his paid playbook) so reps get more asks in without walking a decision tree. The people who ask most sell most, and also offend most, so the training job is balancing those two.

### Pick the best human as sales leader, not the best salesperson (vid: MPHn-nW30QU — "14 Years of Good Advice")

Promote the top producer and you can lose their production as well. If someone is both a great leader and great at the job, you've already promoted them, so the real choice only comes up between the two. When it does, take the best human. Alex says he'd die on this hill, and it applies to any role.

### Top rep stealing clients who also carries your income (vid: R7McLmx73Xw — "30 lives in 30 days, what he learned")

Default answer is fire on the spot, but not if you have no idea how the sales motion works. Then he has leverage over you.
Sequence: record everything the rep does, go on hard recon, sell alongside them, and try to match their numbers. Once you are close and your income is safe, let them go.
Promoting, converting and delivering are the three skills an owner cannot outsource. Accounting, legal and tech can go to someone else.

### Removing the star rep who poisons the floor (vid: TLgSkSQUql4 — "Answering your sales questions live")

Top seller demeaning others after repeated warnings. First isolate what she does that others do not, and teach only those actions, while keeping the team away from her influence. Do not fire blind if it could bankrupt you.
Then run a 30 or 60-day plan: tell trusted reps that if they get to a target level you can make changes they will like, without naming the firing. Recruit people who will follow process meanwhile.
His experience every time: the rest step up, nobody beats the star, the team average rises, overall production does not drop, and process gets easier without exceptions. A huge gap between one rep and the rest usually signals missing process and training.

### The vault model: what a sales team can and can't do (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

Treat each prospect as a vault with about 10 numbers (can they do it, do I like them, is there proof like me). Marketing hits all 10 repeatedly; each person arrives missing a different set. A rep fills the gaps live.
So adding sales gives a one-time conversion lift, maybe a double, on existing leads, by reaching people with fewer numbers solved. Too many missing and a call stops being worth it. It won't 10x you: awareness has to grow next.
Friction rule from an email-only course seller whose sales calls flopped: the missing piece was a VSL between lead magnet and booking. Show price and qualify with BANT there. Hormozi says adding friction has almost always made him more: fewer calls, higher close rate, better rep morale from fewer nos, then room to raise price.

### Keep rep calendars near 70% booked (vid: 2nTkffKVnas — "12 rules of thumb, trades, learning")

Target 70%, keep it between 60% and 85%. Fully booked reps get more total sales but worse lead conversion: appointments push further out and to worse times, so shows fall, and nobody works the pipeline or does outbound. Near 30% booked, morale drops and reps get desperate on every call.
Tell: his sales stayed flat across big and small ad weeks, because reps only squeezed pipeline when calendars were empty. Fix is usually more reps, not a new comp plan. He says he has yet to earn less by adding salespeople, and reps who hit their personal number by mid-month coast.

### Door-to-door reps quit by day three: train on actual doors (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

Windows company, 100% commission, reps vanish in week one. Hormozi: training problem. Stand two or three freestanding doors in the office. Every rep is a real knock; on a mistake, stop, step back, knock again. Move fast between doors to build pace. Include a rude homeowner so slammed doors stop being scary.
Award most knocks, most opens, biggest cuss-out, not only sets. Pitch the job as a lifelong skill, stated honestly. Hire from what past winners share, in demographics, experience and application behavior. Big teams run a ladder: sell, teach one friend for an override, recruit a team.

### Update: he no longer cuts the bottom 10% or runs six-week contests (vid: 7TmfdhOwIzU — "Brutally Honest Advice")

Hormozi reversed earlier advice. The forced 10% cut was a forcing function for removing people who should already have gone. With a strong enough culture, weak reps get weeded out faster by default, so it no longer needs doing on purpose.
Same with six-week incentive competitions: dropped. What remains is a monthly leaderboard with small prizes for first, second and third, which he says is good enough.

### If you can sell, install a product instead of building a sales agency (vid: 0RjIw9M1j28 — "Walk Away From $1.4M Business?")

Advice to a young commission rep: Hormozi has yet to see a sales agency go past maybe a million a month. Big clients bring sales in-house because outsourcing costs too much and sales is core; the small ones are bad at selling and you will want to fix their whole business.
Better: pick an outbound-friendly, low-churn product (insurance, pest control, alarm systems, M&A) and own it, keeping far more of the upside.

### Train stage closers in days: script the whole pitch onto 1,700 slides (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

In-person business training firm: free events of about 100 owners feeding a $1,000 boot camp, and pitchers took forever to train. Fix: standardize the pitch, not the people.
Hormozi's book launch pitch ran 1,700 slides, one sentence max per slide, a new slide every 6 to 10 seconds, visuals matched to the words. Rep only has to learn speed, volume and hands.
Build it from AI over transcripts of the best pitches: words first, visuals second. Drill: rep records the 90 minutes at home, rests, repeats, sends the best take; you review at 2x, then they go live. His claim: he has swapped speakers countless times, and a pitch that works keeps working.

### First-day advice for a new SDR: copy the top rep, double the volume (vid: WeH2E1fGgaw — "Daily Q&A: selling, comp and failure")

What Hormozi told his 15-year-old neighbor who started as an SDR at Gym Launch: sit beside the best person on the floor, listen to everything, do what he does, at twice the volume.
Modeling supplies the skill feedback. Double reps is required because the top rep is already ahead and improving, so matching his effort means never catching him. Work like you are coming from behind, because you are.
Can't land a role? Take a higher-risk one such as door-knocking solar: they will take you, and you learn skills while possibly getting paid.

### Hire reps by group tryout, not resume or early one-on-ones (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

Hormozi puts little weight on resumes, and experienced reps can bring bad habits. His funnel: a 30-60 second video for a vibe check, then send a script to practice, then a group call where each runs the first minute or two and gets live feedback.
That one call shows who prepared, who changes right after coaching (intelligence and ego), and who roots for others versus snipes. One-on-ones come only at the offer stage, so the top of the funnel stays wide cheaply.

### Zero to 80 reps in 90 days (vid: _ooj0V4hpgw — "Hormozi Hotline caller breakdowns")

How one portfolio company staffed a sales team fast: ran all the ads and funnels and hired 15 sales recruiters at once. Standard fee was $10K a head; he negotiated to $5K on promised volume. Hired 80, kept 50, and within 90 days the company did $5M a month in extra sales. If one or two recruiters failed you, that's like working two leads and declaring the channel dead.

### Two interview screens: reply speed and the coachability re-try (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

Treat the applicant as a lead on you: how fast they answer shows work ethic. Then role play with little context, say the sale itself doesn't matter, give one note and run it again. Ego on the note means no. No change on the second attempt means they won't improve on the floor either.

### 14-day trainee ramp to a half schedule (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

Days are mostly watching good recorded calls, roughly 40 total (software shows whether they watched), with script study between and a role play each morning. They earn calls by passing the role play, then go on half a schedule: calls in the first half of the day, reviewing their own in the second, manager meetings at both ends. Full schedule only after hitting the minimum close rate.

### The manager's hour per rep: 30 / 5 / 25 (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

Weekly per rep, daily for trainees. 30 minutes: hear one or two calls at 2x with written notes, then bold the one or two highest-impact fixes. 5 minutes: tell them. 25 minutes: role play the change and drill live objections. Keep a tab per rep with dated fixes, and open next week by checking whether they did it and whether it made deals. Extends the Golf Swing Rule.

### Sales management is a tolerance skill: the standards reset (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

The weakest team belongs to the manager who tolerates the most; a new NFL coach can flip a losing roster in a year for this reason. When his own team got bloated he cut more than half, told the rest they stayed because they were valued and were expected to buy into a world-class standard, and gave them a night to sleep on it and quit with no hard feelings. Spell out the rules (5am drill, on time, clean pipeline, CRM notes) and that even the top closer gets fired for skipping them.

### PIP loop sold at hire (vid: 2lA_A8BGRRs — "How To Build Large Sales Teams")

Two weeks below KPI means back to trainee treatment with more attention. Two more weeks without improvement means out. State this when hiring and pitch it as the price of excellence, so the right people get fired up by it.

### Manage out via the calendar and route best to best (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

Sales seats are resources, so allocate them on results: top closers get top leads and best times, which also limits losses from the weakest. Below KPI: calendar cut in half, then a week or two to prove it or out. Track below close rate (show rate, offer rate, losses by obstacle) to personalize coaching.

### Cash-collected comp favors paid in full (vid: 4GQLJjH9-oA — "This Business Was Stuck")

The company paid closers the same either way. Worked example: five payments of $2K pays $200 at 10% today; pay in full pays $1,000, five times as much, so reps push real decisions and those buyers stick better.

### Do not hand door knockers inbound leads (roofer, about $3M) (vid: 0EqJD2o-Mnk — "Helping 6 Business Owners Scale")

The team stopped knocking once fed leads, and leads were expensive. Hormozi: inbound and outbound are separate. Inbound is the prize you earn after proving you can sell, with fewer people, more sales a day, and a lower commission since you pay for the leads. Reps will trade for steadier income and no sun. New to inbound? Give it all to your one best closer, so offer, lead source and process are the only variables, and put everyone else back on the street.

### Secret shop first, then make the script unskippable (vid: 1UhvBSQFy6A — "10x Revenue with 1 New Sales Process")

Before closing the investment Hormozi's sales director secret shopped against a checklist: questionnaire, card, current and desired. Expect horror: you will be happy if the team remembers half. For low-wage teams, skill is how simple the process is: make point of sale refuse to continue until a checkbox forces the question, and drill until the line comes without thinking.

### Turning around a sales team: script first, then the right trainer (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

Caller's top closer became manager, the #2 quit, and closing fell from 70 to 40 (caller's figures). Hormozi: teams hired for raw ability sell however they like, so training and script edits reach nobody and CS inherits uneven promises. Step 1: everyone says the script word for word, so a rep who follows it and misses a sale did it right. Then a change actually changes how all reps sell. Simplify it; he usually cuts half. Owner takes about five calls to see what can go, and learns enough that no sales director holds leverage over them. Train with game tape, then drill one script section at a time. Hire the caring closer type as trainer, not the killer.

### Boiler room: drill clients' teams too (vid: BYpTRiRqS1Y — "4 Educational Business Owners")

At Gym Launch he sold an add-on that ran his own team's morning drill for clients' trainers and front desk staff, across thousands of gyms. The weekly rotation: intro, discovery, offer, objections and looping, then a hot-topic day. Answer to a financial-adviser lead-gen caller asking if training sales-averse clients can work: yes, and they ban the word "sales" there, calling them consults.

### Train in visible behaviors, not adjectives (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

"Have more charisma" means nothing and is why companies take so long to train presenters. Say raise your voice, talk faster, pull your shoulders back. Describe what a camera could see; others will then call it energy or confidence. It cuts ramp time, and that skill becomes the founder's real edge.

### Sales AI wins on reps, not charm (vid: KYqEK_T_5M4 — "Get RICH in the A.I. Revolution")

His prediction: feed a voice AI millions of sales calls and it gets more volume in practice than any human lifetime, then takes calls around the clock. He thinks buyers may one day be relieved to learn the rep is an AI, because it means they got the best version of the rep.

### Rep with every strike against him: do double the best guy (vid: Luvfw14pVx4 — "You're (Probably) Not Good Enough")

Jacob, his young neighbour, blamed age for failing at wholesale deals; Hormozi told him he simply sucked at sales. He got only an interview at Gym Launch, then was told to copy the top rep and do twice the work, since matching him never catches up. He topped outbound, then the qualifying team. At 18, with better setter stats, the closer manager still made him source and close three deals himself before joining as low man. He became the top rep among older professionals.

### Select salespeople, don't build them: same 10 hours, different return (vid: _33XVdvO4Gc — "The day everything changed")

Ten training hours move a rep from a 2 to 4.5, or from a 6 to a 9. So spend on the pick. His first real killer took a walk-in before any training, knew almost no pitch, and closed her paid in full on rapport. Top sales teachers he names were natural sellers first (he closed thousands before any sales training), so training reminds great reps how good they are. Exception by design: a shop that hires anyone and culls brutally, like 24 Hour Fitness, where the process is the selection.

### One full sales cycle is the killer test (vid: _33XVdvO4Gc — "The day everything changed")

Extends "cut it fast". Scale the deadline to the cycle, whether two weeks or a month. Every killer he has had closed in their first full cycle, then improved with tweaks. "Give me another shot, I just need to learn" drags out the same ending. Hire when you feel you have to have this person, not when they could probably do the job.

### Recruit by what you can pay (vid: _33XVdvO4Gc — "The day everything changed")

Pay well: the best reps already have jobs, so take them from businesses like yours; only the product changes, so ramp is short and you can judge them fast. Can't pay well: run ads, use your network, interview far more people; also ask whether your price point is the real problem.

### You can train yourself for a year, not a hire (vid: _33XVdvO4Gc — "The day everything changed")

A bad-at-sales owner should still grind: all your off hours are free to spend on yourself, and it teaches you to spot good reps later. A business can't justify that on an employee, because finding a good rep costs far less than building a mediocre one. Owner's turning point: the first text saying someone else closed two of four walk-ins, after keeping every sale from fear of wasted leads.

### Reps as accidental support kills conviction (vid: afbP6sB_Atc — "Keep more customers")

Buyers call the person they spoke to longest, so reps field last week's angry customers while pitching today's. That costs calendar and belief. Fix: a dedicated escalations team as triage, so reps can pass anyone over. He saw close rates rise from that move alone.

### Lunch for the rep who sells the low ticket (vid: XwZH-lOKG9c — "Big 4 Customer Acquisition Models")

Reps ignored the downsell for unqualified leads, so each day's low-ticket seller gets lunch bought in their honor. He says it worked better than commissions. Downsells are often near 100% margin for him, so they earn their place even at less money.

### Lead nurture specialist: split dialing from reminding (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Setters who both dial and remind do neither well: no double dials, leads sat for hours, no morning-of reminders. Promote one strong setter to own shows, with a checklist bridging setters and closers. Two items: a three-way text intro of setter, closer and prospect after the set, so a known person vouches for an unknown one; and the closer's personal voice memo or video the morning of.

### Shrink the team by utilization (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

If reps have room for many more calls than they take, you have too many reps. Cut the lowest closers and overall close rate rises on the same leads. Thin calendars also make reps desperate, pressing and not listening; full ones let them sell relaxed and in rhythm. A low performer left on the team demotivates the high ones.

### Two SOPs: on the call and off the call (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

Most companies script the call and say only "work the leads" for the rest of the day. Output tracks opportunities, and opportunities are made between calls. Give a separate off-call checklist: follow up these people, drag this one forward, cancel that one.

### Script creep: reps add a cough (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

A rep coughs before the close, it closes, so the cough stays. Then a second question stays. Prospects who would have bought anyway reward every addition, so calls keep getting longer. Fix: morning role play on one script section a day, and after each correction three clean reps in a row, the way a trainer he watched drilled just the opening 30 seconds. Goal is to "breathe the script": you can't listen while recalling your next line. Shorter calls also mean more calls.

### Dogs, horses, tigers (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

Analogy he credits to a friend. Dogs starve unless fed and petted daily. Horses gallop only while whipped. Tigers kill for sport, even when full. Hire tigers. Tigers treat unqualified leads as paid practice, like Jacob, who took everyone's rejected leads and outgrew the team on reps. Skipping effort on a weak lead is a warm-up set done with bad form: it trains bad habits.

### Best leads to best closers: timeshare proof (vid: cy2k1GdA-9o — "9 Concepts")

New example for routing best to best. A timeshare rep making about $3M a year in commission told him he won an hour with the CEO and asked for the best leads. It worked in his division, then nationally (he says both income and business went 5x). Needs lead scoring first. Side effects: a bigger top earner recruits for you, like a lottery, even at lower entry pay, and new reps train on the leads that cost you least. Complaints about lead quality go one on one to the manager, as data, never on the group call.

### AI setter: compare apples to apples (vid: fr78adfAnuA — "How to Use AI in Your Business in 2026")

Owners install a half-built bot, compare it to a team tuned for six years, and declare the humans won. Fair test: give it the same training and SOPs. His AI SDR now matches his human team. Dropping his setter into your business fails like moving his rep would. Would you have a human call every opt-in with no process? AI speeds up the sales motion; it doesn't excuse you from having one.

### Farm system: AA, AAA, the bigs (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

Treat the sales org as a pro baseball pipeline: dialers are AA, value-call specialists AAA, closers the big leagues, and the training system itself becomes a company asset. Reps who were hung up on for months treat inbound as a gift. He says his inbound close rate went from roughly 20-25% to 60% once closers came up through it. Promote from the qualified pool when added dialers trickle up to a need for another closer.

### Four-rung title ladder paced at eight weeks (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

He believes a rep has six to eight weeks of steam before getting disheartened, and high-churn seats churn because people run out of future. Each tier (dialer, specialist) has junior, associate, full, senior. Advancing takes an objective count paced at about eight weeks of production, for example 50 shows then 100. Each step gives a title and a nearly symbolic raise ($50 to about $55 a show). Fall below the qualifier and you drop, publicly. Senior does not get you promoted, only eligible. It also ends the "I want more money" talks: here's the path.

### Recruit on the lottery ticket, not the seat (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

On-target earnings as he gave them: dialers about $40K, specialists about $55-65K, closers about $150K-225K. You can't attract talent to a $40K seat on $40K; you attract them with a visible path to $200K in nine to twelve months with no degree. Everyone sees the top closer's checks.

### Closer tryout: 10 deals in two weeks (vid: okA9Yt2KZuk — "How I Scaled My Sales Team")

A promoted rep gets about two weeks and 10 at-bats. No close, back to AAA. His evidence after building many teams: plenty start hot and fizzle, none started cold and became killers. Closers at 25% or less get bumped down.

### Three sales directors: hunter, process, people (vid: rhVxX5_8xUw — "Business Owners, We Have a Problem")

First: promoted his best closer, lost the closing and got someone who hated managing. Second: all operations and process, mediocre, asked to step back to closing. Third: organized, understood sales, loved one-on-ones and watching reps improve; performance rose. He hasn't missed since. Close rates and cash flow lost in between are ignorance debt you pay once per role.

### Record the calls when someone is on fire (vid: rhVxX5_8xUw — "Business Owners, We Have a Problem")

One of his two biggest regrets: no recording from about 4,000 in-person closes. Keep a playlist of each closer's hottest calls, captured when they feel they could close anyone. Being on fire is just different pauses, phrasing and tone, so it can be studied like game footage and repeated.
