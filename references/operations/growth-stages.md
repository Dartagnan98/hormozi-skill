# Growth Stages
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## The 4 Stages of Entrepreneurship (Business Growth Stages)

### Stage 1: $0 to $1M/year
- **Formula**: One product, one channel
- Reliable system of acquiring customers -- that's literally it
- People get stuck here because they still haven't nailed repeatable acquisition
- Also get stuck due to personal/interpersonal problems (sleeping with employees/customers, drama)
- Channel = cold email, Facebook ads, Instagram ads, YouTube ads, referral partners, organic/earned media
- "You gotta sell one product and one channel. That's it."

### Stage 2: $1M to $10M/year
- **Formula**: Multiple products (cross-sells + upsells), potentially multiple channels
- NOT starting multiple businesses -- it's creating a value ladder / backend
- "What's the next level of service? What do they naturally want/need after buying our initial product?"
- You're selling the same number of units but increasing LTV (e.g., 4-5x your LTV)
- Higher LTV opens up traffic platforms you couldn't afford before
- Superior backend allows broader acquisition channels

### Stage 3: $10M to $30M/year
- **Formula**: Professionalizing the business
- This is painful -- your "special sauce" starts getting diluted
- Still running on Google Sheets and Trello boards -- need real infrastructure
- Must hire corporate people: HR, legal, accounting, IT systems
- Become a target for lawsuits -- contracts and legal protection matter
- Pendulum risk: going too far toward corporate stifles innovation and creative energy

### Stage 4: $30M to $100M+
- **Formula**: Intrapreneurs running profit centers
- Need more "souls" -- more people with entrepreneurial drive who can take initiative
- Structure: Core operations hub (HR, legal, IT, finance) with profit center spokes
- Each profit center has: Product + Acquisition (marketing/sales)
- **Critical distinction**: Hire intrapreneurs, NOT entrepreneurs
  - Entrepreneurs either lost their juju and complain, OR regain confidence and leave taking business with them
  - Intrapreneurs love driving growth but don't have same appetite for risk
  - They like the "safe place" to experiment while getting a paycheck
- Jeff Bezos calls his directors "CEOs" of sub-businesses within Amazon
- Use existing resources/capital/infrastructure to create similar-but-different product lines
- Each new product line needs a TRUE leader (as smart as you) before launching

## Scale Zero Framework
- From $500M mastermind with 8 entrepreneurs
- "For me to get bigger, I must get smaller within the company"
- Must become less and less important to the company's success
- If you're doing 20 hours/week and you double, you'll be at 40 -- you become the bottleneck
- Goal: Get to literally ZERO requirement of you
- "Only then will I be able to scale, otherwise I'll be the bottleneck"

## Business Growth Simple Math
- $1M/year = $83,333/month = ~$21K/week = ~$3K/day
- Pick your LTV, work backwards to units needed
- At $1K LTV: need 21 units/week = ~5 deals/day
- At 1-in-3 close rate: 15 calls/day showing up
- At 66% show rate: 22 set appointments/day
- **Key insight**: Easier to start with premium ($5K-$15K) than high-volume low-ticket
- Below $1K/unit is brutal operationally -- need high volume, need operations skill
- "The operations behind it -- you have to support so much volume"
- LTV must be high: unique, expensive, sticky, habit-forming

## FOUR CHAPTERS EXPANDED (Deep Pass -- Batch 3)

### Hub-and-Spoke Model for $30M+
- Central ops: HR, legal, IT, finance (keeps everyone out of jail)
- Profit centers spin off the hub, each with Product + Acquisition
- Each run by an intrapreneur who's a "sub-CEO"
- Don't launch new ones without a leader "as smart as you are"

### Your Spirit Gets Diluted at Scale
- At $30M+, your vision/care/drive gets diluted across the organization
- "You need heads that can reinvigorate the business and drive innovations"
- Need multiple "souls" -- not just one founder driving everything
- "We did 28, 37, we've just really been stuck at this level" -- reason is founder spirit dilution

## PERFECT BUSINESS MODEL (Deep Pass -- Batch 4)

### U.E.S.A. Framework
- **U**nique -- can't be comparison-shopped
- **E**xpensive -- premium pricing
- **S**ticky -- habit-forming, high switching cost
- **A**ir (digital/weightless) -- zero marginal cost
- Buffett: "Costs a penny, sells for a buck, habit-forming"
- Margin math: 90% -> 95% margin = **doubling profitability** (costs halved)

## INSURANCE AS ULTIMATE BUSINESS MODEL (Deep Pass -- Batch 4)

- Gets paid far before ever having to do anything
- May never have to fulfill at all
- Reverse of depreciation: collects money that appreciates over time
- Oldest companies (180-300+ years) are insurance companies
- Predate the tax code

### The Money Ladder -- Who Gets Paid When
- Employee: work first, paid 2-4 weeks later
- Self-employed: split (half now, half on delivery)
- Professional (doctor): paid first, then work
- Banks: preferred creditor, paid first if things go wrong
- Insurance: paid the whole time, may never deliver
- God/Franchisors: 10% off the top, forever
- Lesson: restructure your business to be higher on the ladder

### Segment after a year of data (vid: mRlSb0O5QNU — "Scale or Fail: Joey, event planning")

A year in you'll notice something like 25 of 100 events produce 75% of the profit. Retool messaging, VSL examples, and scripts to attract those. Do the same volume, earn four times the money, and get narrow enough to expand from.

### A second location tests whether the success was you or the model (vid: 3Cw_BR6utgc — "Advice for Hard Days")

Gym owner, 2-3 years in, torn between a second site, online training, or an app. Any of them could work; running them together will not, because each swing pulls cash and attention from the other and you bounce back and forth.
Stack knowledge in the vertical. Open somewhere that isn't your cozy town: if incentives, training, and procedures rebuild the five-star experience without you, you have enterprise value. A charismatic owner can make any shop work, which proves nothing. Either way the first step is the same: hand off the first gym.

### Check the largest version of your model before scaling it (vid: bpIW-dfKFoE — "Degrees of change: jackhammer vs sandpaper")

Look up the biggest company your model has ever produced. If an old category has nobody at your target, assume the model resists scale unless you intend to be the innovator. His example: no billion-dollar coaching company exists despite plenty of people wanting one, while insurers, banks and software companies reach that size. Reaching a local peak means taking stock of your skills, money and assets and asking where they'd earn the best return.

### You do not need to go online to leave the building (extends second location test) (vid: 0_Gf5v8DEMY — "Overcome Fear of Failure")

Gym owner wanted online for freedom. Hormozi owns about 70 locations across companies and works in none, so that link is false. Test: can you not go to the gym for a year? Whichever path comes next, that is step one.

### Three margin eaters of mass-market scale (vid: EUW3rMp-Uvg — "Past $30M mistake")

Scaling consumer, biz-opp or mass services guarantees three things: CPMs rise, audiences get colder so conversion drops, and infrastructure costs climb. Bulk-buying savings mostly land in the first two jumps in order size. So margin compresses while revenue grows, until one hiccup makes you a large nonprofit you're personally liable for. The counter is word of mouth, so around $1M to $5M pause what got you there and fix the product: track time to value, NPS, customer health, referrals. Going slower here is the correct sequence.

### Startup debt: choose which kind you take on (vid: 7NqsFh5ej0w — "The first 5 hires")

Every new business runs up debts: life, management and culture, financial, tech. A weak CRM, bad books or inexperienced managers all get repaid with interest later, each able to cost a quarter or a year. He used to dismiss investors as not real entrepreneurship. Now he reads funding as choosing financial debt to carry less of the others. It is also why his later companies grew faster: he can pay up front for better systems and been-there people, and skip stages.

### Quality creates growth, growth creates bloat (vid: F84olnKkseM — "Business Was Hard, 4 Concepts")

Make bigger the goal and you get bigger and worse; make better the goal and bigger follows. A revenue target with no inputs attached leads to stupid builds: a second location when the first is mediocre, clients taken for a number. The root is selling too many too fast, before you can hire and train backfill, so you have bodies and not brains and must sell more to cover them. The fastest route to $100M is often slower to $10M. Backing out means less selling and a profit dip first.

### The direct response doom loop: why SKU portfolios do not sell (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

Caller at about $3M run rate with four SKUs wanted 4-5 brands to exit. Hormozi hears this weekly and has yet to see it work. Media arbitrage runs well to about $10M; then CAC climbs, margin shrinks, supply chain bites, and dupes undercut you on Amazon. You end up selling just to feed the team. Even a 9/10 product gets copied; investors buy brands, not products. Only a brand or an enforceable patent defends you, and legal works better once you are big. Fix: back one product you believe in, recruit influencers who love it, not mercenary affiliates, and let product results close the brand loop.

### You can't be busy and broke: every startup picks a debt (vid: H_ZLs1-jOKQ — "Stop Being Broke. Get Busy.")

Extends the debts idea. Venture-backed firms take financial debt and buy talent at market. Bootstrappers take management debt: you do the job of three or four people until the swamp ends. If you have free time and no money, you have room for the calls you refuse. He booked two to three people for every 30-minute slot to cover shows and did 4,101 closes (his count) across his gyms, which is his only sales training.

### Set goals you can't miss, then reset them (vid: QQGHCG8d1So — "Emailed myself my failures")

Portfolio founders ask to double every 90 days and keep falling short. He calls 10 to 20% growth per quarter unbelievable over a long span. His method: pick a goal you are certain to hit, crush it halfway through the quarter, set the next one. Modest timelines also stop people doing something crazy to hit a number they made up.

### Education curve vs traditional curve: pick by goal and by problem (vid: Rm4zRdLAyjw — "Easiest Million Dollar Business")

Coaching and courses are the fastest route to $1M to $3M a year: no inventory, a team of about 10, little capital. Then most cap out, because they don't know how to lead; at $30M he says he could name those without a brand on two hands. It is income, not an asset: knowledge isn't sticky (once taught, they leave) and you carry three key-man risks: you bring customers, you make the product, the team follows you. Traditional is slower and eats capital, and fewer finish, which is exactly why it becomes sellable for $10M plus. Chains stall at the same point: from around 5 to 25 locations needs a further layer of leadership.

### Self-employed build, owners assemble (vid: EdUAAVKl_io — "Business that runs without me")

Words matter: the self-employed build the business; owners assemble the people who build it. His hedge: growth may cap near where you stop running it, so he doubts the owner at $50K a month who is ready to step out. He says he could be wrong.

### You scale fast up to your prior level of competence (vid: SasEJE4FI-I — "Your Failures Can Make You Rich")

Gym Launch grew quickly partly because of ad arbitrage, partly because he'd already run six gyms, 30 plus staff, eight sales guys and 30 to 100 sales a day. Same activity with more zeros on the price tag, so he could go straight to that level. Past it lies what he calls virgin territory: hiring a CFO, taking on debt responsibly, going from 5 to 50 salespeople. Expect speed only for levels you have already beaten.

### Chain test: operator profit share and the six-month absence (vid: h6y0nYVZgwE — "Not Unbelievably Rich Yet")

He's never seen a brick-and-mortar service chain scale without an operator on profit share, usually 10-20%, on top of being able to earn $75-100K. Tune one unit so it pays them and you. Don't sign a second lease because the first went profitable lately. Test: bored for months, hitting KPIs, and you could leave six months while it still grows.
