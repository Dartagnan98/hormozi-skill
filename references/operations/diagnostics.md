# Diagnostics
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## Multiple Metrics for Success + Constraints

### Beyond Profit
- First metric: profit and net worth increase
- As marginal utility of money decreases, add constraints
- "Can I build a billion dollar portfolio working 2-3 days a week?"
- "Can I do that while being in competitive physical shape?"
- Constraints make goals more interesting without breaking the machine

### Two Phases
1. First learn to sacrifice everything to achieve the goal
2. Then add constraints to make the game harder and improve life in other areas
- "You have to achieve mastery over the thing before you can truly give it away"

## Being Stuck: Beliefs, Traits, Skills Framework

### Why Entrepreneurs Get Stuck
- Three possible deficiencies:
  1. **Belief**: Incorrect belief about what's possible (e.g., "no one in my space does over $2M/month")
  2. **Trait**: Character trait deficiency (patience, persistence, genuine belief in product)
  3. **Skill**: Skill deficiency (don't know how to do something OR don't know how to find someone who can)

### Market Cap Test
- Add up all competitors' revenue -- that's what you could do if you were better than all of them
- If you haven't sold 30%+ of your marketplace, you haven't capped your market
- "You just have capped your skill or your beliefs or your traits"

### Product Belief Check
- Many people subconsciously stop growing because they don't genuinely believe their product is good enough
- They cap out at meeting their own individual needs
- Beyond personal needs, growth requires belief that the product helps others

## Speed / Execution Frameworks

### Being Fast Makes You Win
- Hard decisions are hard because you've already made all the easy ones
- "The only thing guaranteed is that if you never take that step back, you will never get the outsized return"
- Spell out both sides of the trade explicitly

### The Trade-Off Framework
- For any hard decision, explicitly spell out:
  - Option A: What you get + what you give up
  - Option B: What you get + what you give up
- Most people don't confront the trade -- they just stay stuck
- "The only thing guaranteed is that if you want everything from life, you will get nothing"

### Fix supply before demand (vid: uaLNfijnp-8 — "Scale or Fail: Hussle, dentistry")

More leads do nothing when you're supply constrained. Solve the constraint so everything downstream can increase throughput, and marketing moves up only after that. Even with bandwidth, ask whether it's the constraint.

### Inputs, not outputs (vid: xRxVpjeHmeA — "Scale or Fail: Paul, adventure park")

A failing business usually tracks outcomes without inputs. The core arbitrage is LTV versus CAC; everything else expands from it. Near-death triage: know the monthly nut (about $180K here), generate cash first, then run the same play repeatedly once it's returning 10 for every 1.

### Outdoor lighting, $1.8M: the leak is the appointment close, not the leads (vid: A8p2HCI_23E — "Answers Your Questions")

Meta lead ads at $36 per lead, 168 leads in 2 weeks, 50 in-person walkthroughs, 5 closes so far (10% of appointments, 3.3% of leads on the year), $5,500 gross profit per deal. Alex called the lead-to-show numbers fine for Meta and an appointment at about $140 workable. The target is a third of appointments, so the job is a triple at the close.
Fixes: reminder texts ask for two confirmations (video watched, spouse attending), morning-of too. Add a second proof video. When the rep steps out to the truck to price the job, leave an iPad playing it so the couple is pre-framed right before the offer.

### Three questions to open any meeting or diagnosis (vid: X39rITUMEPA — "First Principles, Content, Brand")

1) What problem are we solving? 2) What do you want to have happen? 3) What would it take?
The first two give you context in seconds when you're pulled into a meeting mid-stream (we have a churn issue; we want this). The third assumes the outcome and makes the other person reverse engineer it. Point it at your own constraint too: what would it take to make 200 calls a day? Then arrange the rest of your life around the answer.

### The 9am job once you have delegated yourself out (vid: R7McLmx73Xw — "30 lives in 30 days, what he learned")

Owner replaced in the work, sitting at a desk feeling useless. Test: if 50% more customers showed up tomorrow, could you serve them?
Yes means demand constrained, so your job is now getting customers: scripting and filming ads, posting content, selling. No means supply constrained, so build capacity.
Channel by situation: local goes to Meta or Google PPC, fastest by far. National starts with content for recognition. Very high ticket or enterprise suits outbound, since there are few targets and each is worth a lot.
If you do not know how to do these, learning them is the first task.

### Make the constraint the headline every day (vid: TLgSkSQUql4 — "Answering your sales questions live")

Once a metric is the main constraint, attack it from three or four angles: a short script, the number called out in the daily huddle by name, recordings reviewed and roleplayed, and leaders turned over if their team lags.
If you are saying it once a month, the team hears it is unimportant. Twice a day, they know it matters.
One portfolio company made client exits and stays the daily huddle number and opened an emergency Slack channel for at-risk customers. Churn fell by more than half, largely from simply trying. That effort is where the red yellow green system came from.
Across multiple locations, you hold the leaders and they hold the teams.

### The problem the owner names is often not the constraint (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

Private lender: asked for a better first-call close because deals don't exist yet when leads call. Answer: that's structural to the business, a feature not a bug (Shopify doesn't agonise over churn from people who never get going). No cash crunch means shortening the cash cycle gains nothing. Real levers: more promotion and a re-engagement cadence. If you did want activation, sell paid pre-diligence or just meet in person.
Archery company chasing price and model questions: volatility was a symptom of too little volume. Under $1M a year the fix is almost always more, so 5x the email outbound before changing anything. Having deals behind deals is what lets you hold price.
General-practice law firm at 7% margins: stop chasing top line. Niche down, bill flat instead of hourly, audit attorney hours, make revenue per head the north star. He wants 80% gross margin in services.

### SMB lead gen no man's land and the two ways out (vid: I0nCuqu7Cac — "Hotline: mental toughness and offers")

Hormozi's graveyard: $1M to $5M agencies selling lead gen to small businesses from about $1,500 a month. Revenue comes fast, then churn catches it, CAC climbs and margin compresses. Clients switch you off because their own business swings, however well you perform.
Exit one: very low ticket, $150 to $400 a month, $500 tops, almost free to deliver, a nuisance item nobody cancels (maps ranking, SEO, review upkeep).
Exit two: go up to businesses doing $2M to $3M or more that already know their LTV and have a sales motion, and don't play below $10K a month.

### Fix the funnel step with the most room, not the one you hate (vid: lidFtb1Ga0o — "He Makes $1.5M and Keeps 1.3%")

Weight loss coach, about $4M, wanted a magic lead magnet to lift webinar show rates. Her real funnel: 5% opt-in on the registration page (peak about 9%), 30% show, about 10% book.
Hormozi compares each step with its realistic ceiling. 30% show will never reach 60%. Booking could go from 10% to 20%. Opt-in can go from 5% to 20%, the 4x. So all testing goes to that page: ad match, simpler copy, visuals, trust, session times and how soon the next one starts. The show rate she feared most was fine.

### Healthcare services: buying growth with recruiter fees (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

$10M revenue, $2M profit, 38 providers, phones ringing with no capacity; only about 14 hires a year from a recruiter and a college program. Each provider earns about $100K gross profit.
Hormozi's math: pay 10 recruiters $25K per hire with a six-month replacement guarantee. $1M buys 40 providers, doubling the business. At 8x, $2M profit is $16M of value and $4M is $32M: about 16:1 on the spend, and it cuts the tax bill.
Process: ask what would truly hit the goal, price the resources, ask if it's worth it, ask what's stopping you.

### When more-better-new breaks: the 100x customer exception (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

Custom apparel middleman, 35% gross margin, cold outreach to college groups. Colleges were worth about $1,500 a year; some construction firms order $50,000 a year. Default advice is still to do 10 to 50x the current outreach, and he says that remains the safest bet because churn is near zero.
He signs off on the new market only because of three conditions: the value gap is around 100x, delivery is identical (only acquisition must be relearned), and the old business keeps running under an operator. If the gap were 3x, stay put.
Test it as one channel, and validate reorders fast. If construction does not reorder, go back to colleges. Judge by absolute profit per order, not margin per shirt. Duds on the list are normal when the winners are worth 25x more.

### Sales teams and ads pull revenue forward, they do not fill the well (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

Picture the audience as a pyramid with buyers at the tip. Adding a sales team or retargeting ads converts a deeper slice of the same pyramid, so revenue definitely rises. But it is forward revenue from people you already reached.
Once that slice is harvested you plateau at the growth rate of your organic, unless you build a true cold acquisition channel, which is a different game. Told to an online back-pain program at $1M a year: double content first, then add sales capacity. The bucket has to keep filling.

### ROAS collapse: fix in this order before adding a high-ticket back end (vid: 0RjIw9M1j28 — "Walk Away From $1.4M Business?")

Teen tech course, $297 front end, $189 upsell, paid ads only. ROAS fell from 2-3 to near 1, top line from about $700K in May to about $150K.
Sequence: 1) more and better creative aimed at the highest-LTV buyers, including five ads made by hand, not AI. 2) Fire the pixel on the post-upsell purchase to train on best buyers. 3) Test a one-time offer near $1,500: 10% take adds $150 per buyer, versus about $70 from the $189 at a one-third take. 4) Only then a sales team selling $5K to $10K.
A one-month drop is usually creative or pixel, not the business model.

### Turbo-bomb the constraint: one bottleneck, 10 ways at it (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

Focus and aggression fit together: one constraint, many attacks on it. Short of techs? Trade forums, relocation bonuses, poaching competitors, local ads, trade shows, trade school internships. Most owners claiming no talent exists have tried one or two.
The constraint is where effort returns most, and it is rarely the part you enjoy, since you already work on what you like.
Under $1M, one channel at huge volume. Going from $10M to $30M takes a bigger bet size: if $1,000 a day stalled, plan how to spend $10,000 a day. Prune later; first learn something worked, not why.

### Volatile is not risky: seasonality can be a feature, not your constraint (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

Sydney event caterer: 65% of annual revenue lands in six months, 12 to 1 on paid search, about 40% margin, profitable even off-season, and spent years trying to fix the lumpy calendar.
Hormozi's call: predictable swings are planned for, not solved (he cites a chocolate company and insurers). Ask one question: if nothing changed but you did twice as much, what stops you? If the answer is nothing, double PPC, crack Meta, and let competitors chase the shiny fix.

### Say the sentence: I would, but I can't because (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Travel agency on Meta, $5,500 ad spend and $30K profit last month, asks how to reach $1M a month. Answer: what stops you spending more? He said agents sell manually and slowly, so the job is simply to get more salespeople. "Well, I would, but I can't because." Whatever follows gets all your resources.
Anything spent off the constraint raises the system's potential, not its throughput. Using AI hard with no more money means you are still off the constraint; automating email reading only automates email reading.
If ad spend caps out: change the offer, find cheaper-CPM channels, or (his pick) expand the money model, since lifetime gross profit per customer is the long-term arms race.

### The fire-fighting symptom and the headcount check (vid: ehmBlLNGgsA — "Focus is subtraction live Q&A")

Constant fires, whack-a-mole, thin profit, and two to four initiatives spinning like plates usually mean one big obvious problem you refuse to confront. He estimates 10% of owners are truly tactic constrained; the other 90% are held back by a hard call they keep avoiding.
Headcount benchmark he gave a fitness owner with five offers and 30 staff: that team should do about $10M a year if poorly optimized, $30M+ if well optimized. Track revenue per head and profit per head. $200K per employee leaves little after an average $70K salary; $1M per employee is very healthy.

### Segment churn by ideal customer before scaling (vid: _ooj0V4hpgw — "Hormozi Hotline caller breakdowns")

Content agency for door-to-door and life insurance sales leaders, $1M, 17% monthly churn, $2,100 CAC. Only 6 of 40 clients were ideal, and those stayed over a year while others lasted about 3 months. Define the avatar: $750K+ a year, a training system, 10+ reps. Report churn on them separately. Scale once ideal churn hits about 5% a month; sub-3% is the goal.
Pricing: $3K and $4K tiers sit in the same buying bucket. Split them: $5K a quarter for small players, $5K a month for big ones and likely $5-10K soon. Rebuild delivery too, since the cost basis came from the old model. Message only the top; the rest still apply.

### 70x profit gap: qualification, not volume (vid: 5MjjpB8SPMo — "Make Everyone Want to Buy Your Stuff")

A consulting day with a gym-space competitor: same units, similar CAC, yet Hormozi made about 70x the profit, as customers were worth far more. He studied his best customers' common traits and made those the requirements stated across ads and funnel: gym owner, signed lease, employees. Trainers and wannabes were told it wasn't for them, and real owners felt relief. Better customers mean changing offer, bonuses and funnel, and turning away money; otherwise it's a complaint.

### The business you are really in (vid: 07jC6ooRIHw — "Sick of Shiny Object Syndrome")

Every business needs the whole car, but one part gives the most return at scale, and it is rarely what you signed up for. Hormozi's list: gyms are sales and marketing, not fitness. Supplements are brand and media traffic, not what is in the bottle. Software is product, since it sells itself (he wrongly outsourced that). Cleaning is recruiting and training low-skill labor; a friend had about $25 CAC yet could not staff. Consulting and law are talent retention. Test: take your model to its extreme and see what the biggest player actually obsesses over.

### Model problem disguised as a people problem (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

Solo cannabis ad agency owner asked how to make a first hire on about $90K a year while working all hours. Hormozi: the founder is the most valuable employee, so there is no room to hire. Scaling this is the wrong move. Either charge about 10x for the same work or make delivery about 10x more efficient. Test: you should be able to take far more customers, or be earning so much that hiring is easy.

### Whale test: no customer over 20% of revenue, he prefers 5% (vid: HKbFUWJwEG0 — "Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business")

A whale can be a minority of revenue and all of the profit, because you staffed up around it; if it leaves you break even with the costs still there. Two decisions: take it at all, and if so, go get more whales or stay with minnows. Moving up market on purpose is fine if you build for 10 more like it. Google is a whale to most, but with Amazon and Netflix beside it, they are all fish in a bigger pond.

### Skill shows in the metric list, and the constraint is the lowest number (vid: HKbFUWJwEG0 — "Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business")

Interview test for a sales manager: weak answer is close rate and cash. Strong answer adds shows, offer rate, objection types on no's, calls to close, cash as a percent of ticket, speed to contact, speed to close. Ask founders the same depth on product: time to value, churn, onboarding. To pick the fix, add the same 5 points to each funnel rate; the lowest rate gives the most throughput, then attack in order. A portfolio company's show rate went 50% to 70% from a 22-item checklist, which is a 40% lift, not 20%.

### Two feature questions to ask customers (vid: H_ZLs1-jOKQ — "Stop Being Broke. Get Busy.")

Ask: if I removed every feature but one, which would you keep? Then the inverse: if I removed one and your life didn't change, which is it? Repeat regularly and you get the most valued and least valued pieces, so you keep trimming down to what matters to the most people. A cheap customer on a call pays you in learning: pain points, hooks, what to drop.

### A channel bottleneck is not a market bottleneck (vid: A4L3byKcYQg — "Scaling explained, one formula")

He calls this the top error before a market switch. You feel maxed out, yet you do $4M in a $60B space. Keep the market; add a channel, or raise lifetime gross profit per customer so you can spend more on the same one.

### The business is a physique: the weak link shows (vid: sSEm3qJUh9s — "Poor and weak")

"Double down on strengths" is partly a lie. You can start lopsided, but over time the weak department caps the business. You don't have to do it yourself; someone must. Pipeline logic too: leads with no sales, or sales with no delivery, isn't a business. Once balanced, add volume at the front and it all grows.

### Finding the real problem: the stoner test (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

Sam Ovens says the hardest part of improving anything is working out what's wrong. On the Skool Games funnel the signal came from a stoner friend in New Zealand who couldn't tell what it was, so the note on the wall became the question itself: what is the Skool Games? Over about a week they ran roughly 30 versions of descriptions, pages and bullets past users until everyone got it at once. Test on extreme cases and fix before launch, without reality's harshness. Then announce: you were struggling, I made this, here's what it does.

### Read close rate against offer rate and cash up front (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

Track close on shows and on offers. A rep offering 30% when the team offers 70%, with a sky-high close, is cherry-picking. High close with low cash up front means taking small deposits from anyone. Offer rate reads lead quality, unless reps pitch the unqualified because they need commission. He treats cash up front as a proxy for buyer conviction: deeper discovery and looping raise it.

### Activities per customer: reverse engineer the goal (vid: h6y0nYVZgwE — "Not Unbelievably Rich Yet")

What he does in companies he buys: find what activity makes money and count how many equals one customer. Illustration: 100 reach-outs per customer means 50 customers takes 5,000 a month, about 150 a day; 6 posts per customer means five a day for 25. Output becomes something you control. Volume also lifts the rate: a week with many sales calls makes you sharper, a dead week makes you rusty.

### Flip the funnel onto talent: the cleaning example (extends the business you are really in) (vid: rj7rzOdj84A — "Your Business Is NOT What You Think It Is")

A gym owner's Airbnb cleaning side business had great LTV and CAC but could not staff. Gyms get trainers easily because fitness fans do it for free; nobody is a cleaning enthusiast. Hormozi told him to run his customer funnel on employees: leads, applications, interviews, onboarding, training, ascension. By Hormozi's memory it went from $30K to $40K a month to over $150K.

### Uneven inputs for an even output (vid: y7O-iTWwTW8 — "Interviewing a Billionaire")

Bodybuilding analogy. A balanced physique comes from the most volume on the weakest muscle, not equal work everywhere. The same in business: marketing, sales, product and success must end balanced, even if the owner only likes one, so spend most on whichever is weakest.

### Don't trust a pretty satisfaction score (vid: yPDQCfrwh8E — "Simple Strategy for 60 Days")

His company hit 98% and he called it suspicious: the survey went out right after a great onboarding. Respondents skew very happy or very sad, and asking live on a call gets a different score than a voluntary form. Sample at set weeks, but not so often that people resent it. The real gain: segment by rep, since knowing a survey follows every call makes reps prepare. Churn is lagging, so find the leading indicator (no gym visit in a month) and act before it.

### Data that changes no behavior is noise (vid: zNiXk_3C_Io — "REAL Reason Not Making Money")

Test for any reported number: if this maxed out, would we do anything differently? If not, stop looking. Direct reports over-report because he loves data. A vendor urged shorter content because a higher share got consumed; he asked how that tied to more customers or more per customer, and without an answer he didn't care. Every meeting ends in a behavior change, or everyone might as well have taken the day off.
