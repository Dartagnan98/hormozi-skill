# Hiring & Recruiting Framework
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## The Internal 5 Core Functions (Mirror of External)

The hiring/recruiting process mirrors the customer acquisition process exactly:

| External (Customers) | Internal (Employees) |
|----------------------|---------------------|
| Lead Generation | Application Generation |
| Lead Nurture | Application Nurture |
| Sales | Interviewing (selling them on why to work for you) |
| Fulfillment | Training |
| Retention + Ascension | Retention + Ascension of talent |

## 6 Ways to Get Employee Leads (Same as Customer Leads)

1. **Paid ads** (promoted job postings)
2. **Earned media** (employer brand)
3. **Owned media** (your lists, CRM, followers)
4. **Manual outbound** (cold outreach, headhunting)
5. **Affiliates** (communities of skilled people)
6. **Word of mouth** (employee referrals)

## Critical Stat

- "10 out of 10 high-quality prospects will find a job within 8 days"
- If it takes you 6 days to make first contact, you've lost them

## Application Nurture

- Should have just as structured a process as lead nurture
- Treat applications with same urgency as sales leads

## Interview Process

- Have a script just like a sales script
- Disqualify people who aren't a good fit (same as sales)
- Should feel like you're being sold TO by great candidates (especially leadership+)
- "If you aren't learning from the person in the interview, waste of time"

## Volume Matters

- Interview MORE people than you think you should
- Use group interviews for frontline to manager roles (saves time, reads vibes)
- Sam (School) interviewed 600 developers to find CTO Daniel
- Pattern recognition = biggest asset (you tune your "picker" over time)
- "To figure out which sales manager was good, I had to hire five and waste six months on each"

## Key Hiring Beliefs

- "One amazing hire away from all the growth you could ever want"
- "A lot of times you don't need a what, you need a who"
- Look for people who come "batteries included" -- already ran and won the Olympic gold

## Who to Hire First

### The Time Stack Method

1. List everything you do
2. Calculate % of time per activity
3. Ascribe value to each activity (what makes you the most money?)
4. Hire for: highest % of time + lowest cost activity

### Objective

- "The objective of the hire should be to give you time"
- "How can I get the most time back for the least amount of money?"

### Typical First Hires (bootstrap founder)

1. Admin/helper (jack-of-all-trades assistant) -- often a spouse or first follower
2. Customer fulfillment person (once you've productized your service)

### The Climb

- Continue outsourcing: lowest value first, working up to highest value
- At the top: you're only making decisions + providing vision
- "The process of entrepreneurship is just buying back time and increasing your level of skill"

### Critical Mistake

- People try to outsource CEO too soon
- "Don't jump out of the CEO seat too soon"
- At $100K/month: "Dude, you're just getting into the game"

## Buy Talent vs Build Talent

- Like buying companies vs building companies
- Buying costs more but speed to money is faster
- As company scales, tend to buy more than build
- "Great lawyers are expensive. Cheap lawyers cost a fortune."
- "Buy nice or buy twice"

## Employee ROI Calculation

### Formula

- Total payroll / Total engaged leads = Cost per engaged lead
- Example: $100K payroll, 1,000 leads = $100/engaged lead
- If 1/10 engaged leads become customer: CAC = $1,000
- Compare against LTV for ROI (target 3:1 to 4:1 LTGP)

### Diagnostic Tool (When CAC > 3x Industry Average)

- Ask: "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve AND the money to spend?"
  - If NO: advertising problem (wrong audience)
  - If YES but not enough: advertising problem (not enough volume)
  - If YES but not buying: sales problem

## HIRING DETAILS (Deep Pass)

### Don't Hire Entrepreneurs -- Hire Intrapreneurs
- Entrepreneurs in your company have only two outcomes:
  1. Lost their juju, sit in your business, complain about not making enough
  2. Regain confidence, start doing well, then take as much of your business with them as possible
- "Either way the outcome's poor"
- "I had to learn that one the hard way multiple times"
- Intrapreneurs: love driving growth and innovation but don't have appetite for risk
- Jeff Bezos calls his directors "CEOs" of sub-businesses within Amazon

### Group Interview Solution
- Stop doing 1-on-1 interviews for frontline positions (too slow)
- Set up group interviews: 5 candidates per hour
- Sales manager picks 1-2 from each group
- Can process 5x more candidates per week

### Smallest Skilled Deficiency Hiring
- When hiring, look for the smallest skill gap
- If a CFO is a "dick" but exceptional: 2 days to train sales vs 2 months to train kindness
- Hire the person who's kind, train them on the technical skill
- Chick-fil-A head of people: "A lot of people are trying to fix process when they really lost the championship in the draft"

### Quad Marketing Calendar (Hiring as Marketing)
- 4 quadrants: Internal Employees, Internal Customers, External Prospects, External Talent
- Most businesses only do External Prospects (marketing to get customers)
- Most missed quadrant: External Talent
- "Run ads for employees, generate leads, work leads, set interviews, make job offers, onboard"
- Parallel to customer journey: you make "offers" to candidates just like you do to customers

### 5 Employee Lead Stages
1. **Uncontacted** -- Application received, no outreach yet
2. **Contacted** -- First touch made (email, call, message)
3. **Engaged** -- They responded and showed interest
4. **Interviewed** -- Completed interview process
5. **Hired** -- Offer accepted, onboarding begun

"Treat employee leads with the same urgency as customer leads. 10 out of 10 high-quality prospects will find a job within 8 days."

### Document-Demonstrate-Duplicate (3Ds) -- Expanded from Book

**Step 1: Document**
- Make a checklist of every step in the process
- Pro tip: video yourself doing it FIRST, then make checklist from the recording
- "You don't think about checklisting while you're doing it -- video captures what you'd forget"

**Step 2: Demonstrate**
- Do the task in front of them while they watch
- They observe your pace, your decisions, your standards
- Let them ask questions during/after

**Step 3: Duplicate**
- They do it in front of you
- You give feedback
- Cycle until they can self-correct (give themselves feedback without you)
- "When they can spot their own mistakes, they've mastered the skill"

**Training notes from book:**
- "If they get it wrong, YOU got it wrong" -- the trainer owns the outcome
- Praise following directions even if the result is wrong -- reinforce compliance first
- One piece of feedback at a time -- don't overwhelm with corrections
- No punishment during training -- punishment is for AFTER training is complete and they know the standard

### Performance Diamond (4 Reasons Performance Drops)

When someone's performance drops, diagnose which of 4 causes:

**1. Communication -- They don't know THAT**
- They weren't told the expectation or standard
- Fix: Communicate clearly what's expected

**2. Training -- They don't know HOW**
- They were told the standard but weren't taught the method
- Fix: Run the 3Ds again (Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate)

**3. Motivation -- They don't WANT to**
- Three sub-reasons:
  - (a) Wrong reward: compensation/incentive doesn't match their motivation
  - (b) Aversion: they dislike the work, environment, or people they work with
  - (c) Outside life events: divorce, death in family, health crisis -- temporary performance hit
- Fix varies by sub-reason: restructure incentives, change role/team, or provide support

**4. Circumstances -- Something is STOPPING them**
- External blockers: broken tools, missing software access, another team blocking them
- Fix: Remove the obstacle

**Pro Tip feedback question:** "Over the last [time period], your [specific task] has changed from the norm. What do you think has gotten in the way and how can I help?"

### Daily Huddles Framework
- Meet with lead-getting employees **6-11 times per week**
- One 30-45 minute coaching session per week (deep dive)
- Daily short group huddles at start AND end of shift
- Start of shift: what are you doing today, what's your target
- End of shift: what did you accomplish, what blocked you
- "The cadence of accountability determines the speed of improvement"

### Employee ROI Calculation (from Book)
- **Formula**: Total Payroll / Total Engaged Leads = Cost per Engaged Lead
- Example: $100K payroll / 1,000 engaged leads = $100 per lead
- If 1 in 10 convert: $100 x 10 = $1,000 CAC
- Compare to LTGP: target 3:1+ ratio
- **Acquisition.com numbers**: ~$100K/month content team payroll / 30,000 leads = **$3.33/lead** with zero paid ads

### CAC Diagnostic (from Book)
- If CAC is within 3x industry average = good enough, focus on increasing LTGP instead
- If CAC > 3x industry average, ask: "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve AND the money to spend?"
  - **No** = advertising problem (wrong audience, wrong targeting, wrong message)
  - **Yes but not buying** = sales problem (script, closer skill, offer positioning)
- "Most people try to fix CAC when the real lever is LTGP"

### Easy in, hard to stay (vid: H8QfAfCbOXs — "If I could start over, culture")

Judging people from a couple of conversations is weak; judging output is strong. So make entry easier and use the first 90 days as the cut, like boot camp. After training, he's known every A salesperson within 7 to 14 days. Don't accept explanations: the best excuses come from good sellers. Napoleon wanted lucky generals.

### Your team's level tracks yours (vid: Uzr0gv4N4-s — "Change My Mind, Win $10,000")

When you were unskilled you hired mediocre people; as you got better, so did your hires. Time isn't a prerequisite for skill. Find whoever has done more in your space in less time, see what they did faster, and model it.

### Talent has a CAC too: price the referral off gross profit (vid: _ooj0V4hpgw — "Hormozi Hotline caller breakdowns")

Construction safety staffing, $2.1M, 13 field staff. Referral bonus was $1,500 total on a person making $130K gross profit ($70K pay, about $200K revenue). Raise it to $10K-$15K, with $1K up front and $9K at 90 days; a recruiter would charge that anyway.
Repeatable roles get a full funnel with cost of acquiring talent against lifetime gross profit per employee: ad, opt-in, VSL, group call. One-off roles go to recruiters. Add a zero: not one or two recruiters, ten on contingency. He calls it a bow tie: demand funnels and supply funnels, and service businesses miss the second.

### Buy your way out: levels of who (vid: 0EqJD2o-Mnk — "Helping 6 Business Owners Scale")

For a $6M owner who will not add hours: pay for talent that leads growth and take a short-term profit hit, since profit can be made up later and family time cannot. Hormozi's staircase: minimum wage, then $50K, six figures, up to multi-million hires, each making the last level look small. His president's line: the best talent is always ahead of you.

### Stuck on local recruiting: national ads plus relocation (Tanner, Bozeman) (vid: 0EqJD2o-Mnk — "Helping 6 Business Owners Scale")

Owner at about $1.5M with high-end clients needs techs who each produce about $300K. Hormozi: would you spend $50K to land a good one? Run national job ads with a generous relocation bonus split across hiring and about six months in, or use recruiters. Serving premium customers means margin to pay for premium people. Then time study and hand the new tech the bottom half of your stack.

### Hire finance first: "find your salary in 90 days" (vid: 6m6DCQMASEM — "10 Millionaires Asked Me")

Caller ran a used car lot with about $500K-$600K profit and six problems at once. Hormozi: six problems means you are missing about three people. Bring in a finance-minded operator first with a deal: I cannot afford you yet, find your cost in savings within 90 days. He guessed $250K a year was sitting in expenses. That funds the next hire. Treat pay levels like new ad-spend territory: you have just never paid someone at that level yet, and each level reveals a different grade of talent.

### First hires by net hourly gap (vid: 7NqsFh5ej0w — "The first 5 hires")

Extends lowest value first. Rank each task you do by dollars per hour you make on it minus what replacing it costs. The usual core team: customer support and admin (either order), then salesperson or setter, marketing assist such as a videographer, a fractional bookkeeper, with tech as next full-timer for CRM and logins. Flip the order to your skill: a strong seller hires a prospector so they only close, a weak one buys sales earlier. Legal, tax and books start outside. Early hires are extensions of you; later each grows a team, mostly in sales, marketing and delivery.

### Operator test: did work fall globally, and do you have more time? (vid: HKbFUWJwEG0 — "Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business")

An operator leads; they don't just manage checklists, and the team should look up to them. Litmus: you gain time and bandwidth. If they hand you new things to do, they are hurting. Judge each new rule by local vs global: CRM notes add work for sales and remove more work from everyone else, so keep it. A bad operator adds systems without removing any, then complains no one finishes the list. His two portfolio cases: the owner's buddy from odd-job businesses added headcount with no new revenue and a very profitable company just broke even for nine months; a veteran who had taken a chain from 100 to 2,000 locations spent a quarter on unit profit alone and margins expanded fast.

### Hire for a stated problem, and title low (vid: HKbFUWJwEG0 — "Reverse Engineered the Perfect Business")

Give each candidate the real stats and the problem and ask how they'd attack it; no hypotheticals needed, and 10 answers show 10 ways of thinking. Track record on comparable work beats who you'd be friends with. Start titles low: manager, then director, VP, COO. Handing COO to the day-one employee leaves no room to hire above them, and few leaders from 1x are still fit at 10x.

### Ideal scene: know what great looks like in each role (vid: Jv3GmZ9Odko — "What Millionaires Know")

Why a second company goes faster when strategy is the same: pattern recognition in people, not tactics. You pay ignorance tax until you know what excellent looks like and the order to hire heads. Failures in portfolio companies: mom who does math as controller, top seller as sales director. A mishire costs twice: time to onboard and offboard, and the next role can't be filled meanwhile. Lower your tolerance; he's seen A players slide, never a non-A become one. Ask someone who has built that department several times, not someone six months ahead of you.

### How a real executive assistant runs the day (vid: Jv3GmZ9Odko — "What Millionaires Know")

After a decade of assistants that never fit, one finally showed him what right looks like. She holds a stack ranked by his priorities with time estimates for each task and fills any opening: 18 minutes free, do this and this. He works all day from what she puts in front of him and will never go without one again. A case of not knowing the ideal scene until you have seen it once.

### Twenty interviews and you learned nothing? Hire none (vid: EdUAAVKl_io — "Business that runs without me")

Each candidate should show you what you're missing. By the end you know the role better, and the person you pick still knows more than you. Knowing a role is what lets you set the bar and spot who clears it.

### Skill test on the same raw file; turn vibe into a checklist (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

Editors: everyone gets one raw file and 48 hours, so editing skill is the only difference. About 1,200 applied and 60 submitted, or 5%; Caleb picks a few and those get interviewed for vibe. Videographers are the reverse: for someone beside you all day, he'd take half the skill if he liked them as much, and it shows in the footage. When filming felt heavy after Caleb moved up, he refused to call it vibe and asked what Caleb did: nodded during takes, praised after, asked follow-ups the audience would ask. That became the checklist. Most people can't teach because they never reduce things to behaviors.

### Same problem, multiple quarters, same owner: it's a who (vid: c_6BrF7jOGk — "Doubled A Business in 60 Days")

When one department repeats a problem quarter after quarter under one person, stop hunting for a new how. Test candidates by asking what data they would collect and how they would fix each result: vague and high-level answers mean shallow execution. For a sales director he wants someone who loves developing people and also names the metrics.

### A VSL that sells the company to recruits (vid: hHkdbr6_JJs — "How Acquisition.com Makes Money")

The whole video was his internal recruiting video: how money flows and where a career goes. He thinks every company needs one, selling the company as the product. Career path sold: reps across 100 businesses in the advisory practice qualify you to run a venture he'd sponsor.

### ACQ's three screens, for hires and founders alike (vid: kW2vDMAmlPI — "How I know who to trust")

Unimpeachable character: the thread running through loyalty, integrity and long-term thinking. Sincere candor: the self-awareness to give feedback and the humility to take it, since unspoken resentments fill the space between people and clarity of communication is what a company runs on. Competitive greatness, from John Wooden: at your best when your best is needed, enjoying the hard challenge. Solid and honest but not driven is a no. Driven and candid with doubtful character is a no. In interviews read how they answer as much as what they say.

### Scaling is talent inflow matching customer inflow (vid: kloJJeiysxg — "4 Steps to Unf*** Your Business")

His definition: the inflow of talent matches the inflow of customers, so you need an exceptional conversion process for talent too. Brand counts twice, once for customers and once for recruits, and strong people read your reviews before replying. It becomes a virtuous or a vicious cycle.

### Every skill is trainable; is it worth it? (vid: kloJJeiysxg — "4 Steps to Unf*** Your Business")

A caller wanted a program to manufacture the senior growth partners he couldn't hire. Hormozi: setters and closers you can teach, but high-level strategy took him about 10 years, so you won't wait for that. You could teach anyone anything if you broke it down enough; the question is return on effort. Take someone already most of the way there and use training only to bridge to your business.

### Sales academy: a recruiting funnel parallel to the customer one (vid: jqo0lVveh98 — "If You Have a Service Business")

For a fence company near $20M with plenty of leads but few reps, afraid to hire. Build the same stages for reps as for customers: applicant gen, nurture, interview, onboarding, retention, ascension, with the same stats. Math: about $200K in pay for a rep making about $1M gross profit is 5:1, among the best returns available, and at an 8x multiple adds roughly $8M enterprise value. Ask why you fear hiring: if not cash flow, it's between your ears.

### Job boards only reach the unhappy (vid: z7X95bn2T6A — "Team Replace Themselves with AI")

Top producers are already employed, so job board candidates skew dissatisfied. Reach the good ones with personalized outreach; his recruiters use AI video by name. Passing interview notes on helps both ways: pass context to the next interviewer, or tell a rejected candidate why.
