# Compensation & Equity Frameworks
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## Phantom Equity

- Equity that doesn't vest unless triggered (usually by a sale/liquidity event)
- Employee doesn't have to pay tax until vesting (unlike real equity transfer)
- If employee leaves, they lose phantom equity (protects owner)
- Employee isn't personally liable (protects employee)

### Typical Structures

- Senior leadership: 0.5% to 2% phantom equity
- True executive leading half the company: 3-5%
- Vesting schedule: e.g., 5% over 5 years (1%/year)
- Cliff: first year vests entirely at 12 months, then quarterly after

### Phantom Equity vs Profit Sharing

- These are separate and independent
- Can be equity owner without profit distributions
- Can participate in profit distributions without equity
- Hormozi prefers to keep profit sharing separate

### When to Use

- "Winners win and they want to participate in the upside"
- "The more money I make other people, the more money I make"
- Sam Walton: wished he'd done employee stock options sooner
- Align employee life outcomes with owner life outcomes

## When NOT to Give Equity

- "Just because you have a need doesn't mean they have to be equity owner"
- Accountant analogy: "I just pay the accountant. He's not a partner."
- Only two reasons someone wants equity: control or exit participation
- If you're not selling and they don't want control, just do performance-based pay

## Value-Based Compensation Philosophy

- "The goal of the company is to pay as little as possible for the highest amount of value"
- Value = what you get, Price = what you pay
- Bigger discrepancy = better deal (same as investing)
- Only raise compensation if: can't attract talent OR can't retain talent
- If neither is a problem, current comp is fine

## Star Employee Economics

- Superstar salesperson outselling 3x normal reps: worth paying 10-20% more
- Losing them = losing half your sales = far worse than the raise
- "Make the economic argument"
- Conversely: amazing customer service person leaves tomorrow -- how much less revenue? "Probably not a lot."

## Specific Benchmarks

- Phantom equity for senior leadership: 0.5-2%
- True executives: 3-5%
- Standard vesting: 5 years with 1-year cliff
- Pay 25% above market for A players, get 5x output
- Star salesperson worth 3-5 regular salespeople

## SUPERSTAR CEO STRUCTURE (Deep Pass)

### Billionaire's Model (Private Call)
- Man doing $1B+/year, 50 privately held companies, mid-70s
- CEO profit sharing: 5% of profits + opportunity to buy increasing equity chunks
- One CEO now owns ~30% of largest company, started at 5-10%
- "I don't believe businesses should make a profit. Profits are unnatural."
- Broke company into 100 pieces, gave equity to superstars, checked out. 5 years later: $1 billion
- "Not being greedy" grows the pie faster than keeping a bigger slice

### A-Player Compensation Rule
- 25% above market pay, 5x the output
- "One A-player is worth five B-players"
- Look for endurance/grit first, teach the rest
- 3 weeks below expectations = cut

## EMPLOYEE VS BUSINESS OWNER MINDSET (Deep Pass -- Batch 4)

- Same $2M income, same business:
  - Owner who does everything = net worth is cash (high-paying job)
  - Owner with team = cash + $10M+ equity (sellable asset)
- Wrong: "If you want it done right, do it yourself"
- Right: "If you want it done right, get someone to spend all their time doing it"
- "100% of humans are replaced every 100 years"

### Pay for A players, by role (vid: Uzr0gv4N4-s — "Change My Mind, Win $10,000")

Better people paid better cost less than poor people paid poorly. It holds horizontally: an A cashier isn't a Fortune 500 CEO, but among cashiers pay significantly more for a clear outperformer. Cut three Bs to fund one A at the pay of two. Pushback he accepted: early on you play the cards you're dealt, and hiring As too soon can set you back.

### Back-solve operator pay from target earnings, then give the phone speech (vid: 6_CCutkM11g — "$100M Money Models Live Launch")

Chiropractor set a new hire at 50/50, then ran the numbers: at $20K gross, ad spend left him nothing. First fix: pay from gross profit, never revenue.
ACQ's method: set on-target earnings at about the 85th percentile of market, then reverse it into activity. His deliberately rough example: $80K base plus $1,000 per unit at 10 units a month gets to $200K.
Keep it to two variables at most, since people need to predict their pay. For an operator running a whole location, skip per-product splits: base plus a 10-20% share of total location profit ties them to the full P&L.
Then state the trade: hold up your phone and roleplay emergencies. Any answer of 'call the owner' is wrong, except named cases like spend over a set dollar limit. Put their photo on the wall so the location feels like theirs.

### Trainer bonus for shorter ramp, with a callback clawback (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

Pool company took six weeks to train a tech. Offer the trainer $500 to get a new tech road-ready in two, on condition he certifies that tech's work: for the next month, any callback from the trainee's rushed job, the trainer goes and fixes it for free. Speed gets paid; quality stays his problem.

### Cost-of-sales cap: 12% all in, and what it means for pay-per-close (vid: QgmRlzh0Y7M — "How To Play To Win")

In-house, Hormozi wants total sales cost at about 12% or less, counting management, software stack and comp.
Outsourced at the same 12%, adding 8% gives a 40% margin, still thin once you add the org that finds and sells clients.
Caller keeps $100 of a $200 fee (10% of a sale) and pays a rep $100. He says 50/50 only makes sense if the agency also gets continuity or an activation fee. Rep pay should follow the skill needed to close; client price follows supply and demand. Offer-driven fractional sales tends to be boom or bust and draws mercenary reps.

### Answering a raise request you cannot justify (vid: WeH2E1fGgaw — "Daily Q&A: selling, comp and failure")

$100,000 employee wants $150,000 without the added value. You can say no; explain why, and more importantly how to earn even more. Agree on the number, then name the skills and activities that would produce outcomes making it reasonable.
Get them to say how their work makes money: when I do this, churn drops, LTV rises, we net more per customer without acquiring more.
Frame: "every business owner will always pay for a fraction of future earnings." Reluctance is about handing over money already being made for work that did not create it.

### Rep pay: self-gen gets upside, fed reps split 50/50 from market value (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

Self-gen hunters who run click to close get mostly performance pay, as much as you can afford, because the outcome is all theirs. Reps you feed leads get a split, because a shut-down ad account isn't on them.
Reverse engineer from the skill's market value. Role worth $200K: base and variable about even. On a $100K base, earning $120K means below KPI and not long for the team. On an $80K base, $160K is on target. Then work out the deals a reasonable rep should close from your lead flow.
Golf test: a pro must drive, chip and putt; anyone can tap in a 6-inch putt. Sales comp eating a high share of revenue usually signals a weak motion and training, not a talent gap.

### Employee raises: shift risk onto yourself and stand next to the money (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Advice for people with stable jobs. You get paid in proportion to risk taken, so to earn more move risk onto yourself: ask for variable pay, or buy stock or put your own capital into an initiative.
His framing: any sane owner will happily hand over a fraction of money they have not yet made that you directly helped make. It's a fraction because you lean on the rest of the business. The clearer you are about where value came from, and the closer you were to it, the more you can negotiate.
Leverage is who needs whom more, and skills raise it. His forecast: people will arrive with their own AI agents as a whole department and be paid a share of that department's cost.

### Invert your pay: what would make you paid the least? (vid: -Pz0NpcILOY — "Inverse thinking on pay")

Low value, asking for little of it, and a line of replacements behind you. Flip each: create more value, capture a bigger share, be hard to replace. Hormozi adds two hidden variables: who you serve, and the vehicle. Pay is a slice of the business, so a dry cleaner cannot pay a million a year. Top talent should pick bigger visions; owners need a pie big enough for others to get rich in. Works the same for owner or employee, with one layer between you and the customer.

### Employee raise script: tie it to a number you move (vid: -Pz0NpcILOY — "Inverse thinking on pay")

Pick the revenue or profit you directly affect, quantify it, and ask if a percentage of that would be unreasonable, not a share of the whole company. The closer you sit to the money, the less your value gets discounted or argued. Value equation applies to bosses too: someone who needs chasing has lower perceived likelihood and more effort than someone who brings ideas and gets it done.

### Paired metrics, cleaning crew version (extends paired metrics) (vid: 07jC6ooRIHw — "Sick of Shiny Object Syndrome")

A gym-owner friend paid cleaners per clean, not per hour, which raised output per person. The pair: if a job is poor they redo it unpaid. It doubled as the customer offer: not happy, we clean the whole house again free.

### Script for resetting broken comp (vid: F84olnKkseM — "Business Was Hard, 4 Concepts")

Revenue shares to every employee, or reps out-earning owners at scale, won't survive growth. Own it: I messed up and mispriced your pay, so I can't fund your growth or new locations. Then show shared pain: you keep a paycheck, I take nothing, for 6 to 12 months, for a shot at 10x bigger. Some will leave, and that teaches you to recruit on the new plan. Avoiding it because they might quit is the trap.

### Pay for saves: 10-15% commission on the back end (vid: afbP6sB_Atc — "Keep more customers")

Everyone was paid to sell and nobody to keep. CS answered maybe, slowly, with no script. Adding a 10% to 15% commission on saved customers got reps calling and texting three to five times. Paying $200 to save a $2,000 sale beats zero, and the churned customer also costs the buyers they warn away. He frames extra CS headcount as capital allocation: a $70K to $100K rep against $2M saved.

### Commission CS on a range, weighted by the metric (vid: yPDQCfrwh8E — "Simple Strategy for 60 Days")

Extends pay for saves. Set a target range and pay by position in it: 100% earns 2x, 50% earns x, 0% earns nothing. In a 16 week program, a customer reaching week 12 weights at 75%. Needs CRM first: no metric, no incentive. Show each rep's stat beside the team's so a laggard sees it and follows the process.
