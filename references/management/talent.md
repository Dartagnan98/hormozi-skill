# Talent Assessment & A Players
## Source: Alex Hormozi (602 video transcripts)

## The Talent Grid (What-How-Who for People)

### Three Levels of Employee Development

| Level | Focus | Title | Activity | Business Stage |
|-------|-------|-------|----------|----------------|
| 1 | WHAT to do | Individual Contributor | Doing | $0-$1M |
| 2 | HOW to do it | Manager | Teaching | $1M-$3M |
| 3 | WHO can do it | Leader | Systematizing | $3M-$10M+ |

### Level 1: Individual Contributor

- Must learn the skills (the "what")
- All time spent doing
- Proficiency doesn't take long: "2 hours of consuming information, then you try"
- "You learn more in your first 20 sales than in the first 20 hours of learning about sales"

### Level 2: Manager

- Understands the sequence (the "how")
- Can teach others to replicate the skill
- Holds people accountable to consistent execution
- "Managers don't need to be better than individual contributors -- they need to understand HOW it works"

### Level 3: Leader

- Finds and develops the right people (the "who")
- "Building the machine that builds the machine"
- Very different from managers -- this is what most people fail to recognize
- Most managers (despite tenure) aren't leaders
- Good individual contributors are often terrible leaders and vice versa

## A Players vs B Players

### How to Identify A Players

- A players take ownership ("I'm not sending that to MY customers")
- Go beyond presenting problems -- they bring solutions
- Do more research, more diligence, higher quality work
- Ask questions you haven't thought of
- Have lower tolerance for anything but excellence than B players

### The Apparel Story

- Brand manager tasked with finding new revenue
- B player: Google 3 companies, pick cheapest, slap logo on
- A player: emailed 20 manufacturers, tried all samples, rejected crap quality, found alternative solution, came with implementation plan
- "None of what I described was earth shattering -- it was just a willingness to do more work"

### A Player Economics

- Harvard study: A players worth 5x B players
- Pay 25% above market, get 5x output
- "Still the biggest arbitrage value creation opportunity every business owner has"

### Chick-fil-A Quote

- "A lot of companies want to compete against us in the championship, but the reality is they lost in the draft"
- Some people come with batteries included -- worth the extra 5-20 interviews to find them

## Measuring Talent

- Measure on RATE OF IMPROVEMENT, not starting skill level
- "Intelligence = rate of learning"
- Someone who starts lower but improves faster will pass the experienced-but-slow person
- "I'd rather have a more intelligent employee who learns faster than a more experienced employee who learns slower"

## The Intolerance Principle

- "The higher the person who runs the division, the lower amount of tolerance for anything but excellence"
- Intolerance trickles down and gets diluted at each level
- If someone below a supervisor has lower tolerance than the supervisor, reverse those roles
- "The person who cares the most should be the one in charge"

## TALENT INSIGHTS (Deep Pass Batch 2)

### The Talent Gap is Everything
- "The difference between small and big businesses is how many A-players they have"
- Most small business owners hire a lot, get lucky with one person
- Big business owners maintain lower tolerance for mediocrity
- "They keep looking until they find six Andreas. Then it becomes too easy."

### The Person You Thought About This Morning
- "If they're not making you money and you're not sleeping with them, don't think about them first thing in the morning"
- That person takes 25% of your bandwidth
- "The biggest lever of growth is on the other side of a few hard conversations"

### Performance Improvement Plan (PIP Script)
- 14-day PIP: "You got 14 days to fix your s***"
- The script: say TO THEM what you say ABOUT THEM behind their back
- 1/5 have genuine turnaround
- 4/5 either refuse or don't change -> exit them

### Confronting Discomfort
- "People who will win vs. won't is based on ability to confront discomfort"
- "Guys cold plunge and lift heavy but can't say 'I think you suck a little bit'"
- "When you're qualifying that someone's heart is in the right place, it's because it's obvious they suck"

### Owner vs. Team-Run Business Value
- Owner-dependent $5M = worth the income only
- Team-run $5M ($2M take-home, doesn't require owner) = $10-20M asset
- Going from $2M to $3M annual income = adding $10M to net worth (at 10x multiple)
- "You become wealthy from the things you own, not the things you do"
- "Right now, many businesses are not valuable. No one would buy them because it's just you with helpers."

### Volume x Leverage = Output
- If you're lazy, you need massive leverage
- "If you want big things, you need a lot of other people's hours working for you"

### Average IQ math (vid: H8QfAfCbOXs — "If I could start over, culture")

Three 140s and an 80: adding another 140 raises the average less than removing the 80. A team improves faster through elimination than recruiting. Concentrated high performers attract and bring others.

### Pull your future standard forward (vid: H8QfAfCbOXs — "If I could start over, culture")

His biggest difference from 15 years ago is who he'd call good enough. As you win, you attract better, so what was good becomes mediocre. Ask how high your bar will be in 5 years, then ask how quickly you can apply it now.

### Two ways to replace the founder as coach, pick your problem (vid: I0nCuqu7Cac — "Hotline: mental toughness and offers")

Solo operator serving 45 coaching communities at $2,000 to $4,000 a month, stuck doing fulfillment. Leila's fork:
Full-time learners: few hires, lots of discretionary effort, little management, but pay anchors high, they leave once they've learned it, and key-man risk is heavy.
Fractional experts at 10 to 15 hours a week: cheap per person, easy to replace, more scheduling and management.
Hormozi's sourcing shortcut: recruit your own best alumni with an extra $5K a month and the chance to give back.

### Pods stop service staff leaving with your clients (vid: 2nTkffKVnas — "12 rules of thumb, trades, learning")

Agency owner's media buyers keep leaving to go solo. Two answers: pay them more, or change structure, and he'd pick structure because it fixes the model. Split client service into components with a pod on each account instead of one provider per book. One person walks with a book of clients easily; four people rarely do.

### Build a hiring funnel that mirrors the customer funnel (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

Duct cleaning company, about $1.8M, hiring one tech every three to four months while its new marketing is about to need far more. Map the stages: applications are leads, interviews are the sale, onboarding is activation, then retention and promotion. Building the parallel process is the whole job.
Techs were trained from scratch, so stop relocating them from other states on up to $10,000 packages. Spend that on Indeed and Facebook ads inside 50 miles, about 300 applicants a week into two group interviews, book 30 each, then one-on-ones. Hire for attitude, cast wide.

### First hire buys your selling hours, not your free time (vid: DfXy_PzlC0o — "Get rich young, plus live Q&A")

Couple running two Swiss boxing and fitness studios, both on the floor all day. Hire a coach for the half day when sales are best (afternoons for them). His rough math: about $20 a session, four sessions a day, five days, $400 a week.
The trade works only if membership sales in those freed hours beat that cost, since buying time with money loses money otherwise. No appointment? Walk the floor so members still see you.
Fear that clients only come for you: move authority over a session at a time, keep greeting people, and hire someone better than you. You will not run every class forever. You and your partner move to higher-priced semi-privates once coaches handle the group classes.

### Nail salon: the real business model is talent retention (vid: 0RjIw9M1j28 — "Walk Away From $1.4M Business?")

In low-skill, client-loyal services (nails, cleaning), demand is easy and labor is the hard part. Techs leave once their book fills and the split looks bad.
The chain he looked at solved it with a ladder: every set number of sessions unlocks a more valuable skill, which raises ticket, hourly and split, reaching $70,000+ a year after two or three years. Later, run a new location for 5 to 10% of profit.
Make staying easy, not leaving hard. And on slow winters: if you can generate demand profitably, you are never seasonal, only capacity limited.

### Your business is capped by who is willing to work for you (vid: TLpLmhWqKEU — "Key man risk and caller breakdowns")

Hormozi's big-picture take: the business does not run without you because the best people will not work for you yet. Top designers want to work for a Steve Jobs.
Two things attract them: track record and the ability to sell the future. A strong future-seller can outsell a thin record. With no business record, generic proof (top of class, leadership roles) shows ambition and buys some credibility.
He says talent at Acquisition.com is orders of magnitude above Gym Launch or Prestige Labs; he almost had to build those to prove he could.

### IQ per square foot, and five signs you hired too low (vid: ehmBlLNGgsA — "Focus is subtraction live Q&A")

His numbers: A players cost 1.5x a B player but are worth 3-5x, and the gap widens higher up. A $100K hire returning $300K is a worse deal than a $1M hire returning $10M. On every hire ask whether IQ per square foot rises or falls.
Signs you are hiring too low: you write rules you can't believe you need, you do their work for them, team speed is slowing, revenue per head is falling, and you like your coworkers less each year. They may have fit the business five years ago.

### Why professional firms offer ownership tracks (vid: 07jC6ooRIHw — "Sick of Shiny Object Syndrome")

Expertise firms scale only with top talent, and great people otherwise leave with clients and open their own shop. So big firms are partnerships: a track to a small slice of something huge beats starting alone, with sub-specialties to climb inside. Low-skill services hit a recruiting wall too, but at a different level: volume and incentives, not skimming the best.

### Contractor bench for burst work (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

For his launch ads he spun up 15 contract editors with one person doing QA instead of hiring full-time. Same for a founder's odd 20-minute chores: list them, ship them to contractors or Fiverr specialists, or train a VA. You trade 20 hours for two, and when volume justifies it one person manages them all.

### Live-selling team: buy proven, and cut what is not your edge (vid: 8C_6qojTA78 — "Helping E-Commerce Owners Scale")

Whatnot bag seller made $5K-$7K per show (caller's figures) and was short on hosts. Buy version: approach micro influencers already selling live on Amazon, pay a percentage of profit, and contract them to sell only for you for a set period. Warehouse roles are commodity, so give them a good leader and move on; value sits in sales and distribution. Her star copy-of-herself seller rubbed people wrong: let her sell, do not have her manage.

### Entrepreneurial is a dial, not a type (vid: QQGHCG8d1So — "Emailed myself my failures")

He retired his old "hire entrepreneurs, not employees" line as a false binary. People sit on a continuum and move with circumstances: laid-off staff found a streak during COVID, and his father-in-law, a tenured engineering dean, started a business after retiring. So he screens for alignment with the destination and intrinsic drive, then builds the environment that brings the trait out.

### Level 10 talent needs a level 10 opportunity (vid: QQGHCG8d1So — "Emailed myself my failures")

Taken from Schwarzman's book. The best people won't join a plan to make some money, and a big goal costs about the same effort as a small one. Stuck around $30M for almost four years, Hormozi says he got out once other leaders drove growth and the incentives let them win big. Better to find a .400 hitter and orient them than teach swinging, when you can afford it.

### Stable of stallions: stuck at $30-40M until he stopped hoarding (vid: ULGT0Qpglek — "17 Life-Changing Conversations")

Stuck at $30M to $40M for about three years, he listed everything he personally planned to do to one of the first billionaires he met, who laughed: it's about everybody else. Give smart people a real slice so they get rich too. Ten people with 20 years each is 200 years of experience; if everyone learned from you, the company runs on one brain and one lifetime. Mastery in every department can be bought faster than learned, and he says that got him through the wall.

### The boardroom that reset his bar (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

Extends knowing what excellent looks like. Across a long table from private equity firms during the Gym Launch sale, he listened to their team's questions and his team's answers. His people had built a $100M-plus company and the contrast still reset what he thought talent was. Normal path: hire a sales director who seems good, lose about six months, fix the process, fire his hires, try again, sometimes one or two years until one works. Then repeat by role. How fast you learn what good looks like is how fast you move through levels.

### Top talent: their dreams must fit inside your vision (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

"Sell me on why I should take you" or a 12-hour offer deadline ends the talk with anyone truly talented, since they have options. He capped at $3M a year, then took home $17M net 24 months after Leila came in; she likes people, he avoided them, and her mission of a company people love fits inside his. Direction needed runs inversely with skill. Sam Ovens's route: money can't beat Google or SpaceX, so it took about 600 interviews in a year and five wrong hires before a co-founder title landed one; after that, referrals from strong engineers. For non-engineers, Skool hires from members, checking the activity chart first.

### Your best talent isn't on the team yet (vid: WsYgWC7NmO8 — "Top 10 Fast Business Lessons")

Extends Level 10 talent needs a level 10 opportunity. A friend's line: the best people exist in the future, so keep room for them in the vision and the equity. A local dry cleaner won't draw them; a true, long-run goal of a new way to clean clothes can, once enough size makes people believe it.

### Decade or a day, plus the wife and wallet tests (vid: kW2vDMAmlPI — "How I know who to trust")

If you wouldn't deal with this client or hire for a decade, don't for a day. Secondhand, from a friend's talk with someone on SEAL Team Six: every SEAL would die for a teammate, so the harder questions are whether you could leave him with your wife for a month and expect nothing, and leave him more money than he has ever had, come back in a decade, and find it in the same corner. High performers with ego are the hard case: they resist feedback, point fingers, and hurt culture. Much of success is fewer dumb mistakes, so treat these people as landmines you can see coming.

### "These ads suck": how little good talent needs (vid: kloJJeiysxg — "4 Steps to Unf*** Your Business")

Sam Ovens's story of early Google: Larry Page printed irrelevant ad results, wrote "these ads suck", and pinned it on the wall. An engineer took it down, stayed up all night, and built the fix Sam credits with AdSense. No meeting, no brief. Sam's read: complaints about communication usually mean the wrong person, since with the right one a few words do it, and with the wrong one no amount of communication works.

### Small companies win talent on character (vid: jqo0lVveh98 — "If You Have a Service Business")

The unsaid limit: strong people don't want to work for you because you haven't achieved or sustained enough. Big names offer huge bonuses and visions; you can't. So you punch above your weight with character: no hot temper, no sudden turns, nobody knows you had a bad day. That talent then pulls the business up.

### Expertise firms: margin is the talent arbitrage, partner track is the lock (vid: rj7rzOdj84A — "Your Business Is NOT What You Think It Is")

In coaching, consulting and law, profit is the gap between what you pay someone and charge for them. McKinsey and Big Four style firms are recruiting and training machines, while the guru-plus-minions model dilutes service and stalls. Train people well and they can leave with clients, which is why these firms become partnerships: staying must beat the risk-adjusted return of leaving.

### Chop the unicorn's horn off (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

Extends replacing yourself with several people. Combinations are what make someone rare; split into chunks and each is commodity. He couldn't find another Alex, but could find someone as good at phone sales alone, then someone as good at ads alone. List what the key person does, hire or teach each piece, and the mystique ends.
