## Definition
A **money model** is a series of offers.
A **good money model** is a series of offers structured to:
1. Minimize CAC
2. Maximize gross profit
3. Collected in the first 30 days

## The Rental Car Story (Money Model in Action)
Started at $19/day reservation. Left paying $400 for 3 days.

Sequence of 7 offers:
1. **Car upgrade** -- "We don't have your reserved car. Is a Tundra okay?" ($19/day -> $69/day)
2. **Late checkout** -- half-day fee to avoid late return penalty (fee they invented)
3. **Insurance upgrade** -- "Do you want to upgrade your insurance?" (implied you already have minimum)
4. **Minimum insurance** -- "So just the minimum then?" (implied you must get it because it's "minimum")
5. **Prepaid gas** -- $3.75/gal vs. market $3.50 (buy the FULL tank upfront; whatever you don't use = pure profit)
6. **Damages** -- implicit upsell (scratch it, you pay)
7. **Late return fee** -- yet another invented penalty

**Key lessons:**
- "Minimum package" naming = masterful. 90% of people take it because it sounds required
- Rental car companies invented the fees, then sell avoidance of those fees
- Making 5+ offers in one conversation is normal. 19/20 customers don't mind.

## How to Build Your Own Money Model (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Start with an Attraction Offer
- Get leads to engage with you

### Step 2: Pick an Upsell Offer
- Menu upsell, anchor upsell, rollover upsell, or classic

### Step 3: Pick a Downsell Offer
- For people who say no: payment plan -> feature downsell -> free trial

### Step 4: Pick a Continuity Offer
- Get cash back in 30 days, more customers, more money

### Step 5: Perfect One at a Time
- Adding all four at once will break your business

### Rules
1. Perfect ONE at a time. Adding all four at once will break your business.
2. Start with a lower price, get yeses, raise prices in stages.
3. Simple scales, fancy fails. Fewest offers possible.
4. Affiliate products can fill gaps without operational cost.
5. Any attraction offer becomes continuity by adding a rollover clause.
6. A business model is NOT patentable. It's a speed game -- innovate, extract, repeat.

### Five ways to monetize an audience, by risk (vid: eW04CvHKvMo — "34.2M views live, SPCL and hotline")

For a media business with no product, lowest to highest risk: affiliate (paid after the sale), media sponsorships, minority deal (a sponsor gives you equity, royalty or rev share in exchange for your push and maybe exclusivity), white label (your brand on a product you do not manufacture), your own product or service.
The first three let you keep making media and only change the economics. The last two put you in the business of business. Owning the product makes the most and is also a distraction without resources. Easiest raise before any of it: more eyeballs and more sponsor spots.

### Offer ladder rule: every tier should double revenue (vid: 32fVmwvLmTM — "Reddit AMA")

Hormozi's math: upsell uptake is usually 20-30% of customers. If 20% buy at five times the price, that one tier doubles revenue. So set tiers at 5x the base, starting from the price and working backward to what would justify it. School example: $9 a month, then $99.
The common mistake is tiers too close together, which just re-sells the same buyer and comes from pricing out of your own wallet. Most people go neither high nor low enough. Wealth is fractal: in his telling the top 1% holds far more than the bottom half combined, and tiers signal who each one is for.
Pick a zone, not a perfect number. Units times gross margin is a curve, with the same dollars on either side, so service businesses should choose the side with fewer customers.

### Price upsells at 5x: customers are fractal (extends tiers too close) (vid: 1UhvBSQFy6A — "10x Revenue with 1 New Sales Process")

80/20 repeats inside your buyers: the top 20% have about five times the spending power. Price an upsell at 5x your average ticket and if 20% take it, you double revenue per customer: $1,000 becomes $2,000. A cheap add-on barely moves it; a $20 item on a $200 customer is the mistake he found in that chain (fine if the goal is staff pay).

### Sell the sawdust: refer out who you cannot serve (vid: Q2VUuUGfpNY — "6 Hacks To Make More Money")

You already paid for traffic that cannot afford your core offer. Besides a cheaper version of your own, send them to a partner who specializes in that buyer, for an affiliate or referral commission. You earn on a lost lead, the partner gets free customers, and the person thanks you, provided the partner is good.

### The ladder ends at the last rung that over-delivered (vid: WrCt0R3FBFs — "1.2M follower audience in 6 months")

Free content worth more than its price leads to a $1K buy that makes them $10K, then "what else have you got?" The relationship stops once they pay $100K and get $100K: you've used up the price-to-value gap and made a satisfied customer, not a raving one. Demand stays above capacity only while each step gives far more than it charges.

### Advertise the best ratio, ascend into the rest (vid: jzKpAtzKQ54 — "You NEED to Know This Number")

Extends the hair salon front-end example with the math. Caller's rough numbers: extension training about $2,000 at about $67 CAC, near 30 to 1; business coaching $15,000 at about $2,000 CAC, near 7 to 1. Stop advertising coaching and make it the back end, so its CAC on those buyers is zero. If 20% ascend, that adds $3,000 per customer: $5,000 total, and 30 to 1 becomes 75 to 1. Exception: a tiny market. "Which product do I run ads on" is a math problem, not an opinion.

### Audience deals scored on exit value (vid: oRqnTOT9ZG8 — "Monetize An Audience in 2026")

Extends the media business ladder. Judge affiliate, sponsorship, partnership and own brand on four things: exit value, cash in or out, work, risk. Affiliate revenue is fast and uncapped, valued at a multiple discounted by how reliable the partner is. Sponsorship pays first, has little exit value unless you run a true media company selling a roster of influencers under an umbrella deal. The more cash you already have, the more to lean toward equity, but demand disproportionate reward for that risk. Own brand: white label is fast and cheap, but he prefers custom so nobody can sell the same thing and undercut your pricing power. His scale marks: over 5,000 Prestige Labs affiliates at $20M-plus a year, 30,000 for the book launch.
