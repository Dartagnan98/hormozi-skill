# Continuity & Recurring Revenue
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Money Models course + 602 video transcripts)

---

## WHY CONTINUITY MATTERS

### The Math
- $1,000 one-time to 100 people = $10,000 from 10 buyers
- $50/mo to 100 people = $40,000 from 40 buyers who stay 20 months
- 4x the customers to upsell later
- Pros: Attract more customers at lower price
- Cons: Less money NOW (which is why continuity goes LAST in your money model)

### The Core Principle
"The way to create a fortune in business is to only sell things that people do not stop buying."

### Customer Surplus
- Customer surplus = value received far exceeds price paid
- When surplus is high enough, people call YOU when their card expires to make sure billing continues
- Examples: Wi-Fi, phone service, Netflix -- you immediately update your card

### The Starbucks/Coca-Cola Test
If you've been buying Starbucks for years and won't stop, that's the model you want.

### The Thought Experiment
- Write down total number of people you've ever sold
- Multiply by your current monthly price
- That's what your revenue WOULD be if nobody ever left
- How much could you spend on acquisition with that revenue?

## RECURRING VS. REOCCURRING REVENUE

- **Recurring** = Netflix (fixed subscription, automatic billing)
- **Reoccurring** = Coca-Cola (repeated purchases, no subscription)
- Both are great. Key: demonstrate that average buyer purchases X more times.

### Enterprise Value Impact
- Recurring company at $10M/$3M EBITDA: **8-15x multiple** (worth $24M-$45M)
- Transactional company at $10M/$3M EBITDA: **4x multiple** (worth $12M)
- Subscription just existing triples LTV -- 3x even with no added value

## THE "DOWNSELL THE UPSELL" TECHNIQUE

- Sell the expensive thing first, then offer MORE VALUE for LESS per month
- Gym Launch: $16K for 16 weeks ($1K/week) then offer 3-year deal at $3,200/mo (half the weekly rate)
- Script: "Have you enjoyed everything so far? Mind if I tell you a way to save a little and get a lot more?"
- People think about monthly cash flow more than duration

---

## BIG HEAD LONG TAIL

"You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once."

- Big upfront payment + small ongoing payments
- $5,000 down, $200/mo
- Sunk cost fallacy prevents cancellation (people don't want to "lose" their $5K)
- Very low cancellation rates
- Helps with CFA (big upfront offsets acquisition cost)
- Example: $1,000 down then $19.99/mo -- people called before card expired to save that rate
- "The bigger the head, the longer the tail"

---

## BACK-OF-NAPKIN LTV CALCULATION

- **Lifetime Revenue** = Price / Monthly Churn Rate
- **Lifetime Gross Profit** = Gross Margin x Lifetime Revenue
- Example: $100/mo, 10% churn = $1,000 LTV. At 80% margin = $800 LTGP
- Example: $100/mo, 3% churn = $3,333 LTV. At 80% margin = $2,667 LTGP

## CONTINUITY SUB-TYPES

### Continuity Bonus Offers ("Stay and you get MORE")
- Pricing data: at **1.33x** standalone price = 50/50 split between continuity and standalone
- At **2x** standalone price = 70% choose continuity
- At **3x** standalone price = 90% choose continuity
- Use this data to set your continuity pricing

### Continuity Discounts (4 Ways to Apply)

**Way 1: Upfront discount, push out term**
- "3 months free, then 12 months paid" (15-month total)
- Works best with enforceable contracts (cell phones, storage, leases)
- PRO TIP: Don't eat into the term. 3 free + 12 paid = 15 months. NOT 3 free + 9 paid = 12 months.

**Way 2: Discount at the end**
- "3 months free AFTER you complete your 12-month commitment"
- Encourages payment adherence
- They "earn" their free time

**Way 3: Spread over time**
- 3 months free on a 12-month at $200/mo = $600 discount spread = $150/mo for 12 months
- After term ends, reverts to $200/mo full price

**Way 4: First payments + free period + contract**
- Get 1-2 payments upfront, then give free period, then contract kicks in
- "First and last month" or "setup fee" + free months
- Liquidates acquisition cost while still advertising "3 months free"

### Lifetime Discount at Churn Point
- Amazon does this: 5% off with continuity, 15% off if you stay past 5 months
- Place the incentive right AFTER your average churn month
- Two benefits: (1) people pay more upfront knowing it'll go down, (2) people stay longer to earn the discount
- Remind them as they approach the milestone: "2 months left! 1 month left!"

### Waived Fee Offer
- Charge a setup/initiation fee that's **3-5x monthly rate**
- Then waive it if they commit to continuity
- Creates urgency and perceived savings

## EDUCATION/INFO PRODUCTS CONTINUITY FIX

- Problem: once you learn arithmetic, the course is worth nothing (methods = one-and-done)
- Solution: separate one-time value (install the sales system) from consumable value (monthly coaching calls, new ad templates, new plays)
- Price continuity based on consumable value alone, not blended
- Or sell annual memberships (buy time to create next offer)

### Models vs. Methods
- Methods = one-and-done (buy the course, learned it, done)
- Models = evolving frameworks that always need updating (franchises, systems)
- Sell models, not methods -- models have inherent recurring value

## THE THREE-STEP FORTUNE PROCESS (from Mark Roberge / HubSpot)

1. **Promotion** (0 to $1M) -- just get customers. Don't optimize pricing. Get feedback.
2. **Product** ($1M to $10M) -- iterate until people don't leave. Survey, interview, improve.
3. **Scale** ($10M+) -- once people don't leave, pour gas on marketing. Everything compounds.

### The Promotion-to-Product Sequence
- Most internet marketers stop at promotion. That's why they can't build fortunes.
- "You can become a millionaire learning to market and sell. You can't build a fortune."
- The BORING work of product improvement is where fortunes are built.

## 8 C's OF RECURRING REVENUE (Stickiness Framework)

Think of each as a CONTINUUM (not a checkbox). To what extent do you do each?

### 1. Consumption
- How well do people consume/use your product?
- Better onboarding, more addictive product, easier to use
- Netflix, Spotify = pure consumption-based retention
- Getting people to consume the product = #1 factor in retention

### 2. Collateral
- Do you hold something the customer needs?
- Physical: storage has your stuff
- Digital: Dropbox has your files, CRM has your contacts
- "Holding hostage" = sticky

### 3. Cost of Switching
- How arduous/expensive is it to switch providers?
- Time cost, money cost, sunk cost fallacy
- Bigger upfront payment = harder to leave
- "The bigger the head, the longer the tail"

### 4. Choice (Lack of Alternatives)
- Are you the only option?
- Patents, trade secrets, unique competitive process
- Niching down = fewer alternatives for your specific customer
- Warren Buffett's "competitive moat"

### 5. Control of Money Flow
- Who gets paid first?
- Uber takes fee before paying driver
- Government takes taxes before paycheck
- Less overt charges = stickier (employees gripe less about taxes than business owners because they never see the gross)

### 6. Cause
- Are people aligned with your mission?
- Churches = 10% tithe based on identity/cause
- Political donations, brand loyalty (buy shoes for the cause, not just the shoes)
- Tom's Shoes model

### 7. Community
- Would leaving mean losing their identity/tribe?
- People don't leave communities -- they leave businesses
- Strengthen community ties = stickier than any contract

### 8. Contracts (Enforceable)
- 6-month contract at $2K/mo vs. month-to-month
- Even if month-to-month averages 4 months, 6-month contract gets 5-6 months
- Sell to better prospects for more enforceable contracts
- Positioning as "6 months then month-to-month" increases stick without scaring buyers

## PI EQUATION EXPANDED (Deep Pass -- Batches 3-4)

### Three Applications
1. **Max Customers**: Inflow / Outflow = Max. 5/month at 10% churn = 50 cap
2. **LTV**: Revenue per client / churn rate. $1K/month at 20% churn = $5K LTV
3. **Revenue Cap**: New biz $/month / revenue churn. $10K at 10% = $100K/month
4. **Golden Ratio**: Referral % > Churn % = infinite growth (net negative churn)

### Three Variables
- Price, churn, inflow -- "First three questions I ask any business owner"
- Growth curve: fast at start, slows as churn eats bigger percentage, caps at ~80-90% of theoretical max

### Virtuous Cycle of High Prices
- Higher price -> higher margins -> better talent -> better service -> better reputation -> more demand -> higher price
- "Pricing is two-way communication"

## FROM $100M LOST CHAPTERS

### Lifetime Discounts -- "The Opener" Story
- Billion-dollar gym franchise opens every single location with 400+ recurring members on day one
- Method: **Founding member lifetime discount**
- Before the gym even opens, sell founding memberships at a locked-in rate
- "Pay $29/month forever" (regular rate will be $49/month)
- People sign up before the doors open because the deal will never be available again
- Creates immediate recurring revenue base before a single class is taught

### Three Ways to Display Lifetime Discounts
1. **Percentage off retail**: "Save 40% for life" -- $49 becomes $29.40
2. **Dollar amount off retail**: "Save $20/month forever" -- $49 becomes $29
3. **Fixed price (Price Protection)**: "Lock in $29/month -- price will never increase for you"
- Price protection is the most powerful framing: people fear price increases more than they value discounts
- "Locking in today's price feels like insurance against future pain"

### Price Protection for Fixed Periods
- Variant: "Your price is locked for 24 months, then reverts to current rate"
- Creates urgency to sign up NOW (current rate is lowest it'll ever be)
- After lock period, natural price increase feels expected (not punitive)

### Sunk Cost Mechanics
- Large upfront payment + locked rate = two retention forces working together
- The upfront payment creates sunk cost ("I already put $500 in, I can't leave now")
- The locked rate creates fear of loss ("If I leave, I lose my $29/month rate forever")
- "The bigger the head, the longer the tail" applies to discounts too

### Initiation Fee Waiver Trick
- Charge a real initiation/setup fee ($300-$500)
- Standard offer: full fee + monthly rate
- Promotional offer: "We're waiving the initiation fee this week only"
- Creates urgency (save $300-$500 by acting now)
- Even if you always waive it, the EXISTENCE of the fee makes the waiver feel valuable
- Pro tip: occasionally DON'T waive it, so the waiver promotion remains credible

### Lifetime Upgrades for Retention
- Use upgrades as retention bonuses: "Stay 12 months and your account permanently upgrades to Gold"
- Gold gets: priority scheduling, exclusive content, VIP community access
- The upgrade is permanent -- even if they pause and come back, they keep Gold status
- Creates loyalty loop: the longer they stay, the more they'd lose by leaving

### Sell the renewal while they're in flight (vid: 8VqSFBMMS4M — "Scale or Fail: Tina, hate selling")

Mention aftercare only at the end and you get about a third. Pitch at the halfway point, two-thirds, and a last chance, and you can reach half to two-thirds. Mid-decision is a light sale; a new decision after finishing is heavy. Pain is still elevated and they've already had value.

### Maintenance plan where money is secondary (vid: o64cI6tebnU — "Scale or Fail: Caleb, roofing")

Roofing VIP: pay a modest annual fee, get $2 of repair credit per $1, and a yearly check. The money doesn't matter; the booked visit does, because that's where repairs get found. Turns one-time work into recurring and sharply raises LTV. Placement and wording are the hard part.

### Agency fix: $10K setup or $0 with a six-month commitment (vid: 6_CCutkM11g — "$100M Money Models Live Launch")

Email agency for e-commerce brands, about $450K in revenue at around 10% profit, sold a $2,950 one-time flow build and then tried to upsell a monthly retainer. Most buyers never converted, so it kept restarting from zero.
Hormozi's rewrite: quote the build at $10K for month-to-month clients, or $0 if they commit to six months at $3K. Cancel early and they owe the $10K they skipped.
The logic to say out loud: if I front the risk, you make the commitment; if you break it, you pay what flexibility would have cost. Some will just pay the $10K, and those are nice cash pops.
Keep the same ads and front end. Only the pitch on the call changes.

### Sell the next thing while the plane is in flight (vid: MPHn-nW30QU — "14 Years of Good Advice")

Hypnotherapist with a 5-week high-ticket package asked how to keep clients once the problem is solved. Don't wait for week five. Make the offer around weeks 2-3, when you can show progress without resolution, and open the next loop: the next issue they mentioned or one you noticed.
A finished client has no reason to stay, but people are always dissatisfied with something, and solving one problem reveals another. Your job is to spot the next problem before they do and have the product ready. Lost 10 pounds? Now tone, a powerlifting meet, a mud run.

### Sell the renewal mid-cycle, and let the math set the target (vid: B2QbLzHvKbg — "Money Models launch replay + hotline")

Insurance-agent marketing agency: $15K for 3 months, about 50 deals a month, wants $1M/month, CSMs re-enrolling after the term with weak take rates.
Diagnosis: pitching at the end makes it a brand-new buying decision. Pitch from the end of month 1 to the middle of month 2, framed as keeping things going.
Three structures: roll into another 3 months with a month free because setup is already done; credit $5K of setup toward the next term; or sell $15K then automatic $3K/mo, where they say yes and no transaction is needed.
Target math: revenue goal divided by deals per month equals required LTV. $1M at 50 deals means $20K-25K LTV, and that number tells you what the back end must do.

### An event is a cost unless you sell at it (vid: lidFtb1Ga0o — "He Makes $1.5M and Keeps 1.3%")

Men's self-development brand, $500K, $3,500 package, close rate fine, but nobody renews and retreats eat about half the margin. Hormozi's two changes: stop selling the pieces separately, and sell from the room.
Package the valued in-person and online parts at $5K to $6K a year, and make it pick two or three of four retreats for flexibility. Pitch the renewal at the second event with a fast-action bonus while they're high on the experience, not after they've gone home flat.
The mistake he names: fear of renewing people before the subscription ends.

### Fix SaaS churn by paying for activation, not by scaling (vid: -HbwUdqUcEs — "More, more, more plus Hotline calls")

Fence-quoting app, 8% monthly churn, about $130 CAC. Customers who sell one fence with it almost never cancel. So: don't scale ads until this is fixed. Charge a setup fee up front (he used $199) and rebate it, or skip a month's bill, if they use the app on three yards in the first 10 days. Say it plainly: I know you stay once you've used it.
Separate trial conversion from post-conversion churn; trials in the churn number wreck the math. Target under 2% a month, ideally closer to 1%. All incentive goes to first use, plus an onboarding call booked immediately.

### Luxury projects: a tier needs about 5x, and the retainer should be a rounding error (vid: IG9IWQVmxqk — "Q&A: swim school, pitch deck, memberships")

Wellness designer to ultra-wealthy families, $80 a square foot, considering a $200K annual tier. Hormozi killed the ladder: price steps of 20% are undifferentiated; fractal pricing needs roughly five times the price.
Instead bundle a tiny annual plan into the job, like home services selling a $500 a year plan on a $100,000 project. Position it as insurance. Its real job is the excuse to walk into a rich client's house every year and ask what else is being bought.
"The small jobs get the big jobs." Quote the job as one number; a per-foot rate means nothing to the buyer.

### $49 content membership with 12% churn: fix velocity and prepay before pivoting niches (vid: TLpLmhWqKEU — "Key man risk and caller breakdowns")

Templates for realtors: $49 a month, just under 300 members, about 50 sales a month, 12% monthly churn. Founder wants to leave the niche; the leader has 5,000 members in a market he pegs at a million realtors.
His math: eight month life times about $50 is $400 per customer, fine at $100 CAC. So velocity is the problem. With LTV at eight months, give away four months for annual since it is equivalent, and pull cash forward.
Add a $197 to $397 self-liquidating offer on the thank-you page (say, the 100 best performing posts). Target agents with a sale in the last 30 days. Find the traits that split churners from stickers, and test ads, before blaming the avatar.

### Signage retainer: every maintenance payment credits the next sign (vid: noE1MQPMvAc — "Counterintuitive fixes + sales motion Q&A")

B2B sign seller to franchises asks how to turn an install into recurring. Hormozi borrows the home services play: sell an annual maintenance plan for bulbs, fixes and cleaning, since stores neglect it and a dead letter hurts them. Bill as often as you visit.
The kicker: signs need replacing about every five years, so all recurring money credits toward the next one. They have to clean it, fix it and rebuy it anyway, so the plan locks in the repurchase.
Works anywhere with a durable item and a foregone replacement cycle.

### Get in the right boat: sell what people already don't cancel (vid: 41EvCgwPrDc — "Make More Profit than 99% of People")

Most of his career went to making people stick. If starting over he'd only look at categories with structurally low cancellation: alarm systems, insurance, payment processing, banking, phone service, cable. Gyms fight it forever because people don't want to work out. Pick the boat before tuning retention.

### Contracts are worth the signature's quality (vid: 41EvCgwPrDc — "Make More Profit than 99% of People")

Enterprise contracts hold. US consumer contracts mostly don't, since card networks side with buyers on chargebacks. They add friction at purchase, because people factor them in when buying, with no matching drag at cancel, when they ignore it. Biggest consumer brands skip them. Weigh the conversion hit against spending that effort on product and targeting. Europe differs: contracts carry more weight there.

### Add a CPI clause and use it rarely (vid: 41EvCgwPrDc — "Make More Profit than 99% of People")

Put a right to match consumer price inflation in every continuity contract. Having it doesn't require using it; people hate price changes, so update every two or three years.

### Events spike churn: plan the next before they leave (vid: 5MjjpB8SPMo — "Make Everyone Want to Buy Your Stuff")

At an event he spent about $2M on, everyone raved and churn spiked after, though it fell going in. Upsells happen at greatest need, not greatest satisfaction: no one wants more steak after dinner. Fix: always have the next one announced and open-looped. Put events behind tenure (6 to 12 months) so it's something to earn. A caller running two events monthly saw churn rise and was weighing a 12-month lock.

### Activation point instead of pitching high ticket to all (vid: 5MjjpB8SPMo — "Make Everyone Want to Buy Your Stuff")

Don't pitch the whole community. Set a metric, say hitting a revenue level, reward it (his gyms got a sword at a live ceremony at $1M a year, with proof submitted, which doubles as ads), then call those people: you'll get there on your own; do you want 5 years or 5 months? Add a high tier only once graduates ask for more. Too early hurts; too late doesn't. Clear line between tiers: unlimited zero-cost access low, implementation high.

### Belonging: one friend, ten regulars, an owner who shows up (vid: -j8_YCWZ05Q — "Customers Stay FOREVER")

School sees owners who show up daily retain far better. Minimum: daily alarm to clear DMs and reports, read complaints each morning, praise good behavior, delete bad members. Find your top 10 members, learn their goals, spotlight them, and introduce new people to them like a host at a party. Layla's stat, from employees but transferable: someone with a close friend at work stays about five times longer, so get each member one real friend.

### Paid community: what people care about vs will pay for (vid: IFpHasZ7jN4 — "54.1% First Dollar Online")

From a ProfitWell study as he recalls it. High care and high willingness to pay: exclusive access (Q&A, you reply, calls), exclusive content (more of what's public, courses, written posts), and events, which surprised him, and in person beats virtual. Expected, so high care with low pay: newsletter, like protein in a supplement stack. Mixed: merch, discounts. Build the first group; a newsletter can just reuse the content.

### Community first: the I-am statement and the group-over-course finding (vid: IFpHasZ7jN4 — "54.1% First Dollar Online")

Sam Ovens saw in his data that buyers watched a handful of course videos and then spent the rest of their time in the group, per Hormozi, which is why Skool put the community at the hub. For any business, ask what lifestyle you move people toward and finish the sentence I am a ___. Musicians, fitness people, authors all share schedules, tools and practice. Each added member adds value to all others. A niche expert with 10 mechanic shops and real rollups keeps paid members for years, while a founder-faced group that churns in two months is income, not an asset.

### Why sell a defined program before continuity (vid: Gh9zWsP8JpI — "Reversing your pricing")

Five reasons. Outcomes are easier to sell than memberships. Higher cash up front funds acquisition. It anchors later continuity, which feels like a downsell though it's the bigger ticket. It pays for costly onboarding instead of going negative. And a bigger payment gets more committed customers.

### Claimed price vs collected: the $30K offer that is really $10K (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

Extends separating one-time from consumable value. A $30K program at $2,500 a month with 25% monthly churn collects about $10K, and you still fight a $30K sale. His illustration: $7K up front, then $500 a month at 4% churn, which is 25 months, $12,500 on the back and $19,500 LTV. Double the LTV, maybe double the conversion, and about 4x the EPC. Year by year the old model hits $20M and stalls as churn eats it; the other adds 8 a year and passes it by year four at 32. Bill the one-time part as fast as they can pay, rather than stretching it over a year. Gym Launch's consumables: a daily sales meeting for gym staff, plus $50K a month of ad testing in 10 markets, with winners licensed to 1,000 locations.

### Start a community with four posts and a coffee call (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

From Skool's Sam Ovens: having a course shows no link to retention in their data, and "I'll launch when I've recorded it" stalls founders, so they planned to hide the classroom for new groups. Start: intro post with a photo of your workspace, favorite book, favorite movie, favorite GIF, plus a no-agenda weekly Zoom. Self-sharing posts beat long articles from the owner. One group launched with no classroom at $1, one-sentence posts and emojis, and reached about $100K a month fast. Add modules once you see what people need. His Games offer is 8 videos under 45 minutes, one weekly call, one monthly mastermind download, about 13,000 people at $99. Limit the details so each can be perfect. Overwhelm causes churn.

### Community culture tools: categories, pins and a closeness ladder (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

Extends culture as what gets rewarded and punished. Category names are the visible rules: "wins, memes, just for kicks" and "conversion rate, questions" read as different communities before anyone posts. A renamed wins category with a meme and a bell makes bragging safe. A pin is the jackpot reward, and the ideal pinned post comes from a member. Sam's ladder for top contributors: praise in comments, pin, DM, Zoom, then the office. New people see the path. A dead group came back from one new "banter" category and a DM to its funniest poster. Bad post: DM the rules with a screenshot. Answer comments with questions. You need only about 10 true regulars, like a sitcom cast.

### Separate promotion from delivery (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

A group owner ran 17 lives a week inside his paid community. Hormozi: take that energy outside. Seventeen lives in public is fine, because watching one might sell someone. Inside, missing 16 of 17 feels like lost value. You can never promote too much, but you can put too much inside the box. When a post is free, its cost is their time, so one return on that time is enough.

### Peel out what's consumed again (vid: aOq1Hdz0w3k — "What I look for to invest")

Why info businesses churn: margins match software with no development cost, but a skill is consumed once. Separate that from what is used monthly, such as community and accountability, then sell the new problems the skill creates. That is how he turns a guru business into something with LTV and sale value.

### In SaaS, marketing exists to fix the product first (vid: bgBIO6nZawg — "7 Millionaires")

Coming from high-ticket, he thought ALAN was a marketing and sales business; it was a product business. Early marketing just feeds users so you can see who stays and where they churn, until growth runs on its own; then pour on gas. Low churn means going up market or creating customers who stay. Look for the cohort whose retention curve flattens and aim messaging at them. The hard part: sales won't want it, and you won't want to refuse money.

### Community loop and the one-line email (Sam Ovens) (vid: kloJJeiysxg — "4 Steps to Unf*** Your Business")

Sam spends about 90% of his community time reading, not posting. Find the recurring problems, validate them with power users, rank them, fix the top one, then announce it. His wall template: hey, I made this thing for you, here's what it does, enjoy, plus a CTA. Hormozi adds: posts cluster around one problem, so answer it, and if the problem persists you didn't solve it, so make it simpler and go again. Skool Games as packaged by Sam: eight videos under 45 minutes total, one call a week, one drop a month; 13,000 people at $99 a month (his figures).

### Free webinar, boot camp pitch, community as the bonus (member's funnel) (vid: lEIqyLE4iOY — "More Followers Won't Make You Rich")

A member's main growth path: weekly free webinars promoted by email, text and social. The pitch sells a monthly 3 to 5 day virtual boot camp, then offers a free ticket to anyone who joins the low-ticket community. The boot camp upsells high-ticket mentorship; those not ready stay in the community, get every boot camp, and keep being nurtured.

### Very-small-business SaaS: fix churn by removing, then pour everything on activation (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

Caller's dropshipping SaaS had 33% monthly churn from first-time founders. Hormozi: largely the nature of the very-small-business market, closer to consumer, trading churn for a nearly endless market. He bet it could get closer to 10% and said Shopify runs about there, on a low entry fee with the upside from payments on the users who win. Top reason to cancel is overwhelm, so improve by subtraction: show only the one next action, hold everything else until it is done. If activation is the constraint, stop growth work and new features until more people reach it.

### One-time high-ticket sale: sell maintenance at the mirror (vid: rc7cxL7ql7Y — "58 Mins of Advice")

Extends the fur storage model. Dentist caller with single-visit full-mouth work and no repeat revenue. Don't upsell a bigger package; sell long-term care after the sale, while they stare at the result: a yearly fee worth a small share of what they just paid (he said about 5%), framed as doubling how long the work lasts. It also brings them back regularly. Aside from his fur job: most luxury buyers aren't rich but want to look it.

### Fill the dead season with an insurance product (vid: vZfatNSouDQ — "Hardcore Business Lessons from a Dealer")

Nobody buys fur in summer, so the store sold care for what they already bought: storage and conditioning so a coat lasts two to five times longer, explained by comparing it to car insurance. Cost: one 18-year-old, air conditioning, a storage room. Insurance needn't be insurance; charge for safety of past value. Yearly visits make you their dealer for life, so they stop price shopping.

### Free community after the program is an endless liability (vid: yPDQCfrwh8E — "Simple Strategy for 60 Days")

It helps sell the front end but creates a forever obligation for one payment, and it lowers success: if they can do it any time, they do it never. A wall at the end, paid to stay, speeds action. Solve the first problem well and it creates the next (lose the weight, now want the six-pack); you should know that next problem better than they do, and sell its solution then, not on day one. His company rolled it out slowly and only after fixing delivery, so finishers were asking what's next.
