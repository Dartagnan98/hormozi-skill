# Client Financed Acquisition (CFA)
## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Money Models course + 602 video transcripts)

---

## Definition
Making enough gross profit from a new customer within 30 days that advertising pays for itself.

---

## Three Levels of Advertising

### Level 1: LTGP > CAC
- Lifetime gross profit exceeds cost to acquire customer
- Minimum 3x ratio
- "The dull level" -- bare minimum to have a business

### Level 2: 30-Day GP > CAC
- Make more than acquisition cost in FIRST 30 DAYS
- Result: Never spend your own money to grow
- Why 30 days? Every business can borrow money interest-free for 30 days via credit cards/credit lines
- Use credit card to get customers, pay card back, recycle cash, get more customers
- Limited only by credit card limit and operational capacity

### Level 3: 30-Day GP > 2x CAC
- Make more than TWICE acquisition cost in first 30 days
- Result: Never limited by money at all
- One customer pays back debt AND funds the next customer
- Doubling happens by CUSTOMER, not by month (can happen daily)

## Month-by-Month Compounding Example (Level 3)
- Reinvest all profit: 1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 8 -> ... -> 4,000+ customers by month 12

## FROM $100M LOST CHAPTERS

### CFA Levels (Detailed from Book with Lemonade Stand Example)

**Level 1: GP < CAC in 30 days (Floating)**
- You spend more to get a customer than you make back in 30 days
- Must float the difference from savings or credit
- Example: Spend $20 CAC, make $8 GP in 30 days = down $12, floating on your own money
- "This is where most businesses live. They're spending money to get customers and HOPING to make it back over time."

**Level 2: 30D GP = CAC (Credit Limit = Ad Budget)**
- You make back exactly what you spent within 30 days
- Your credit card limit becomes your advertising budget
- Cycle: charge card -> acquire customer -> collect GP -> pay card -> repeat
- "Every business can borrow money interest-free for 30 days via credit cards"

**Level 3: 30D GP > 2x CAC (Doubling by Customer)**
- Each customer pays back the acquisition cost AND funds the next customer
- Doubling happens by CUSTOMER, not by month (can happen daily)
- One customer's profit pays for two more customers

- 30D Cash = gross profit extracted from a new customer in their first 30 days

### Whale businesses: volume cures volatility, and the downsell must fit the lead (vid: I0nCuqu7Cac — "Hotline: mental toughness and offers")

Comic book buyer, once $1M top line and $500K profit, buying 600 leads a month at $10 with about 1% being whales. Hormozi: big and small months are a symptom of too little volume. Triple spend to $18K a month and a once-a-quarter whale shows up monthly.
Liquidating the rest: a $97 course on selling their own comics hit about 1% across two years and seven VSLs. That isn't trying hard, it's repeating the wrong offer. Sell what a seller wants instead: front of the line, an answer within a day, the fee credited on purchase, or a $37 listing to a buyer network.

### Need cash in 48 hours: three pools, in order (vid: 0RjIw9M1j28 — "Walk Away From $1.4M Business?")

1. Active customers (smallest pool): ask them to prepay a year. Prefer adding free services over discounting, but discount if you must.
2. Past customers: an IOU campaign that credits what they previously spent toward a bigger current package, framed as you can deliver better now.
3. Leads who never bought (largest pool): a sharp attraction offer such as buy one get one or win your money back.
Payroll, tax bills, broken machinery: each pool is bigger and less likely to pay than the one before.

### Assisted living: pull cash forward with one-time fees at the transition (vid: TLpLmhWqKEU — "Key man risk and caller breakdowns")

Private-pay senior living, billing monthly, takes a few months to get good profit. Three moves: a setup or enrollment fee, requiring the first three months up front, or a bundle of things the family must buy anyway (moving, transport, hair and nails, furniture upgrades).
People are very sensitive to the monthly price and much less to a one-time fee. A life transition means the wallet is already open, and in a stressful moment many will pay extra for one party to handle everything. Same logic as the storage facility selling locks, boxes and insurance.

### Gym Launch terms for owners who are broke (vid: TooAB8Ow6cQ — "3 Hours of Money Making Advice")

For its first three years everyone started at $1,000 down and $1,000 a week, because gym owners have no cash. The second payment waited 14 days so they could collect from their first new members, and a discount for prepaying in the first 30 days turned $1,000 down into about $16K in month one. It only works because the first 30 days are tracked: average gym adds over $20K in new revenue, and profit goes from $36K to $112K over the year net of fees. Caleb came in losing money with his last $10K already spent on another coach, put $1,000 down and made $38,000 in 30 days.

### Buy X get Y free: protecting the price (vid: mHrAjWni65E — "How to get rich this Black Friday")

Extends free with prepay. New service level lets you reset price: take a $100 a month service to $200, add one or two cheap extras so later $100 members have no complaint, then buy six get six. No payment plans on the deal: full prepay only, or it's normal price. Also a grand opening play: the first 50 patients at a clinic can fund the build out. Cap quantity to limit downside. His warning: he has passed on investing in businesses that had become dependent on this, because cash is front loaded.
